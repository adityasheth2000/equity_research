## Page 4

**Topic: Strategy Pillars 1, 2 and 3 (HBL)**

*   **Strategic Approach to Technology Gaps:**
    *   The company identifies that foreign technical collaborations are often unavailable for Indian companies due to unwillingness of foreign firms to transfer technology.
    *   HBL positions itself to capitalize on these "technology gaps" by leveraging internal engineering talent to develop products where others cannot.

*   **Market Selection Strategy (Niche Markets):**
    *   Focuses on choosing "niche markets" defined as spaces that are too small for large conglomerates but too difficult for small competitors.
    *   Objective is to create secure market spaces to ensure business survival ("survive infant mortality") over a long period.

*   **Competitive Positioning & Returns:**
    *   Anticipated outcome of successful execution in niche markets is reduced competition ("less competition").
    *   Management expects this positioning to drive better pricing power and consequently higher returns on investment.
    *   Historical Context: Management claims HBL has succeeded technically in most attempts and commercially in enough attempts despite delays to achieve its current status.

**Topic: # 9. KAVACH (Train Collision Avoidance System)**

*   **Market Opportunity & Government Mandate:**
    *   HBL pioneered the Kavach system with assistance from RDSO; initial demo trial conducted in October 2012 at Tandur, SCR.
    *   Indian Railways has announced a mandate to install Kavach (TCAS) over at least **30,000 km** of track within ten years.

*   **Competitive Landscape & Moat:**
    *   Currently, there are only **three companies approved** for the supply of TCAS.
    *   Interoperability among these three designs has been established.
    *   New suppliers face a barrier to entry as they must demonstrate interoperability with current suppliers, described as a time-consuming process.

*   **Order Book & Contract Visibility:**
    *   HBL holds **four EPC contracts** on hand totaling **Rs. 686 crores**.
    *   **Contract Structure:** The EPC portion is subcontracted. The TCAS component varies by tender but averages **70%** of the total contract value for tenders currently on hand.
    *   **Execution Timeline:** Each contract requires approximately **two years** to complete.

*   **Recurring Revenue Model (Maintenance):**
    *   Expected service life of a TCAS system is **15 years**.
    *   Post-warranty maintenance creates a recurring revenue stream where railways pay annually for electronics system maintenance after the first few years.

**Topic: Electronic Fuzes for Guns, Rockets, Bombs (Strategic Initiative & Competitive Advantage)**

*   **Product Definition:** Electronic fuzes are complex devices used to ignite ammunition. They are preferred over mechanical fuzes due to higher reliability.
*   **Competitive Moat / Indigenisation:**
    *   HBL has developed 100% in-house technology for Electronic Fuzes for grenades and artillery guns over the last 15 years.
    *   HBL is positioned as the only Indian company with 100% indigenisation of this technology.
*   **Market Context & Regulatory Tailwinds:**
    *   Current suppliers ECIL (DAE) and BEL (MoD) supply electronic fuzes for artillery but have high import content (~80% of Bill of Materials).
    *   The Ministry of Defence (MoD) is driving an "Atma Nirbhar" (self-reliance) agenda, creating a potential demand shift toward indigenous players like HBL.
*   **Operational Status & Guidance:**
    *   HBL's fuzes have undergone multiple tests on army guns.
    *   Eligibility for bulk supply is expected by the end of FY24.
*   **Growth Visibility (Exports):**
    *   Management notes the global market is large with very few competitors.
    *   Export revenue is anticipated following domestic validation.

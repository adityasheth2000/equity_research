## Page 4

**Strategy Pillars 1, 2 and 3**

*   **Market Opportunity & Moat (Technology Gaps):**
    *   The company identifies a strategic opportunity based on "technology gaps" in the Indian market.
    *   This gap exists because most Indian companies rely on foreign technical collaborations, but foreign firms are unwilling to transfer technology.
    *   HBL leverages its own "Technical judgement" to decide which products can be developed using available domestic engineering talent rather than relying on foreign tech transfers.

*   **Competitive Positioning (Niche Markets):**
    *   The strategy focuses on selecting "niche markets."
    *   These markets are defined as being too small for large competitors ("big firms") yet too difficult for smaller firms to enter.
    *   Objective: To create secure market spaces that allow businesses to survive the "infant mortality" phase.

*   **Commercial Performance & Outlook:**
    *   **Competition:** Success in these niches leads to reduced competition ("less competition").
    *   **Pricing Power:** Reduced competition enables better pricing, leading to good returns on investment.
    *   **Track Record:** Management states that HBL has succeeded technically in "most attempts so far." Commercially, they have succeeded in enough attempts despite facing "long delays," establishing the company's current standing.

**Topic: # 9. KAVACH (Train Collision Avoidance System)**

*   **Market Opportunity & Order Book Visibility:**
    *   **Total Addressable Market:** Indian Railways announced an installation target of at least **30,000 km** of track over the next ten years.
    *   **Current Contract Backlog:** HBL holds EPC contracts valued at **Rs 592 crores**.
    *   **Tender Pricing Benchmark (2021):** Nine EPC tenders covered **3,000 km** with a total value of **Rs 2,000 crores** (approx. Rs 66.67 lakhs/km).
    *   **Contract Duration:** Each contract requires approximately **two years** to complete.

*   **Revenue Mix & Commercial Model:**
    *   **EPC vs. TCAS Split:** For existing tenders on hand, the TCAS component averages **70%** of the total contract value. The remaining EPC portion is subcontracted.
    *   **Recurring Revenue Stream:** The expected service life of a TCAS system is **15 years**. Post-installation (after the first few years), Railways pay for annual maintenance, creating a recurring revenue model.

*   **Competitive Landscape & Moat:**
    *   **Barriers to Entry:** Interoperability among the three approved designs has been established. New suppliers face a time-consuming process to ensure their systems are interoperable with current suppliers, acting as a barrier to entry.
    *   **Competition:** There are currently only **three companies** approved for the supply of TCAS.

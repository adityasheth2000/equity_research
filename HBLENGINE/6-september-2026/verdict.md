**52-week range:** ₹1,122 / ₹603 (stock ~38% off high, ~14% above low)

---

## 1. What the company does (plain language)

HBL Engineering is an Indian technology-manufacturing company that has been making **specialised batteries** since 1986 and has, over the last decade, branched into **defence electronics and railway safety systems**. Think of it in three buckets:

1. **Batteries (the historical core):** lead-acid and nickel-cadmium batteries for telecom towers, railways, UPS, data centres, oil & gas, and metro; plus silver-zinc and lithium batteries for submarines, torpedoes, missiles, and fighter aircraft. These are mostly sold to institutional customers (BSNL, Indus Towers, Indian Railways, the Navy, the Air Force).

2. **Defence & aviation batteries/electronics:** torpedo batteries, missile batteries (including Agni V), aircraft batteries, submarine batteries, and electronic fuses for ammunition (grenades, rockets, bombs, anti-drone rounds). It is one of very few companies in the world (and the only one in India) that makes the full fuse — battery, detonator, safety device — in-house.

3. **Railway electronics — the current growth engine:** HBL is a pioneer and one of only ~3 approved suppliers of **KAVACH** (India's indigenous Train Collision Avoidance System), plus Train Management Systems (TMS) and electronic interlocking/signalling.

**How it makes money:** It manufactures high-value, IP-protected hardware, wins multi-year government tenders (mostly Indian Railways and the Ministry of Defence), and books revenue as it delivers. It earns a recurring post-warranty maintenance annuity on KAVACH (15-year service life). The strategic model is deliberately "Mittelstand" — pick niche, high-margin technology areas, invest heavily in R&D (often 10–20 years before commercial success), and avoid low-margin commodity volume (e.g., it will not make commodity lithium-ion cells or chase telecom tower battery price wars).

## 2. Company overview & industry positioning

- **Founded:** 1986 by Dr A. J. Prasad. Formerly HBL Power Systems Ltd (renamed HBL Engineering Ltd).
- **Listing:** BSE: 517271, NSE: HBLENGINE. Face value ₹1. ~27.7 Cr shares outstanding. Promoter holding **59.11%** (unchanged for years); FII 5.62%, DII 0.97%.
- **Industry:** Industrials → Capital Goods → Electrical Equipment / Industrial Products.
- **Subsidiaries/associates:** HBL America Inc., HBL Germany GmbH, Torquedrive Technologies, TTL Electric Fuel (EV charging); associates Tonbo Imaging India and Naval Systems & Technologies (₹86.7 Cr invested). Subs contribute <5% of TOI.
- **Competitive position:** #1 or #2 in India in nearly all product lines (exception: telecom tower batteries, #3 behind Amara Raja/Exide). In KAVACH, it is the **first and one of only two vendors RDSO-certified for KAVACH Version 4.0**, giving it a lead over three of the five originally-awarded players who are not yet 4.0-qualified. In TMS it claims to be the only approved/proven Indian company.
- **Credit rating:** CARE **A+ / A1+** (LT outlook **Positive**) — see §9.

## 3. Key metrics to track (and why)

1. **KAVACH order book & quarterly order inflows (₹ Cr) — the single biggest driver.**
   Order book went ₹1,178 Cr (Aug 2024) → ₹4,479 Cr (Jul 2025) → ₹2,999 Cr (Dec 2025), then was replenished by ~₹3,000+ Cr of fresh wins in early 2026 (CLW ₹1,714 Cr, BLW ₹800 Cr, ICF ₹575 Cr, plus smaller). Execution is 12–24 months, so this book *is* near-term revenue. A declining book = a future revenue cliff.

2. **KAVACH 4.0 qualification / competitive moat.**
   Only 2 of 5 awardees are 4.0-certified. If HBL keeps converting its first-mover lead into order share, it captures the ≥30,000 km / 10-year Indian Railways rollout disproportionately.

3. **EBITDA/OPM margin profile (currently ~34% FY26 vs ~20% FY24–25).**
   The spike is KAVACH/defence mix. Whether margins settle at 25%+ or mean-revert to ~18–20% is the crux of the valuation. Note PBILDT margin was 37.2% in 9MFY26 (CARE).

4. **Working-capital / inventory days.**
   Elongated to **147 days** in FY25 (KAVACH inventory built ahead of RDSO approval). High inventory = the company's main balance-sheet risk; it ties up cash in a lumpy government-sales business.

5. **ROCE / ROE.**
   ROCE hit **58%** in FY26 (vs 27% FY25), ROE ~45% — both flattered by peak earnings on a thin asset/capital base (net worth ₹2,215 Cr). Sustained 25%+ ROE is needed to justify the ~8.7x P/B.

6. **Electronic fuses ramp (₹ Cr, timeline).**
   Management targets fuses becoming the **second-largest business in 3–5 years (FY28+)**. Track fuse order wins, RFI/trial conversions (155mm, 40mm, 23mm anti-drone, 30mm naval), and whether the "FY28 onward" revenue inflection materialises.

7. **Export revenue (% of total).**
   Exports grew ~40% in FY25 and are a margin tailwind (Ni-Cd and defence batteries to Middle East/APAC/Africa/Europe). FX is now ~₹300 Cr net positive.

8. **Capex discipline / lithium-ion cell investment.**
   ~₹200 Cr committed to defence lithium-ion cells (Navy) by FY26-end. Track whether it is "profitable from year one" as promised and stays internal-accrual funded (any unplanned debt-funded capex is a rating negative trigger).

## 4. Management track record (has management walked the talk?)

**Hits:**
- **FY24 beat:** Sep-2023 deck guided FY24 sales ₹1,750 Cr / EBITDA ₹262 Cr (15%). Actual: **₹2,233 Cr / ₹423 Cr (19%)** — a ~28% revenue and ~61% EBITDA beat.
- **FY26 beat:** Same deck guided FY26 ₹2,900 Cr. Actual **~₹3,303–3,310 Cr**. Chairman's "next orbit" floor of ₹3,000 Cr was met.
- **KAVACH 4.0 first-mover execution:** delivered the CLW 2,200-loco order on time for the 76% that wasn't cancelled, and won the largest-ever single KAVACH order (₹1,714 Cr, May 2026) off the back of the 4.0 certification lead.
- **Balance-sheet discipline:** 40 years without rescheduling any bank obligation; gearing ~0.03–0.07x; self-funds capex; returned ~100% of intended FY25 profit as dividend and is adding a second (March) dividend.
- **20-year R&D persistence paying off:** KAVACH R&D began 2005; electronic fuses ~20 years in development — now the main growth engines.

**Misses / caveats:**
- **FY25 miss:** guided ₹2,300 Cr (Sep-2023 deck); actual **₹1,967 Cr** — a ~14% miss caused by the KAVACH "order drought" and an RDSO approval delay on the CLW order (partly external, but the company had committed to a trajectory it couldn't control).
- **Order cancellation:** of the 2,200-unit CLW order, **541 units (~25%) were cancelled** (1,659 delivered) — a reminder that "order book" ≠ guaranteed revenue.
- **FY28 softness flagged:** management itself warned KAVACH "may fall a little bit" in FY28 — i.e., growth is lumpy, not linear.
- **Succession/age:** founder Dr A. J. Prasad is elderly; there is deliberately no single CEO (moving to a holding structure with ~9 quasi-independent divisions). This is a work-in-progress, not a proven transition.

**Verdict on management:** Strong on engineering and capital discipline, honest about lumpiness, but the Sep-2023 segment projections (e.g., Emerging segment to ₹1,200 Cr by FY26) were directionally right with poor timing accuracy. Treat their forward revenue numbers as ambitions, not commitments.

## 7. Strategy, growth outlook & management quality

**Strategy:** Move up the value chain from commodity batteries to IP-heavy, tender-driven defence/railway electronics; run ~9 divisions as independent businesses under a future holding structure; avoid M&A and debt; return surplus cash; incubate an ecosystem via a "Mittelstand Technology Partners" trust.

**Growth outlook:** FY26 budget ₹3,000 Cr (achieved ~₹3,300 Cr); **FY30 target ₹4,500 Cr**. Growth vectors, in order of conviction:
1. KAVACH 4.0 + TMS + trackside orders (booked, near-term visibility).
2. Electronic fuses (DRDO approvals in hand, ramp FY28+).
3. Torpedo motors (FY28 sales) and Navy lithium-ion cells (~₹200 Cr invested, "profitable year one").
4. Exports (Ni-Cd/defence, ~40% FY25 growth).

**Management quality:** High on engineering integrity, capital discipline and conservative communication. Watch: single-buyer (government) dependence, succession, and the deliberate slowness ("avoid indigestion") which caps near-term upside.

## 9. Risks (incl. credit-rating trajectory)

**Credit-rating trajectory (CARE):** Clear, steady upgrade — LT **A−** (Oct-21) → **A/Stable** (Oct-22) → **A/Positive** (Aug-23) → **A+/Stable** (Oct-24) → **A+/Positive** (Oct-25) → **A+/Positive reaffirmed** (Feb-26); ST A2+ → A1 → A1+. Outlook **Positive** (upgrade trigger: sustained TOI >₹2,800 Cr *and* PBILDT margin >18%; downgrade trigger: debt/EBITDA >1x or sustained TOI/profit decline). Total bank facilities ₹1,177 Cr; gearing 0.03x; interest cover 81x. **Credit risk is currently a non-issue** — this is a balance-sheet strength story, not a concern.

**Key risks:**
1. **Customer concentration / single-buyer risk:** Indian Railways + MoD drive the growth; the CLW order already saw ~25% cancelled. Government budget or tendering pauses = direct revenue holes.
2. **Earnings cyclicality/lumpiness:** FY26's ₹814 Cr PAT is not a steady-state; management flagged FY28 softness. Peak-multiple-on-peak-earnings risk.
3. **Raw-material volatility:** lead is ~60% of battery cost; raw materials are ~50–60% of TOI.
4. **Working-capital intensity:** 147-day cycle, high inventory (KAVACH build-ahead).
5. **Succession/age:** founder-led; no single CEO; holding-company structure untested.
6. **Environmental/regulatory:** lead-recycling is toxic and tightly regulated.
7. **Execution on unproven lines:** fuses, torpedo motors, e-trucks, lithium-ion cells are long-dated bets with uncertain conversion.

HBL screens *cheap on P/E* — but that P/E is on trough-of-cycle-likely-peak earnings, so the "cheapness" is misleading if earnings normalise. Conversely, if KAVACH/defence revenue sustains ₹3,000 Cr with 25%+ margins, FY27 EPS of ₹22–25 puts the forward P/E at ~28–32x, which is defensible for a high-ROCE, net-cash, IP-rich compounder. **The stock is a leveraged bet on whether FY26 was a new base or a spike.**

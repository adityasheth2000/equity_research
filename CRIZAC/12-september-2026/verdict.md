# Crizac Limited (CRIZAC) — Investment Verdict

**Analysis date:** 12 September 2026
**CMP:** ₹186.68 (NSE, 11 Sep 2026) | **Market cap:** ₹3,267 cr | **P/E:** 14.8x | **Div yield:** 4.3% | **Net cash:** ~₹570 cr
**52-wk range:** ₹388 → ₹160 | **Promoter holding:** 79.94%
**Sources:** FY26 Annual Report (AR2026), four investor presentations (Oct'25–Aug'26), four concall transcripts (Oct'25, Jan'26, May'26, Aug'26), Screener.in, and web research. Page refs are to the underlying documents.

---

## 1. Bottom line

**Verdict: Watchlist / show-me — not a multibagger candidate at today's price.** This is a genuinely high-quality-margin, asset-light, debt-free franchise **welded to a single, highly policy-sensitive revenue corridor (UK international students)** that has just entered a severe air pocket. The FY27 guide has been reset to **flat revenue**, Q1 FY27 revenue fell 4% YoY, and ~98% of revenue is still UK-linked. The stock is down ~48% in a year and looks statistically cheap (14.8x trailing P/E, 4.3% yield, ~₹570 cr net cash, 48% ROE), but "cheap" here is a bet on a UK visa-cycle bottom that has not yet been evidenced.

I would **not** buy today. I would revisit on hard evidence of (i) UK visa volumes bottoming, (ii) non-UK revenue becoming a real share of the mix, and (iii) two consecutive quarters of stable-to-rising revenue with revenue-per-student recovering. Until then, the flat FY27 guide and the concentration dominate. This is a **policy-cyclical special situation**, not a compounding franchise you can underwrite without watching the UK Home Office every month.

The single most important distinction: **the company has diversified its *sourcing*, not its *revenue*.** Applications now come from 85+ countries (India ~50%, Africa ~22%), but 97% of FY26 revenue and 98.7% of Q1 FY27 revenue still came from the **UK destination**. The headline "global platform" narrative runs well ahead of the financial reality.

## 2. What the business actually is

Crizac is a **B2B international student-recruitment intermediary**. It sits between independent study-abroad agents ("counselling partners") and universities:

- Agents source and counsel students; Crizac verifies, screens and processes applications on its proprietary platform, markets universities, and manages the admission/compliance workflow.
- **Crizac does not interact with students for counselling and does not recommend universities** — management stated this explicitly (Oct'25 transcript, p.14).
- It earns a **commission from the university** once the student enrols and clears ~30 days; it then pays the agent its share (typically 15–30 days after receiving university payment).
- **Cost of services = agent commissions = 62.5–70.5% of revenue** (FY26: ₹703 cr of ₹1,042 cr). This is the crux of the economics: the largest cost is **variable**, so growth does **not** produce software-style operating leverage. Management itself conceded the model is not "Zomato-like" (Oct'25, p.10-11).

|---|---|

| Enrolments | 24,697 (+13.8%) |

| Partner universities | 400+ (450+ by Q1 FY27) |
| Source countries / destination markets | 85+ / 11–12 |

**Promoters:** Dr. Vikash Agarwal (MD), Pinky Agarwal (largest holder), Manish Agarwal (CFO & Whole-Time Director) — family-controlled at ~79.94% post-IPO (AR2026; Screener). "Crizac Ltd" (India, listed) vs "Crizac UK" naming overlap recurs in filings.

**IPO:** pure offer-for-sale of **₹860 cr at ₹245**, listed **9 Jul 2025** (no fresh capital to the company; promoters sold down). Oversubscribed ~63x, listed at ₹281.

**Recent strategic pivot:** from pure B2B recruitment toward a self-described "AI-native global mobility platform" — ancillary services (accommodation, student loans, visa, insurance, forex), AI matching (EduMentor, US$2.5m over 5 years; ForeignAdmits), and a rapid string of small acquisitions.

## 3. Financial quality: excellent margins, deteriorating cash conversion, rising concentration

### Growth & profitability (consolidated, ₹ cr)

| | FY21 | FY22 | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 | 5-yr CAGR |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | 111 | 264 | 473 | 530 | 849 | **1,042** | **56%** |
| Operating profit (Screener) | 27 | 89 | 107 | 145 | 215 | **286** | — |
| OPM % | 25% | 34% | 23% | 27% | 25% | **27%** | — |
| PAT | 21 | 68 | 112 | 116 | 155 | **219** | **60%** |
| EPS (₹) | — | 67.77 | 112.15 | 6.62 | 8.86 | **12.52** | — |

FY26: revenue +22.7%, EBITDA +31.0%, PAT +41.4% (AR2026, pp.38-39). **ROE 48% (5-yr), ROCE 52% (FY26)** (Screener). Debt-free; net cash ~₹570 cr. These are genuinely excellent headline economics.

### But three quality-of-earnings cracks

**a) Cash conversion is weakening sharply.** FY26 CFO fell **21.5% to ₹144 cr** while PAT rose **41.4%**; CFO/OP fell to **76%** from **111–120%** in FY24–25 (Screener; AR2026 pp.240-241). Receivables are **₹238.5 cr (~84 days, ~23% of revenue)** with **zero expected-credit-loss provision** (AR2026 pp.261,269). Payment terms are 90–180 days. This is a working-capital-heavy business dressed as asset-light; the "asset-light" label is about fixed assets, not capital intensity.

**b) Related-party margin distortions.** A **₹56.74 cr termination payment to Crizac Informatics Limited UK** (a promoter-linked entity) was paid in FY26 for terminating a commercial agreement and a 10-year non-compete (AR2026 pp.267,277-278; concall May'26 p.6). Separately, a **related-party contract termination in FY24** appears to explain why the *presentations* show FY24 EBITDA of only ₹72.6 cr (11.4% margin) while *Screener* shows operating profit of ₹145 cr (27%) — a **~₹72 cr gap** (Presentation May'26 p.25 vs Screener). Also: Crizac **bought proprietary software from a group company for ₹104 cr** (Dec 2023, per web research). The independent audit did not cover two subsidiaries with **₹443 cr of assets and ₹1,012 cr of revenue** (AR2026 pp.232-233). Standalone revenue is mostly **intercompany charges to Crizac UK** (₹225 cr) — the standalone audit's **key audit matter is the transfer pricing of these processing fees** (AR2026 p.172).

**c) Concentration is extreme and rising.**
- **UK = 97% of FY26 revenue; 98.7% in Q1 FY27** (AR2026 p.68; Presentation Aug'26 p.13).
- **Top 10 universities = 65.5% of FY26 revenue; top 3 = 41% of Q1 FY27** (AR2026 p.29; Aug'26).
- **Two customers = ~₹420 cr = ~40% of consolidated revenue** (AR2026 p.280).
- Agent side is diversified (top 10 agents ~0.5%), which is the one genuinely balanced axis.

### Q1 FY27 — the inflection

| Metric | Q1 FY27 | Q1 FY26 | YoY |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Revenue | ₹201.2 cr | ₹209.5 cr | **−4.0%** |
| EBITDA | ₹60.0 cr | ₹64.9 cr | −7.6% |

**The central contradiction of the quarter: enrolments +15%, but revenue −4%.** Management's explanation: students shifted to lower-fee universities and Crizac **"missed a couple of bonuses and slabs"** at top-ranked partners, cutting revenue per student (Aug'26 transcript pp.7,10-11). That is plausible but unquantified and, crucially, signals **monetisation vulnerability**, not just a timing issue. FY27 guidance was reset to **"broadly in line with FY26" (flat)**, with Q2 also expected down and a hoped-for H2 recovery; EBITDA margin guided to **25–27%** (Aug'26 pp.6-9,17). Analysts recalled a prior 15–17% growth expectation; management disputes having given formal guidance.

## 4. The moat question: is there a durable advantage?

**Management claims** a technology moat (CAMS platform, AI screening, predictive matching), network effects, 5+ year average tenure with top-30 universities, compliance positioning under the UK Agent Quality Framework, and a data advantage. The company is **gaining share in a shrinking market** — its claimed share of total UK study visas rose from 3.5% (FY24) to 6.0% (FY26), and of UK visas to Indian students from 9.0% to 13.9% (Aug'26 p.18).

**The evidence cuts against a strong moat:**
- Management **explicitly confirmed there is no contractual exclusivity** with agents or universities (Oct'25 p.12). Almost all universities work with multiple platforms (Jan'26 p.9).
- The platform is **not a classic high-fixed-cost software business** — the dominant cost is variable agent commission, so scale doesn't produce structural margin expansion.
- Technology/AI claims are **never quantified** — no automation %, conversion uplift, cost saving, or AI revenue disclosed across any presentation.
- Revenue is concentrated in a handful of universities despite a 400+ network, and replicating deep relationships is a multi-year process (Aug'26 pp.11-12).

**Net:** the advantage looks like **practical stickiness + regulatory-compliance scale + a large agent network**, not a durable, defensible moat. That can be a real edge in a consolidation — but it is not the software-like moat the "AI-native platform" language implies, and it does not protect against a shrinking addressable market.

## 5. Industry & policy: the dominant risk is exogenous

Crizac's revenue is effectively a **levered derivative of UK study-visa issuance ~6–9 months prior**. That cycle is now in sharp decline:

| Policy / market development | Data |
|---|---|
| **UK sponsored study visas** (yr to Jun 2026) | ~383,500, **−11% YoY, −41% vs 2023 peak**; Q2 2026 India **−51%** YoY |
| **UK Graduate Route** | Cut from 24 → **18 months, effective 1 Jan 2027** |
| **UK International Student Levy** | **£925/student/yr** above first 220 students, **from 1 Aug 2028** |
| **Canada** | 2026 study-permit cap ~309,000 applications; higher proof-of-funds; high India rejection rates |
| **Australia** | Student visa fee raised to **A$2,500**; stricter Genuine Student rules |
| **US** | F1 refusal rate ~10-year high (~35%); processing delays |
| **IDP Education (closest listed comp, ASX)** | FY26 revenue **−11%**, Student Placement **volumes −27%**, NPAT **−74%**; guides FY27 volumes **down another 20–30%**; share price **−71% YTD** |

Management's stance is that tightening regulation favours large compliant players (share gains support this) and that students will redirect from the US to the UK/Ireland/NZ. **Both claims are partly true but incomplete:** compliance consolidation helps share within a market whose *absolute size is shrinking*, and management admitted it **does not track students' post-graduation employment or immigration outcomes** (May'26 pp.15-17) — even though those outcomes drive the willingness to pay for a UK degree. India's outbound study numbers fell ~31% from the 2023 peak to 2025 (Bureau of Immigration), and India's push for in-country branch campuses is a longer-term structural headwind.

**This is not a company-specific problem the company can manage away.** It is the entire sector's demand shock.

## 6. Management & governance: a mixed record

**Positive signals**
- Unmodified statutory audit; clean secretarial audit; no contingent liabilities or going-concern issues (AR2026).
- Management was reasonably candid on hard questions: conceded no exclusivity, admitted new geographies start at lower margins, disclosed FX bonuses inflated margins, and openly reset FY27 guidance to flat rather than defending a number (Aug'26).
- Chairman/MD roles separated (Nagle became Non-Executive Chairman; Vikash Agarwal remains MD) — a governance positive, though the new Chairman is non-independent.
- 40%-minimum dividend payout committed for 3 years (AR2026; Aug'26 p.13). FY26 paid ₹8/share = ₹140 cr (64% payout).

**Cautionary signals**
- **Thin disclosure discipline:** CFO could not explain ~₹57 cr of other liabilities on the Oct'25 call; Q3 applications first stated as ~1.2 lakh then corrected to ~1.02 lakh; presentation figures don't reconcile across quarters (e.g., FY26 EBITDA shown as both ₹2,824m and earlier ₹2,156m; Q2 FY26 PAT appears as ₹483m in one table and ₹929m on a P&L page).
- **Related-party complexity** (see §3b) is the biggest governance watch item — large intercompany flows, a ₹56.74 cr termination payment to a promoter-linked entity, and a ₹104 cr related-party software purchase.
- **Internal auditor resignation** (Ghoshal & Co., May 2026, "professional commitments"), replaced by Grant Thornton. Minor, but worth noting.
- **Depreciation method changed WDV → SLM prospectively from 1 Apr 2026**, flattering Q1 FY27 PAT by ~₹1.25 cr.
- **Management churn:** UK CEO exited the executive role; Pinky Agarwal resigned as director (Aug 2026); new CTO/CPO/CIO installed — occurring precisely when UK policy risk peaks.
- **Employee attrition climbed** 20.7% (FY24) → 28.7% (FY25) → **33.5% (FY26)** (AR2026 p.95).
- **ESOP pool expanded to ~7% of shares** outstanding (AR2026 pp.205,278) — future dilution risk.
- **Institutions are leaving:** FII holding fell 2.99% → **1.61%** and DII 7.05% → **3.22%** between Jun'25 and Jun'26 (Screener).

**Also an accounting oddity to resolve:** the AR notes that had the Global Tree acquisition occurred at the start of FY25, group revenue would have been higher by ₹8.24 cr but **profit lower by ₹17.31 cr** (AR2026 p.273). This disclosure is hard to reconcile and needs management clarification.

## 7. Capital allocation: cash-rich, M&A-heavy, acquisition economics opaque

- **Net cash ~₹570 cr**, debt-free, 40% minimum dividend — strong downside cushion and firepower.
- **Seven small deals in ~2 years:** StudiesPlanet (51%, ₹4.4 cr, LATAM), Global Tree Careers (51.04%, ₹21.1 cr, India B2C), EduMentor (US$2.5m, AI), Medway team (NZ), ForeignAdmits (37.4%, financing/visa), Inova (100%, <₹7 cr, Mexico/Netherlands). Combined 2026 deals <₹10 cr.
- These generated **₹24.4 cr goodwill + ₹13.3 cr brand value** (AR2026 pp.258-259). Management admits they are **not near-term revenue/EBITDA contributors** — they buy market access (Aug'26 p.8,14). Acquired entities contributed ~₹6 cr of Q1 revenue.
- **No acquisition-level revenue, margin, integration-cost or ROI disclosure.** That makes capital-allocation quality impossible to assess, and B2C (Global Tree, StudiesPlanet, ForeignAdmits) is a **new capability** for a B2B company.

Verdict on capital allocation: prudent balance sheet and shareholder returns, but the diversification-by-acquisition strategy is **too small to matter near-term and too opaque to judge**. The planned reduction of UK revenue to <60% within 3 years is an **aspiration with no quantified contract wins behind it**.

## 8. Valuation: not the bargain the multiple suggests

At ~₹187: **14.8x trailing P/E, ~9–10x EV/EBITDA** (net of cash), 4.3% yield, 48% ROE. On FY26 numbers that looks cheap. But:

- **FY27 revenue is guided flat and Q2 is guided down.** On a flat ~₹219 cr PAT, the forward P/E is ~15x — not distressed, and earnings quality is deteriorating (CFO/PAT ~66%).
- **The recovery is hoped-for, not evidenced.** The flat-year outcome depends on H2 "pent-up demand" offsetting a weak H1; management offered no quantified pipeline (Aug'26 p.17).
- **It trades at a premium to IDP Education**, a larger, more diversified, far more established peer that is at distressed valuations after guiding volumes down another 20–30%. If IDP is the right anchor, Crizac is not cheap.
- **Sell-side is thin (2–3 analysts) and stale:** an Equirus Buy at ₹345 (Dec'25) and a consensus target cut from ₹371 → ₹342 (Simply Wall St, Aug'26) sit far above the market, indicating estimates have repeatedly lagged reality. Some "broker targets" circulating online are AI-generated and unreliable.

The market has de-rated Crizac from ~28x at IPO to ~15x. The debate is whether this is a cyclical trough or the start of structural decline — and the present evidence is insufficient to declare a bottom.

## 9. Bear case vs. bull case

**Bear case (what could permanently impair the investment)**
1. UK study volumes are in **structural, not cyclical, decline** (levy from 2028, 18-month Graduate Route, dependants ban, tighter compliance). Crizac's revenue is levered to that one corridor.
2. **Monetisation pressure:** Q1 FY27 showed enrolments +15% but revenue −4% via mix/bonus loss. If revenue per student keeps falling, volume growth is worthless.
3. **University concentration** (top 3 = 41% of a quarter) means a few partner decisions on commissions/intake can swing results materially.
4. **Acquisition-led diversification is too small and too early**; the <60% UK target has no evidence behind it.
5. **Quality-of-earnings overhang:** related-party transactions, transfer-pricing as key audit matter, uncounted subsidiaries, receivables unprovided, weak and volatile cash conversion.
6. **Falling knife:** persistent institutional selling, new 52-week lows as recently as early Sep 2026, and a management transition at the worst possible time.

**Bull case (the multibagger path)**
1. Long-term structural growth in global student mobility (India + Africa + LATAM middle class); Crizac is a **share gainer in a shrinking market** — a classic consolidation winner if the market stabilises.
2. **Asset-light, 48% ROE, debt-free, ~₹570 cr net cash, 4.3% yield** — strong downside cushion and optionality to buy growth.
3. Multiple **new corridors** (LATAM source; Mexico/Netherlands/NZ/Ireland/US destinations; B2C and financing) that could re-rate growth once policy headwinds ease.
4. **Much of the UK shock may already be priced in**; if H2 FY27 confirms a trough, the stock could re-rate sharply from ~15x.
5. **UK visa data is a leading indicator** — an inflection there would be visible before revenue recovers.

**What would make me constructive (de-risking milestones):**
1. UK student-visa volumes demonstrably bottoming (monthly Home Office data).
2. Non-UK destination revenue becoming a **material, disclosed share** (>20–25%) of the mix.
3. **Two consecutive quarters** of stable-to-rising revenue with revenue-per-student no longer declining.
4. Evidence that Q1's revenue-per-student drop was mix/seasonality, **not structural commission compression**.
5. Cleaner related-party/transfer-pricing disclosure and better cash conversion.

## 10. Contradictions and open questions

- **UK concentration:** presentations/concall cite 97–98.7%; earlier Oct'25 call said 90–95%. The truth is likely ~97%+; management says it will fall below 60% in 2–3 years without quantifying a single new destination contract.
- **Guidance:** analysts recall a 15–17% FY27 growth signal in Q4 FY26; management says no formal guidance was given. Either way, expectations moved from ~15% growth to ~flat.
- **FY24 earnings:** presentation EBITDA (₹72.6 cr) vs Screener operating profit (₹145 cr) differ by ~₹72 cr — likely the related-party contract termination; needs reconciliation.
- **Net cash definition:** AR capital-management note implies ~₹163 cr; management cites ₹467 cr (then ₹570 cr) using a broader "cash + deposits + financial assets" definition (AR2026 pp.69,220,263,284).
- **Application counts and margins** are stated inconsistently within single documents (e.g., Q1 PAT margin 22.6% vs ₹47.1/₹201.2 = 23.4%).
- **Acquisition contribution:** GTCPL pro-forma disclosure (higher revenue, ₹17.31 cr lower profit) is unexplained.
- **Ancillary services:** the Aug'26 guide of "2–5% EBITDA" from ForeignAdmits is ambiguous (percentage points vs growth) and unquantified.

## 12. Final assessment

Crizac is a **profitable, capital-light, high-ROE, debt-free UK student-recruitment aggregator that is simultaneously gaining share and losing revenue** as its addressable market contracts under UK policy. The business quality is real but narrower than the "AI-native global mobility platform" narrative suggests: variable agent commissions cap structural margins, there is no contractual exclusivity, and ~98% of revenue — and ~41% from just three universities — sits in one policy-exposed corridor. Management is promotional in its presentation materials but reasonably honest in Q&A, yet governance and disclosure carry a related-party and quality-of-earnings overhang.

At ~15x trailing earnings with FY27 guided flat, the valuation is **not the deep-value opportunity the 48% drawdown implies**. It is a levered bet on a UK visa cycle bottom that has not yet been evidenced. The correct posture is **watchlist with a specific de-risking checklist** — not a multibagger buy today, and not an outright avoidance either, given the net-cash cushion, share gains and genuine long-term industry growth. Wait for evidence, then act.

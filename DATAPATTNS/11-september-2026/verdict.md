# Data Patterns (India) Ltd — investment verdict

**Date:** 11 September 2026  
**Ticker:** NSE: DATAPATTNS | BSE: 543428  
**Recommendation:** **Watchlist / accumulate only at a more reasonable valuation; not a fresh aggressive buy after the rerating**

## Executive view

Data Patterns is one of the more credible listed Indian defence-electronics businesses. It designs and manufactures mission-critical electronics across radars, electronic warfare, avionics, communications, satellites and automated test equipment. Its strongest attributes are unusually high in-house engineering content, a debt-free balance sheet, a long operating history, growing product ownership and a demonstrated ability to deliver high margins.

The business has genuine multibagger ingredients: a structural Indian defence-indigenisation cycle, expanding radar/EW content, a widening product catalogue, export optionality and a management team willing to fund product development before orders are fully visible. FY22–FY26 revenue grew from ₹315 crore to ₹925 crore, EBITDA from ₹141 crore to ₹371 crore and PAT from ₹94 crore to ₹271 crore. The FY26 annual report reports 32% revenue CAGR and 27% EBITDA CAGR for FY22–26, 17% ROE, 23% ROCE and zero debt (Annual Report 2025–26, pp. 4, 21–23).

However, the stock price already discounts a very successful outcome. At roughly ₹4,782 on 10 September 2026, market capitalisation was about ₹26,745 crore and trailing P/E was about 100x according to the market-data source consulted. Against FY26 PAT of ₹271 crore, this is close to 99x earnings. At that multiple, strong growth, high margins, order conversion and cash generation must all persist. My conclusion is therefore **excellent company, demanding price**: retain/track for long-term exposure, but require either a meaningful valuation reset or clearer evidence that the new order pipeline converts into profitable, cash-generating revenue.

## What the company does

Data Patterns is a vertically integrated defence and aerospace electronics solution provider. Its capabilities span design, embedded software, prototyping, manufacturing, testing and system integration. The company supplies products to programmes involving the Ministry of Defence, DRDO, BEL, HAL, BrahMos and other customers. Its stated product areas include radars, radar warning receivers, electronic warfare, avionics, communications, satellite subsystems, fire-control systems and automated test equipment.

The strategic direction is important: management is moving from supplying components or subsystems toward complete systems, while building reusable in-house IP. The FY26 report says the company has invested ₹131.19 crore in product development over the last two years, including ₹22.81 crore in FY26, with products in radar, EW and communications moving through customer evaluation. This creates a potentially valuable product pipeline, but also means reported growth depends on long development and acceptance cycles.

FY26 revenue grew 31%, EBITDA 35% and PAT 22% year-on-year. Gross margin was 63.2% and EBITDA margin 40.1%. The balance sheet is exceptionally strong for a manufacturing business: FY26 net worth was ₹1,736 crore, current ratio 10.45, and the company remained debt-free. CRISIL reaffirmed ₹775 crore of bank facilities at A+/Positive and A1 in September 2026, according to the Screener announcements page.

The weakness is cash conversion. Receivables rose 22% in FY26 and stood at ₹728 crore; receivable days were still 288, while inventory days were 318. The company explains this by government/customer acceptance cycles and project-specific advance procurement. The historical cash-flow series is volatile: FY25 operating cash flow was negative ₹899 crore despite ₹222 crore of PAT, while FY26 improved to positive operating cash flow of roughly ₹801 crore in the Q4/FY26 presentation. This is better, but not yet proof of consistently cash-generative economics.

## Latest quarter: the key reality check

Q1 FY27 revenue was ₹116 crore, up 16.8% year-on-year. EBITDA was ₹31.4 crore, down 2.2%, and EBITDA margin fell from 32.3% to 27.0%. PAT declined 13.5% to ₹22.1 crore, with PAT margin at 19.0% (Q1 FY27 presentation, slides 13–14). Management attributes the margin pressure mainly to product mix, timing and fixed-cost absorption, and continues to guide to 20–25% revenue growth and 35–40% FY27 EBITDA margins.

This explanation is plausible, but it is also the central item to verify. The business has a high gross margin—78.9% in Q1 FY27—but staff cost was 36.7% of sales and other expenses 15.2%. If quarterly mix remains unfavourable or the company hires ahead of product revenue, the 35–40% full-year margin target will be difficult. Investors should judge the next two or three quarters by both revenue and EBITDA/PAT conversion, not by order announcements alone.

## Order book: encouraging, but headline numbers need discipline

FY26 order inflow was approximately ₹1,121 crore, a 216% increase, and the reported order book was ₹926 crore at 31 March 2026. The Q1 FY27 presentation showed ₹928 crore of contracted order book at 30 June 2026, while also presenting ₹2,654 crore including orders received and negotiated. Management expects roughly ₹2,000 crore of fresh FY27 inflows beyond orders already received and negotiated.

The distinction matters. A negotiated order is not equivalent to a signed, executable backlog. Management said in the August 2026 call that about ₹17 billion of orders were negotiated or in the process of conversion, but the timing depends on customer approvals, contract formalisation and delivery schedules; HAL-related contracts were specifically described as separate from some of the negotiated pool. The company also said order-book growth is needed to reach at least three years of revenue visibility.

The correct interpretation is: the opportunity pipeline is large and management has credible customer access, but the near-term contracted backlog is much smaller than the headline pipeline. This reduces confidence in precise forward estimates and makes order conversion a key monitoring variable.

## Growth runway and competitive advantages

The strongest parts of the thesis are:

- **Engineering and IP:** in-house hardware/software design and deep experience in radar, EW and avionics can create switching costs once a product is accepted into a defence platform.
- **Indigenisation:** India’s policy direction is favourable. Defence production reached ₹1.78 lakh crore and exports ₹38,424 crore in FY26; the government also simplified export procedures and the Open General Export Licence framework in August 2026. This improves the addressable market, although policy support does not remove programme and acceptance risk.
- **Full-system ambition:** moving from components toward complete radars, EW suites and integrated systems can enlarge revenue per programme and create more repeatable IP-led economics.
- **Exports:** the company has supplied radar-related products to Europe and has a UK relationship; FY26 export order book was about ₹53 crore and Q1 FY27 international order book about ₹39 crore. This is strategically promising but still immaterial to current revenue.
- **Counter-drone and new radars:** management says it is developing active and passive counter-drone solutions, airborne radars, surveillance radars and newer EW products. These markets could be large, but most of the value remains contingent on trials, qualification and production orders.
- **Balance-sheet capacity:** zero debt and substantial cash/bank/investment balances allow continued R&D, capacity expansion and selective acquisitions without immediate financial stress.

The company is not without competition. It faces government-owned and private-sector defence-electronics suppliers, including BEL and specialist players such as Astra Microwave and Paras Defence, as well as customer-led development and imported alternatives. Data Patterns’ advantage is not a monopoly; it is a combination of speed, engineering breadth, qualification history and the ability to customise systems.

## ST Advanced Composites acquisition

Data Patterns agreed to acquire 100% of ST Advanced Composites for approximately ₹10 crore. Management says the acquisition adds composite manufacturing, supports radomes and other sensor applications, and helps the company offer more complete systems. Strategically this is sensible and financially small relative to the balance sheet. The burden of proof is execution: the asset should improve qualification, delivery control or customer access rather than simply add another low-scale manufacturing activity.

## Management and governance

Management communication is generally detailed on products, margins, order categories and working capital. It has also acknowledged customer approval delays, long receivable cycles and the difference between negotiated and contracted orders. That is a positive sign. The founders/promoters have substantial operating experience and promoter holding has been stable at 42.41% in the Screener shareholding table through June 2026; FII and DII ownership increased to 12.50% and 11.98% respectively.

The governance item to monitor is the cessation of independent director Sastry Venkata Rama Vadlamani, effective 9 September 2026, disclosed on Screener. This is not evidence of wrongdoing, but it warrants reading the company’s explanation and ensuring committee independence and board continuity remain strong.

## Main risks and thesis breakers

1. **Order conversion risk:** large negotiated/pipeline figures may be delayed, reprioritised or not awarded. Defence procurement is political, procedural and customer-timed.
2. **Customer concentration:** a meaningful portion of revenue is linked to government, DRDO, BEL, HAL, BrahMos and other strategic programmes. A programme delay can move quarterly revenue materially.
3. **Working-capital risk:** receivables and inventory remain very high relative to revenue. A return to negative operating cash flow would weaken the “high-quality compounder” thesis.
4. **Margin normalisation:** FY26 margins were strong, but Q1 FY27 already fell to 27% EBITDA. More lower-margin production, hiring or full-system ramp costs could reduce returns.
5. **Technology and acceptance risk:** R&D spend may not convert into production orders. Trials, certification and platform integration can take years.
6. **Valuation risk:** at about 100x trailing earnings, even excellent operating execution may produce disappointing shareholder returns if the multiple contracts.
7. **Export risk:** export contribution is still small and subject to end-user clearances, export controls, customer qualification and geopolitical changes.

## What would change my view

I would become more constructive if the company demonstrates, over the next 4–6 quarters:

- contracted order book growing without relying mainly on “negotiated” additions;
- revenue growth near or above 20% with EBITDA margin sustainably above 35%;
- operating cash flow broadly tracking PAT and receivable days moving materially below 250;
- export revenue becoming meaningful rather than remaining a small order-book option; and
- successful conversion of the new radar, EW, counter-drone and composite capabilities into repeat production orders.

I would turn cautious or negative if order conversion stalls, cash flow again diverges sharply from PAT, receivables continue rising faster than revenue, or margins remain near the Q1 FY27 level.

## Verdict

**Data Patterns is a high-quality strategic business with real long-term optionality, but the stock is priced for substantial success.** The company merits a serious long-term watchlist position because its engineering depth, product development and policy tailwinds are stronger than those of many small defence names. It does not merit an aggressive fresh purchase at roughly 100x trailing earnings unless the investor has very high conviction that 20–25% growth, 35–40% EBITDA margins and order conversion will persist for many years.

The most attractive risk/reward would likely come from either a valuation correction or a period in which cash conversion and contracted backlog improve faster than the market expects. Until then, classify it as **watchlist / selective accumulation on weakness**, not a conventional value buy.

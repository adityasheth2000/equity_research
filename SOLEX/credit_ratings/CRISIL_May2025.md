## Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its 'Crisil BBB/Stable/Crisil A3+' rating on the bank loan facilities of Solex Energy Limited (SEL). The reaffirmation reflects SEL's extensive industry experience of the promoters, significant growth in scale with improving margins, favourable demand outlook for the solar industry, and an above-average financial profile.

These strengths are partially offset by susceptibility to increasing competition and volatility in raw material prices & regulatory changes, a concentrated order book, and risks related to large, planned capex.

---

## Key Rating Drivers — Strengths

*   **Extensive industry experience of the promoters:** Promoters entered the solar modules/panels business in 2007 and have significantly ramped up operations. Currently, the company has a Solar Photovoltaic (PV) module manufacturing capacity of **1.5 GW** (enhanced from 700 MW).
    *   Successfully executed >10,000 projects.
    *   Associated with **24 OEMs** (2 international clients).
    *   Supplied >2 million modules till December 2024, depicting strong execution capabilities.
*   **Significant growth in scale with improving margins:**
    *   Revenue estimated at **Rs. 665 crore** in Fiscal 2025, up from Rs. 163 crore in Fiscal 2023.
    *   Orderbook of ~**Rs. 1,450 crore** to be executed in 12-18 months.
    *   Operating margins improved to **~10%** in Fiscal 2025 (from 8.3% in FY23), backed by capacity expansion and reduced rejection rates.
*   **Favourable demand outlook for the solar industry:** SEL benefits from the Indian government's emphasis on solar power and renewable generation plans.
    *   Protectionist measures (Basic Customs Duty of 40% on modules) and ALMM implementation increase competitiveness of domestic modules.
    *   PLI scheme and government initiatives (PM Surya Ghar, Kisan Urja Suraksha, etc.) are expected to drive demand.
*   **Above-average financial profile:**
    *   Net worth estimated at **over Rs 150 crore** as on March 31, 2025.
    *   Gearing remains at unity; Total Outside Liabilities to Adjusted Net Worth (TOL/ANW) at **1.9 times** (March 2025).
    *   Debt protection metrics are comfortable: Interest coverage and Net Cash Accrual to Total Debt (NCATD) estimated at ~**5.5 times** and **0.3 times** respectively for FY25.

---

## Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses

*   **Susceptibility to increasing competition and volatility in raw material prices & regulatory changes:**
    *   Exposure to domestic capacity additions and Chinese imports.
    *   Operating margins vulnerable to sharp fluctuations in raw material prices, which account for **80-82%** of operating income.
    *   Majority of raw material is imported, exposing profitability to forex rate fluctuations.
*   **Concentrated order book:**
    *   High customer concentration risk; **Rs. 1,275 crore** (~88%) of the Rs. 1,450 crore orderbook is from a **single client**.
    *   Exposure to risks associated with demand fluctuation and negotiating power shifts.
*   **Risks related to planned capex:**
    *   Undertaking brownfield expansion to increase capacity from **1.5 GW to 4 GW**.
    *   Project cost around **Rs. 200-210 crore**, with **75% debt-funded**.
    *   Reliance on timely commissioning without cost overruns and subsequent offtake of enhanced capacity.

## Liquidity

*   **Assessment:** Adequate.
*   **Bank Limit Utilisation:** Moderate at around **60%** for the past twelve months ended March 2025.
*   **Cash Accruals:** Expected to be around **Rs 100 crore**, sufficient against term debt obligations of Rs 18-40 crore over the medium term.
*   **Current Ratio:** Moderate at **1.22 times** (as of March 31, 2024).
*   **Cash & Equivalents:** Free cash of **Rs. 16.92 crore** as on March 31, 2025.

---

## Outlook & Rating Sensitivity

*   **Outlook:** **Stable**
*   **Upward Factors:**
    *   Sustained improvement in scale of operation and sustenance of operating margin, leading to higher cash accruals.
    *   Improvement in financial risk profile marked by TOL/ANW less than **2.2 times** on a sustained basis.
    *   Improved working capital management.
*   **Downward Factors:**
    *   Decline in scale of operations or operating margin, leading to cash accruals of **lower than Rs 35 crore**.
    *   Substantial increase in working capital requirements or higher debt-funded capex, weakening liquidity and financial profile.

## 1. What the Company Does

Solex Energy manufactures **solar photovoltaic (PV) modules** — the flat glass panels that convert sunlight into electricity. Think of them as the engine of every solar power plant. The company makes these modules at its factory in Surat, Gujarat.

**How they make money:**
- **Module sales (~90% of revenue):** They sell solar panels to large Independent Power Producers (IPPs) who build solar farms, as well as Commercial & Industrial (C&I) customers. These panels are sold under the Solex brand. Historically they did contract manufacturing (OEM) for 28+ Indian and 2 international brands, but have now transitioned to nearly 100% own-brand sales.
- **EPC services (~10% of revenue):** Engineering, Procurement & Construction — they design and build solar power projects for clients (rooftop, ground-mounted utility scale).

**Who are their customers?** Large IPPs (giga-scale solar developers), C&I customers, and government entities. The business is tender/contract-driven — orders are won competitively and executed over 12-18 month periods.

**Why it matters:** India is in a massive solar buildout — installed capacity went from 2.8 GW (2014) to 100+ GW (2025). The government wants 500 GW of non-fossil capacity by 2030, with ~60% from solar. Domestic module manufacturing is protected by import duties (40% BCD), mandatory local sourcing for government tenders (ALMM), and soon mandatory local cell sourcing (ALCM). Solex is a direct beneficiary.

## 3. Management Track Record — Hits and Misses

### Hits
- **Revenue delivered vs guided:** FY25 guidance of ₹800 Cr — actual ₹665 Cr (missed by 17%, blamed on 2-month line delay). FY26 guidance of ₹1,700-1,800 Cr — actual ₹1,621 Cr (missed by 5-10%).
- **NSE Mainboard migration:** Promised in Jan 2025 (Nov '24 call), delivered Oct 2025 — 9-month delay but executed.
- **Capacity addition:** Promised 1.5 GW by Mar '25 (from 700 MW) — delivered, though Line 2 came 2 months late. Promised 4 GW by June '25 — delivered by Nov '25 (5-month delay).
- **Brand transition:** Promised 80% Solex brand by H2FY25 — delivered 100% own-brand within a year.
- **PAT margin:** FY25 guided 5%, delivered 6.4%. FY26 guided 6-6.5%, delivered 6.1% (within range).

### Misses and Red Flags
- **Chronic timeline slippage:** Every single production line has been 2-5 months late. Nov '24 call promised Line 2 by Jan '25 → delivered Mar '25. Promised Lines 3-4 by "June 2025" in May '25 call → delivered Oct-Nov 2025. This is a pattern — execution is consistently slower than management claims.
- **Revenue miss:** FY25 missed by 17%. FY26 missed the ₹1,700 Cr lower end of guidance (₹1,621 Cr actual). FY27 guidance has already been revised down from ₹3,300 Cr (Nov '25) to ₹2,600 Cr (May '26) — "geopolitical uncertainty" cited.
- **Gross margin opacity:** Management consistently refuses to disclose gross margins on calls ("not readily available"). Only in Nov '25 call did they reveal ~29% gross margin after repeated analyst pressure.
- **CRISIL order book vs PPT order book discrepancy:** May '25 PPT showed ₹1,756 Cr order book, CRISIL (same month) showed ₹1,450 Cr. Management called it a "PPT typo" (Millions vs Crores).
- **ESM (Enhanced Surveillance Mechanism):** Stock was under ESM — reason never disclosed. Blocked mainboard migration until resolved.

**Verdict on management:** Promoter-CMD Chetan Shah (with his sons Vipul and Hemal as executive directors) is ambitious and knowledgeable about the solar industry. But ambition outpaces execution. Timelines are consistently optimistic. The scale-up from ₹665 Cr (FY25) to an aspirational ₹26,000 Cr (FY30) requires flawless execution — and this team has not demonstrated flawless execution.

## 6. Strategy, Growth Outlook, and Competitive Position

### The Bull Case
1. **India solar demand is structural, not cyclical.** Module demand growing from 21 GW (FY24) to 47 GW (FY30E). Exports potential of 30 GW from India.
2. **Protectionist moat:** ALMM (import ban for govt tenders), BCD (40% duty), proposed ALCM (cell sourcing mandate) create a walled garden for domestic manufacturers.
3. **Vertical integration roadmap:** Cell manufacturing (2.2 GW by Dec '27) will improve margins — management targets 15% PAT from cell business, FY29 EBITDA >20%.
4. **Scale advantage:** 4 GW module capacity makes Solex one of India's larger players. ALMM registration of 3.78 GW provides regulatory access.
5. **Low valuation:** P/E of 10.6x on TTM earnings vs peer median 32.6x. Even with execution discount, this appears cheap for 119% revenue CAGR.

### The Bear Case
1. **Module manufacturing is low-margin, commodity-like.** Barriers to entry are capex, not technology. Competition is intensifying — India targeting 150 GW module capacity. Price wars are inevitable.
2. **Chronic timeline underperformance.** Every production line 2-5 months late. FY25 missed guidance by 17%. FY26 missed by 5-10%. FY27 already revised down.
3. **Cell manufacturing is an unproven vertical.** Solex has zero experience making cells. CRISIL noted that "a lot of existing players were struggling in achieving the desired output and efficiency."
4. **Massive capex ahead.** ₹1,050 Cr for first cell line (₹700 Cr debt, ₹350 Cr equity). Plus ₹4,000 Cr MoU for 5 GW cells + 10 GW BESS over 4-5 years. Even with project financing, equity dilution and balance sheet strain are real.
5. **Client concentration risk** may have improved but was 88% just 14 months ago. Hard to verify without audited disclosures.
6. **Working capital and credibility concerns:** Inventory ballooned to ₹153 Cr (Sep '25), trade payables surged to ₹458 Cr (Mar '26).

### Competitive positioning
Solex is a tier-2 domestic module manufacturer. It is not comparable to tier-1 Chinese players (Jinko, Longi, Trina) or large domestic peers like Waaree, Adani, Tata Power Solar. Its edge:
- **ALMM registration** (highest number of models) — mandatory for government tenders
- **MCS BSI Kitemark** (first Indian company) — enables UK/Europe/US exports
- **Technology partnership with ISC Konstanz** (German R&D institute, inventors of back-contact technology)
- **Speed-to-market advantage** on G12R N-Type TOPCon modules (first in India, Oct 2024)

## 7. Risks

### Credit Rating Trajectory
| Date | Agency | Rating | Key Concern |
|---|---|---|---|
| May '25 | CRISIL | **BBB/Stable, A3+** | 88% client concentration; 75% debt-funded capex |
| Oct '25 | CRISIL | **BBB/Stable, A3+** (reaffirmed) | Bank facility update only |
| May '26 | CRISIL | **Monitoring/Under Review** | ₹4,000 Cr MoU — "nascent stage, impact on financial risk profiles will be monitored" |

CRISIL BBB is the lowest investment-grade rating. The "Stable" outlook from May '25 has transitioned to "monitoring" status post the Gujarat MoU. A downgrade to BBB- would be a serious red flag. Since Oct '25, rated bank facilities increased from ₹620 Cr to an expanded ₹619 Cr (bank mix changed but total similar).

### Key Risks (Priority Order)
1. **Execution risk on cell manufacturing:** 2.2 GW N-Type TOPCon Plus cell line is a first-time venture. Target Dec '27. If this fails or delays significantly, the backward integration thesis collapses.
2. **Commoditization of modules:** As domestic capacity explodes (150 GW target vs 47 GW demand by FY30), margins could compress. Solex has no differentiated technology moat in modules — everyone has TOPCon now.
3. **Balance sheet strain from capex:** Net D/E rose from 0.78x (FY25) to 1.34x (Mar '26 screener). Cell capex of ₹1,050 Cr (₹700 Cr debt) will further stress leverage.
4. **Customer concentration:** Down from 88% but exact current figure undisclosed. IPP dependency means one client's COD delay = Solex's revenue delay.
5. **Raw material price risk:** 80-82% of costs are raw materials (cells, EVA, glass, aluminium frames). Cell prices spiked 110-120% in FY26. Contracts have dynamic cell pricing clauses, but margin compression risk remains.
6. **Regulatory dependency:** ALMM, ALCM, DCR, BCD — any policy change could reset the competitive landscape.
7. **Technology obsolescence:** Industry shifting from PERC → TOPCon → back-contact → HJT → tandem. Existing Line 1 (Mono-PERC) needs upgrade capex.
8. **Geographic concentration:** Single manufacturing site in Surat, Gujarat. Any disruption (natural disaster, regulatory, labor) shuts down all production.

## Summary

Solex Energy is a high-growth solar module manufacturer riding India's renewable energy capex super-cycle. Revenue has grown 22x in 4 years (₹72 Cr → ₹1,621 Cr). The business generates strong returns (ROE 38%, ROCE 32%) with improving working capital cycles.

The core risk is that **module manufacturing is a commoditized, increasingly competitive business**, and Solex's plan to escape this via backward integration into cells (and forward into BESS) requires flawless execution over the next 3-5 years. Management has consistently promised more than they've delivered on timelines. The stock's low P/E of 10.6x reflects the market's skepticism about execution capability.

**At ₹940, the stock is interesting but not actionable without evidence that: (a) cell manufacturing is on track, (b) FY27 guidance of ₹2,600 Cr is credible, and (c) client concentration has genuinely diversified.** Track the next 2 quarterly results and the cell line fundraise closure before taking a position.

# Solex Energy Ltd — Discussion Notes

**Date:** 11 July 2026 | **CMP:** Rs 998 | **M-Cap:** Rs 1,078 Cr | **P/E:** 11.3x

---

## 1. The Industry in Simple Terms

Think of a solar panel like a car. The value chain is:

```
Sand (silicon) → Ingot/Wafer → Solar Cell → Solar Module → Installation
  (raw material)   (sliced wafers)  (the "engine")  (the "car")    (someone puts it up)
```

Right now, **Solex only builds the "car"** (solar modules). They buy "engines" (solar cells) from other companies, mostly Chinese. The Indian government is pushing everyone to **also make engines in India** (a policy called ALCM). Solex's big bet is to start making their own engines — which is much harder but doubles the margin they keep.

The broader tailwind: India is going from 82 GW solar capacity (FY24) to ~500 GW by 2030. That means 5-6x more panels need to be manufactured and installed.

They also do **EPC** (turnkey solar plant installation) — but this is only ~10% of revenue, done through a subsidiary.

## 3. What They're Trying to Do (Future)

The core bet is **making their own solar cells** (the "engine"):

| Milestone | Timeline | Capex |
|---|---|---|
| **2.2 GW solar cell line** | Dec 2027 | Rs 1,050 Cr |
| **2.5 GW module capacity addition** | FY28 | ~Rs 200 Cr |
| **3 GW additional cells** | FY28 | Part of larger plan |
| **5 GW more cells** | FY30 | Part of larger plan |
| **10 GW BESS manufacturing** | By 2030 | Under MoU |
| **2 GW ingot/wafer** | By 2030 | Future |

**Why this matters:** Right now Solex buys cells at market price and adds ~6-7% PAT margin making modules. If they make their own cells, management estimates PAT margin goes to ~15%. That's a structural margin upgrade.

The **BESS** (Battery Energy Storage System) is a completely separate new business. Think of it as giant batteries that store solar power made during the day to use at night. A separate subsidiary (Solex BESS Pvt Ltd) was incorporated in June 2026.

## 5. Management on Debt Leverage

**From the May 2026 concall:**

- **Current net D/E:** 0.57:1 (very comfortable)
- Cell line funding: **Rs 700 Cr debt + Rs 350 Cr NCD/CCD** (NCDs are like bonds, CCDs are convertible — a mix of debt and quasi-equity)
- NCD/CCD term sheet already received from investors; closure expected by **June 2026**
- Split: ~Rs 200 Cr NCD, ~Rs 150 Cr CCD

**From the Feb 2026 concall:**

- **TOL/TNW** (Total Outside Liabilities / Tangible Net Worth) will stay **below 4x** even after the cell capex — this is the key metric they're monitoring
- Management's words: "below 4 with the cell line"

**From May 2025 concall (earlier plan):**

- Had initially targeted Rs 1,000 Cr debt + Rs 500 Cr equity for a Rs 1,500 Cr total capex (which included additional module lines)
- "Two-third debt, one-third equity" was the stated philosophy
- Peak debt expected in FY27 (when borrowing is drawn but cell line revenue hasn't started yet)

**In plain English:** The company is taking on significant debt to fund growth, but keeping leverage within manageable levels (<4x total outside liabilities). The Rs 350 Cr NCD/CCD route is notable — it avoids full equity dilution while providing capital for the cell line.

### Why Revenue Was Missed (The Nuance)

The revenue shortfalls have genuine explanations:

1. **Line 2 (800 MW):** Delayed 3 months because they upgraded to the latest technology mid-execution rather than ship older equipment. Management chose tech quality over timeline — arguably the right long-term call.

2. **Lines 3 & 4 (2.5 GW):** Extended monsoon (6 months instead of 4) delayed customer site readiness. Finished goods piled up — Rs 150+ Cr inventory waiting for sites to dry. Money was already collected (Rs 400+ Cr advances from one customer). Not a demand problem — a timing problem.

3. **H1 seasonality:** Solar is structurally back-loaded. Management warned about this in every call. Q4 FY26 alone was Rs 886 Cr — equal to the **entire FY25**.

### Bottom Line

Management is **credible on strategy execution but unreliable on revenue forecasts.** They deliver on margins, technology, and operational efficiency. They miss on timelines (due to ambition + real external factors) and revenue (due to over-optimism). The shift to "conservative" FY27 guidance is a positive signal — they learned that disappointing the market hurts more than sandbagging and beating.

The key question: **Do you trust the Rs 2,600 Cr FY27 number?** If management's historical pattern of 30-40% over-estimation holds, actual FY27 may be Rs 1,800-2,200 Cr. But if the "conservative at 55% utilization" is genuine, there's upside to Rs 2,600 Cr — which would mean the stock at 11x trailing is deeply undervalued.

## 7. The Simple Investment Question

At Rs 998 with FY26 EPS of Rs 88.9, the stock is at **11x P/E**. If they execute the cell line and hit Rs 2,600 Cr in FY27 with 6-8% PAT margins, forward P/E is **5-7x** — very cheap for a company growing at triple digits. But that cheapness exists because the market is pricing in the risk that the cell capex (Rs 1,050 Cr of a Rs 1,078 Cr market cap company) goes wrong, takes longer than planned, or that revenue guidance continues its historical pattern of downward revisions.

**Date:** 11 July 2026 | **CMP:** Rs 998 | **M-Cap:** Rs 1,078 Cr | **P/E:** 11.3x

## 1. Company Overview & Industry Positioning

Solex Energy Ltd (SEL), founded 1995, is one of India's oldest solar PV module manufacturers. It operates a fully automated **4 GW module facility** in Surat (Gujarat) and has transitioned from an OEM-heavy model to a 100% owned-brand Solex portfolio. The company listed on NSE Main Board in Oct 2025 (migrated from NSE Emerge) and is pursuing backward integration into **solar cells (2.2 GW by Dec 2027)**, **BESS (10 GW)**, and eventually **ingots/wafers (2 GW)** under Vision 2030.

SEL holds the **highest number of ALMM-registered modules** in India — a critical competitive moat. Technology partnerships with ISC Konstanz (Germany, rear-contact cell R&D) and TT Vision (Malaysia, automation) support its premium positioning.

**Peer Context (Screener):** SEL trades at P/E 11.3x vs industry median P/E 37.9x — a deep discount attributed to its small-cap/SME origins and lower institutional ownership (DII 0.8%, FII 0.1%).

## 3. Strategy & Growth Outlook

### Near-Term (FY27)

Management guided **FY27 revenue of Rs 2,600 Cr** at conservative 55% capacity utilization, with PAT margin of 6-8%. This is lower than the earlier aspirational Rs 3,300 Cr — the downgrade is attributed to geopolitical uncertainty and raw material price volatility (cells up 110-120% post-Chinese holidays). The order book exceeds **Rs 3,400 Cr** across confirmed POs, signed MSAs, and advanced-stage deals.

- Module capacity: 4 GW operational, with 2.5 GW expansion being evaluated (potential greenfield in South India)
- Cell line: 2.2 GW TOPCon by Q4 CY2027 (Dec 2027) — Rs 1,050 Cr capex, funded Rs 700 Cr debt + Rs 350 Cr NCD/CCD
- BESS: Separate subsidiary (Solex BESS Pvt Ltd, incorporated June 2026); 10 GW MoU with Gujarat Govt

### Medium-Term (FY28-FY30)

Management expects cell manufacturing to drive EBITDA >20% by FY29. The integration is expected to add ~15% PAT margin from cells (vs current 6-7% standalone module margins). Module capacity target: 10 GW by 2030. Cell capacity target: 10 GW by 2030. Ingot/wafer: 2 GW. Total Vision 2030 capex: ~$1.5 Bn. 

Gujarat MoU (May 2026): Rs 4,000 Cr for 5 GW solar cells + 10 GW BESS manufacturing — phased over 4-5 years.

### Industry Tailwinds

- Solar installed capacity: 24.7% CAGR (82 GW in FY24 → 198 GW by FY28)
- Module demand: 37 GW in FY26E → 47 GW by FY30E
- Budget FY27 allocation for solar: Rs 30,540 Cr (+32% YoY)
- ALCM (cell localisation mandate) expected from June 2026 — likely to be extended given domestic cell shortage
- BESS market: 50 GW pipeline expected over next 24 months

## 6. Risks

### Credit Rating (CRISIL May 2026)
- Current rating: **BBB/Stable (long-term), A3+ (short-term)** — investment grade
- Rating placed **under review/monitor** following the Rs 4,000 Cr Gujarat MoU
- Key risk: execution and funding of Rs 4,000 Cr capex plan; CRISIL notes the project is at a "very nascent stage"
- No specific financial metrics disclosed in this update

### Operational Risks
- **Customer concentration:** Historically high (single client ~Rs 1,020 Cr in FY25). Management claims this has reduced with diversified IPP base
- **Raw material volatility:** Cell prices up 110-120%; silver, EVA, logistics costs rising. Contracts have pass-through clauses for some orders but FY27 renegotiation needed
- **Seasonality:** H1 is structurally weak for solar delivery; Q2 & Q3 FY26 demonstrated this sharply
- **ALCM risk:** June 2026 deadline for domestic cell mandate — if enforced strictly, SEL relies on third-party domestic cell supply until its own line starts (Dec 2027)
- **Geopolitical:** US tariffs, dollar volatility, equipment imports from China all uncertain

### Financial Risks
- **Leverage post-capex:** TOL/TNW expected to stay below 4x even with cell capex. Net D/E currently 0.57x
- **QIP dilution:** Rs 500 Cr equity raise being explored; exact structure not finalised
- **Working capital:** Improved to 35 days in FY26 but historically volatile (32-116 day range)
- **Free cash flow:** Negative Rs 114 Cr in FY25; turned mildly positive (Rs 7 Cr) in FY26

### Execution Risk
- Cell manufacturing is technologically complex; teething issues common across Indian cell manufacturers
- Utility (power, water) availability a key bottleneck — Sel has learned from peers and designed around Indian conditions
- BESS is an entirely new business with different technology, customers, and risks

## 9. Summary Assessment

Solex Energy is a **high-growth solar manufacturing pure-play** at an inflection point. FY26 demonstrated the power of operating leverage: 4x revenue growth in a single year, ROE at 38%, and balance sheet strengthening. The FY27 guidance of Rs 2,600 Cr (60% growth) is conservative relative to the order book (Rs 3,400 Cr) and industry tailwinds (24.7% solar capacity CAGR).

The valuation at 11x trailing earnings appears undemanding, but the discount is explained by: (a) the capital-intensive Rs 1,050 Cr cell capex that will stress the balance sheet before generating returns (18-24 month gestation), (b) raw material and geopolitical headwinds creating margin uncertainty, and (c) illiquidity given small free float (~33%).

**Key Call:** The investment case hinges on management's ability to execute the cell line on time and within budget, while maintaining module margins at 11-12% EBITDA through the transition. Success on the cell line would structurally lift margins (PAT from 6% to ~15%) and close the valuation gap with peers. The CRISIL rating being placed under review adds a layer of caution — monitor for any rating action as capex proceeds.

The recent Rs 628 Cr order (9 July 2026) and the Rs 3,400 Cr order book provide 12-15 months of revenue visibility. Combined with Rs 2,600 Cr conservative guidance, FY27 appears reasonably de-risked on the top line. The margin story is the swing factor.

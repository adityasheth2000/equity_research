# Connplex Cinemas — Supplementary Analysis

## 1. Business Models Explained

### Business A: Cinema Construction (the Builder)

Connplex acts as a **turnkey contractor** for franchisees. A local businessman in a Tier-2 town wants to open a cinema but lacks the expertise. He pays Connplex ₹2–2.5 Cr per screen and says "build it for me."

**What Connplex does:**
- Designs the cinema layout (acoustics, seating, lighting)
- Procures all materials centrally in bulk (chairs with Connplex logos, carpets, sound systems, projectors)
- Manages the entire construction process (3–4 months)
- Hands over a ready-to-operate cinema

**Economics:**
- Revenue per screen: ₹2–2.5 Cr
- EBITDA margin: 25–30%
- Nature: One-time, lumpy, project-based

**Simple analogy:** Connplex is like the contractor who builds a McDonald's outlet for a franchisee. The construction fee is earned once, then the relationship shifts to Business B.

---

### Business B: Operations / Royalty (the Landlord)

Once the cinema is running, the franchisee operates it day-to-day — sells tickets, popcorn, runs advertisements. Connplex earns a **20% royalty on nearly everything**, plus 100% of several ancillary streams.

| Revenue Stream | What Happens | Connplex's Cut |
|---------------|-------------|----------------|
| Movie Exhibition (Tickets) | Customer buys ₹100 ticket | Connplex gets ₹20 (20% royalty); ₹80 goes to franchisee |
| Food & Beverages | Customer buys ₹50 popcorn | Connplex gets ₹10 (20% royalty) |
| Advertising | Brands pay for on-screen/off-screen ads | Connplex keeps 100% |
| VPF (Virtual Print Fee) | Distributors pay for digital projection | Connplex keeps 100% |
| Convenience Fees | BookMyShow/Paytm booking charges | Connplex keeps 100% |
| Private Events | Corporate screenings, parties | Connplex keeps 100% |

**Why F&B royalty is accounted differently:** F&B revenue is recorded in the franchisee's books (not Connplex's) to avoid 18% GST on revenue sharing. Connplex's P&L only shows the ₹7.8 Cr as its 20% royalty share. For ticket sales, 100% of gross revenue flows through Connplex's P&L (₹62.1 Cr in FY26), and the 80% paid to franchisees is an expense line — so the P&L looks bigger than the economic reality.

**Simple analogy:** It's like McDonald's corporate earning a royalty on every burger sold worldwide. Connplex's royalty rate (~20%) is significantly higher than typical franchise royalties (4–6%), justified by Connplex handling more of the value chain (construction, procurement, technology).

---

### The Strategic Logic

```
Construction  ──►  Royalty
(fuel)              (engine)

One-time            Recurring
Lumpy               Compounding
Cash-heavy          Cash-light (once built)
Earns once          Earns forever
```

**The bet:** Construction fees fund the network build-out. As the screen count crosses ~250–300, royalty income dominates total revenue (>70%). At that point, the company transitions from a construction firm to a royalty compounder.

**The risk:** Construction consumes so much working capital that the company burns cash faster than royalty can replenish it.

## 2. What is SOTP (Sum-of-the-Parts Valuation)?

A valuation method for companies with **multiple distinct businesses** under one roof. Instead of applying one P/E ratio to all earnings, each business is valued separately based on its own economics, then summed up.

**Why use it for Connplex?**
- The construction business (project-based, lumpy, 5–8x EBITDA) should NOT be valued the same as the royalty stream (predictable, recurring, 12–15x EBITDA)
- A blended P/E would either overpay for construction earnings or undervalue royalty earnings

## 3. The Cash Flow Problem

### The Core Issue: You Pay Now, You Collect Later

The construction business has an intrinsically brutal cash cycle:

```
Month 1–2:  Connplex buys chairs, projectors, carpets, sound systems
            → CASH GOES OUT (₹1–1.5 Cr per screen)

Month 3–4:  Workers build the cinema, get paid salaries
            → MORE CASH GOES OUT

Month 5:    Cinema is complete → Connplex books ₹2.5 Cr as REVENUE
            → PROFIT APPEARS ON THE P&L

Month 6–7:  Franchisee takes 2–3 months to pay
            → CASH FINALLY ARRIVES
```

**Profit is recognized in Month 5. Cash arrives in Month 7.** With 40–45 cinemas under simultaneous construction, the gap between profit and cash compounds dramatically.

### The Numbers

| FY26 | Amount | Impact |
|------|--------|--------|
| Revenue booked | ₹148 Cr | Looks healthy |
| PAT reported | ₹26 Cr | Profitable |
| **Operating Cash Flow** | **-₹60 Cr** | Bank account tells the truth |
| **Free Cash Flow** | **-₹72 Cr** | After capex |
| | | |
| *Where the cash went:* | | |
| Inventory build-up | +₹9.1 Cr | Materials bought but not yet in completed cinemas |
| Receivables increase | +₹8.1 Cr | Cinemas delivered, franchisees haven't paid |
| Other working capital | +₹45 Cr | Advances, construction WIP, etc. |

### Why FY26 Was Worse Than FY25

| Metric | FY25 | FY26 | Driver |
|--------|------|------|--------|
| Screens added | 17 | 47 | 3x more construction = 3x more upfront cash |
| Screens under construction | ~10 | 40–45 | More WIP = more trapped cash |
| Employees | 96 | 143 | Hiring ahead of growth |
| Ad/Marketing spend | ₹9.6 Cr | ₹17.8 Cr | Brand building before revenue arrives |
| Debtor days | 50 | 53 | Worsening collections |
| Inventory days | 80 | 160 | Doubled — bulk procurement ahead of demand |

### The 4-Year Cash Flow Record

| Year | PAT (₹ Cr) | OCF (₹ Cr) | FCF (₹ Cr) |
|------|-----------|-----------|------------|
| FY23 | 2 | +9 | +3 |
| FY24 | 4 | +4 | +1 |

The inflection point happens when new construction starts slow relative to the base of completed, royalty-paying cinemas. Management estimates ~250–300 screens. Current count: 113.

## 4. What Happens If They Run Out of Cash?

Current cash buffer: **₹76 Cr** (IPO proceeds parked in short-term FDs, as of Mar'26).

### Option 1: Debt

| Pros | Cons |
|------|------|
| Strong balance sheet (D/E = 0.01) | Construction business has no hard collateral (assets sit on franchisee premises) |
| High interest coverage (146x) | SME companies rarely get large working capital lines |
| Market credibility from IPO | ₹50 Cr of debt at 10% = ₹5 Cr interest — PAT is only ₹26 Cr and shrinking |
| | **Realistic capacity: ₹20–30 Cr** — buys ~6 months |

### Option 2: Fresh Equity

| Scenario | Dilution | Shares Post-Raise |
|----------|----------|-------------------|
| Raise ₹50 Cr at CMP (₹195) | 13.4% | 2.16 Cr |
| Raise ₹50 Cr at ₹150 | 17.5% | 2.24 Cr |
| Raise ₹100 Cr at ₹150 | 35.0% | 2.58 Cr |

Key risk: **timing matters.** Raising when the stock is beaten down (₹150–160) causes heavy dilution. Raising from strength (₹250+) is manageable. Current trajectory (down 37.5% from 52-week high) suggests they'd raise into weakness.

### Option 3: Slow Down Construction

The unspoken option — reduce new project starts:
- Let the 113-screen base generate cash
- Collect ₹21.3 Cr of outstanding receivables
- Right-size ₹22.8 Cr of inventory
- OCF likely turns breakeven to slightly positive

**Trade-off:** Destroys the 80% revenue CAGR narrative that the stock is priced on.

### The Worst Case

Burning through IPO funds → raising dilutive equity at a low price → simultaneous growth slowdown as the market realizes the hyper-growth story is over. Result: earnings downgrade + dilution = stock gets cut in half.

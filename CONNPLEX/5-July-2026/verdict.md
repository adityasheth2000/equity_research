# Connplex Cinemas Ltd — Verdict

**Date:** 5 July 2026 | **CMP:** ₹195 | **Market Cap:** ₹372 Cr | **NSE SME: CONNPLEX**

---

## Company Overview

Connplex Cinemas Ltd (incorporated 2015, listed August 2025) operates an asset-light franchise cinema chain targeting Tier 2/3/4 cities in India. The business has two main revenue streams: (1) **Cinema Construction/EPC** — designing and building cinemas for franchisees (one-time revenue), and (2) **Operations/Royalty** — earning 20% royalty on ticket sales, F&B, and advertising from franchise-operated cinemas (recurring revenue).

The company runs three cinema formats: Express (compact, 55–120 seats), Signature (premium, 50–100 seats), and Luxuriance (luxury, 30–90 seats). As of FY26, it operates 41 cinemas with 113 screens across 9 states, with Gujarat (47 screens) and Bihar (29 screens) being dominant geographies.

**Business Model Moats:**
- Asset-light franchise model (FOFO and FOCO) minimizes company capex
- Focus on underserved Tier 2–4 towns with limited competition
- Lower setup costs vs. multiplexes (~₹7,000 sq ft vs. 30,000–40,000 sq ft, and ₹2.5 Cr/screen vs. ₹10–12 Cr)
- Higher utilization rates from smaller, right-sized auditoriums
- Centralized procurement for franchise construction creates bundling advantage

**Competitive Position:** SME player with no direct listed peer. PVR Inox operates in a different scale/format. The company effectively creates new cinema markets rather than cannibalizing existing ones. However, the franchise model means Connplex's recurring take-rate is limited to ~20% of exhibition revenue.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue Trajectory (₹ Crores)
| Metric | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|--------|------|------|------|------|
| Revenue | 25 | 60 | 96 | 148 |
| Operating Profit | 3 | 6 | 26 | 35 |
| OPM % | 10% | 10% | 27% | 24% |
| PAT | 2 | 4 | 19 | 26 |
| PAT Margin | 6.5% | 6.8% | 19.9% | 17.7% |
| EPS (₹) | 33.0* | 81.8* | 13.55 | 13.65 |

*Note: Pre-IPO EPS reflects smaller equity base (₹0.5 Cr). Post-IPO equity = ₹19.1 Cr.

The revenue story is compelling: 3-year CAGR of 80%. The inflection point from FY24 to FY25 is stark — revenue went from ₹60 Cr to ₹96 Cr, and PAT jumped from ₹4 Cr to ₹19 Cr, reflecting operating leverage kicking in as screens scaled from 49 to 66.

However, **margins peaked in FY25 and are now compressing** — EBITDA margin fell from 27.5% to 23.7% and PAT margin from 19.9% to 17.7% in FY26. Management attributes this to "aggressive brand positioning" and front-loaded hiring for the franchise sales team (employees up from 96 to 143). At the current run-rate, the business appears to be sacrificing short-term margins for scale.

### Revenue Mix (FY26)
| Segment | Revenue (₹ Cr) | Nature |
|---------|---------------|--------|
| Cinema Construction | 64.8 | One-time, lumpy |
| Movie Exhibition | 62.1 | Recurring (but 80% pass-through) |
| F&B Royalty | 7.8 | Recurring (20% take-rate) |
| VPF Share | 3.2 | 100% margin recurring |
| Advertising | 2.9 | Growing |
| Convenience Fees | 2.0 | 100% margin |
| Other | 4.9 | Lease sub-lease + misc |

Notably, cinema construction is ~44% of total revenue (down from ~50% in H1) — the mix is shifting toward recurring royalty as the screen base grows. Management indicates that once the company crosses ~200 screens, royalty will contribute >70% of total income.

### Balance Sheet Health
Post-IPO (Aug 2025, ₹90.27 Cr raised at ₹177), the balance sheet transformed:
- Net worth: ₹134.3 Cr (vs. ₹24.6 Cr pre-IPO)
- Net cash: ₹76.2 Cr (includes IPO proceeds parked in current investments)
- Debt: negligible (₹1.6 Cr total borrowings)
- Current ratio: 5.64, Quick ratio: 4.72 — extremely liquid

### The Red Flag: Working Capital and Cash Flow

This is the single biggest concern:
| Metric | FY23 | FY24 | FY25 | FY26 |
|--------|------|------|------|------|
| Debtor Days | 18 | 23 | 50 | 53 |
| Inventory Days | 97 | 55 | 80 | 160 |
| Working Capital Days | 52 | 17 | 54 | 255 |
| Cash Conversion Cycle | 43 | 10 | 63 | 124 |
| Operating Cash Flow (₹ Cr) | +9 | +4 | +3 | **-60** |
| Free Cash Flow (₹ Cr) | +3 | +1 | +1 | **-72** |

FY26 operating cash flow was negative ₹60 Cr despite PAT of ₹26 Cr — a massive divergence. The CFO/Operating Profit ratio fell from 336% in FY23 to -147% in FY26. The ₹72 Cr negative FCF is not just a timing issue; it signals that the EPC-heavy business model consumes far more working capital than it generates in reported profits.

**Why this matters:** The cinema construction business requires Connplex to procure materials centrally (inventory), complete construction, and then wait for franchisee payments (receivables). With 40–45 cinemas under construction simultaneously, the cash lock-up is enormous. Inventory has doubled year-on-year (₹13.7 Cr → ₹22.8 Cr) and trade receivables have grown from ₹13.1 Cr to ₹21.3 Cr.

The company also recognized that 10 screens completed in FY25 only started operations in FY26 due to licensing delays in Bihar (election-related). This lumpiness creates a structural mismatch between cash outflows and inflows.

**Bottom line:** The business model is profitable on a P&L basis but consumes significant working capital during the growth phase. The IPO proceeds (₹76 Cr remaining) provide runway, but if the cash burn rate continues, the company may need another capital raise before the royalty base becomes self-sustaining (~250–300 screens, per management).

## Strategy & Growth

**Near-term (FY27–FY28):**
- 230+ upcoming screens in pipeline (order book)
- FY27 target: 80–100 screen additions → likely 190–213 screens by Mar'27
- Expansion into 6 new states: J&K, Nagaland, Punjab, Jharkhand, Karnataka (Bangalore), Chhattisgarh
- Capex funded by IPO proceeds (₹76 Cr available) — primarily for projectors, LED screens, corporate office

**Long-term (by 2030):**
- Management vision: 1,000 screens (India currently has ~9,500 screens total vs. 45,000 in the US)
- At ~200 screens, royalty income expected to become the dominant revenue stream (>70%)
- Process patent received for non-ECI servers on active LED screens (future tech play)

**Positive:** The ambition and execution pace are undeniable. 113 screens from 24 in FY23 is a 68% CAGR in screens. The order book of 343 total screens (113 operational + 230 pipeline) provides multi-year visibility.

**Concern:** Real estate delays are a recurring theme — management notes that developers are taking 6–12 months for civil work handover, and licensing delays (especially Bihar elections) create lumpiness. The 230-screen pipeline timeline extends through FY28, suggesting construction cadence may be slower than guided.

## Management Quality

**Leadership:**
- **Anish Patel (MD):** 6+ years in entertainment, IT background. Focus on innovation and customer experience.
- **Rahul Dhyani (JMD):** 6+ years, leads franchise expansion and technology integration.
- **Pujan Thakkar (CFO):** Handles financial reporting and investor relations.

**Assessment — Mixed:**

*Positives:*
- Clearly articulated vision and business model
- Execution track record (68% screen CAGR over 3 years)
- High promoter holding (70.1%) with zero pledged shares — alignment with shareholders
- Responsive to investor queries (offers offline follow-ups)

*Negatives:*
- **Concall evasion:** Management repeatedly deflected direct questions on margins, revenue recognition, and cash flow with vague answers (e.g., "let me complete the question first," "we'll discuss separately offline")
- **Lack of segmental disclosure:** Investors repeatedly asked for construction vs. operations margin split. Management claims it's "difficult to determine separate margins" due to shared costs — unconvincing for a company with two clearly distinct businesses
- **Experience depth:** 6 years of industry experience for both MD and JMD is relatively limited, though they've grown the business impressively from scratch
- **Recent red flag:** Company Secretary/Compliance Officer Raj Kumar Pareek resigned on July 4, 2026 — just weeks after the FY26 concall
- **Promoter background:** The company was formerly "VCS Industries Limited" — unclear what business it was in before the cinema pivot

**Verdict on Management:** The aggressive growth story is their strength, but the opacity around financial reporting and the working capital problem raise governance concerns. The CS resignation is worth monitoring.

## Risks

### Business Risks
1. **Content Dependency:** Exhibition revenue depends entirely on film quality. A weak release slate can decimate footfalls (occupancy is only 30–35% even in good periods)
2. **OTT Competition:** While Tier 2/3 cities have less OTT penetration, this remains a structural risk
3. **Franchisee Concentration:** The model depends on franchisee financial health. If franchisees struggle with profitability, the construction pipeline collapses
4. **Real Estate Delays:** Licensing and developer handover delays create revenue lumpiness

### Financial Risks
5. **Working Capital Spiral:** If construction pace accelerates, working capital consumption will worsen before improving. The ₹76 Cr IPO corpus may deplete faster than modeled
6. **Revenue Recognition Risk:** Completion-based recognition (not % of completion) means P&L can show strong numbers while cash flow deteriorates — a classic red flag in project-based businesses
7. **Margin Dilution:** Employee costs up 90% YoY, other expenses up 85% — outpacing 54% revenue growth
8. **Post-IPO Dilution:** ROE compressed from 163% (FY25, pre-IPO small equity base) to 44% (FY26) — the real return on the expanded capital base is much lower

### Governance Risks
9. **FII stake declining:** FII holding reduced from 2.5% (Sep'25) to 0.7% (Mar'26) — DII also from 8.1% to 7.0%
10. **No dividend:** Company has never paid dividends despite profitable operations

| P/B | 2.77x | 1.33x | 4.11x |
| EV/EBITDA | 9.66x | 7.03x | 9.66x |
| ROE | 32.8% | 3.3% | 7.2% |
| ROCE | 43.6% | 4.8% | 7.5% |
| Debt/Equity | 0.01 | 0.92 | 0.69 |

*Note: PVR Inox numbers are weak due to post-merger write-offs and industry headwinds. The comparison is somewhat apples-to-oranges given scale difference.

Connplex screens as cheap on P/E but this ignores:
- The P/E denominator includes one-time construction revenue (not recurring)
- Negative FCF means the earnings quality is poor
- The stock is down 37.5% from its 52-week high of ₹312
- At IPO price of ₹177, listing gains have all but evaporated (CMP ₹195)

On an **EV/EBITDA** basis (9.66x), Connplex is in line with the sector median but trades at a premium to PVR Inox (7.03x). For a company with negative FCF and working capital stress, this valuation appears full.

A reasonable approach would be to value the royalty business and construction business separately:
- **Construction:** At ~₹65 Cr revenue, 25% EBITDA margin = ~₹16 Cr EBITDA. This is a lumpy, project-based business — 5–8x EBITDA is appropriate → ₹80–130 Cr
- **Royalty/Operations:** At ~₹78 Cr exhibition revenue, 20% royalty take-rate = ~₹15.6 Cr effective revenue. Plus F&B/VPF/Ads/Conv. fees (100% margin items) = ~₹10 Cr. Total royalty EBITDA ~₹19 Cr (at the guided 27% blended). This is recurring — 12–15x → ₹228–285 Cr

Sum-of-parts EV: ₹308–415 Cr. After adjusting for net cash (₹76 Cr), equity value = ₹384–491 Cr. Stock appears **fairly valued to slightly undervalued** at ₹372 Cr market cap, but the negative FCF warrants a significant discount.

## Overall Verdict

**Connplex Cinemas is a high-growth SME with a differentiated business model but serious cash flow problems that the market appears to be underappreciating.**

The bull case is compelling: a capital-light franchise model in underpenetrated Tier 2–4 markets, 80% revenue CAGR, 343-screen order book, and a long runway for growth in a country with only 9,500 screens.

The bear case is equally strong: negative ₹60 Cr operating cash flow in a year the company reported ₹26 Cr PAT, ballooning working capital (255 days), margin compression, and management opacity around segmental reporting. The construction business masks the low take-rate on the exhibition business.

At 14.3x P/E and 9.66x EV/EBITDA, the stock is not expensive on headline numbers but appears fully valued given the FCF deficit. The ₹76 Cr net cash provides a buffer, but at current burn rates, it buys perhaps 18–24 months before the capital raise question resurfaces.

**For a new investor:** Wait for evidence of OCF normalization and working capital discipline. The FY27 screen addition target (80–100+) increases the risk of working capital stress before the royalty base becomes self-sustaining. A price below ₹150–160 (below IPO price) would offer a better risk-reward.

**RATING: NEUTRAL with a negative bias.** Structural story is intact but financial execution needs to improve materially before the risk-reward turns favorable.

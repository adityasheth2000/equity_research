# Agarwal Industrial Corporation Ltd (NSE: AGARIND)

**Analysis date:** 11 September 2026  
**Price:** ₹432  
**Market capitalisation:** ₹646 crore  
**Conclusion:** **Watchlist / avoid fresh buying until earnings and bitumen volumes recover**

## Executive view

Agarwal Industrial Corporation (AICL) is an integrated bitumen-import, storage, manufacturing and logistics platform, with a smaller LPG logistics, ship-chartering and wind-power presence. The integration is real: AICL owns/controls vessels, terminals and road tankers, and management says roughly 60–70% of bitumen is moved through its own vessels. That can lower freight costs and improve supply reliability.

However, the current evidence does not support a multibagger thesis yet. The business is still fundamentally exposed to a low-margin, cyclical and working-capital-heavy commodity chain. The FY26 downturn was severe, not cosmetic: consolidated revenue fell 31%, bitumen volume fell 39%, and PAT fell 62%. The latest external industry data also points to weak FY27 conditions, with West Asia-related supply disruption, sharply lower imports/consumption and slower highway construction. AICL may eventually benefit from a recovery or gain share when smaller competitors struggle, but that is an option—not an established earnings trajectory.

At ₹432, the stock is not expensive on depressed FY26 earnings (roughly 15x reported consolidated EPS of ₹29.13 and 0.94x book value), but the discount is justified by earnings volatility, supplier/geopolitical concentration, low current returns and the uncertain payoff from recent capex/acquisition. The right posture is to wait for evidence that volumes, margins and credit headroom are normalising.

## What the company does

| Activity | What matters economically |
|---|---|
| Bitumen and allied products | Core business; imported bitumen is sold in bulk and processed into paving/industrial/modified products. FY26 standalone bitumen/allied volume was 3.26 lakh MT versus 5.36 lakh MT in FY25. |
| Shipping and chartering | 11 vessels in the FY25 presentation, with approximately 1.14 lakh MT capacity; own vessels transport a majority of AICL's bitumen and also earn chartering revenue. |
| Road logistics | 350+ owned bitumen tankers and a larger combined fleet including LPG tankers; contracts with major oil companies and customers provide utilisation, but margins are not structurally high. |
| Storage/manufacturing | Port-linked storage and plants across western, southern, central and eastern India create a distribution advantage and reduce turnaround time. |
| Other | LPG logistics, windmills and an authorised Ashok Leyland service centre. These diversify the group but are not yet large enough to change the investment case. |

The competitive advantage is therefore operational rather than intellectual-property based: location, storage, fleet ownership, sourcing relationships and the ability to bid with a lower delivered cost. It is useful, but vulnerable to freight, crude/bitumen prices, vessel downtime, monsoons, supplier concentration and customer working-capital stress.

## Financial trajectory

### Long term: strong scale-up, but with a cyclical break

Screener's consolidated history shows revenue rising from ₹222 crore in FY15 to ₹2,399 crore in FY25, while PAT rose from ₹6 crore to ₹116 crore. That is impressive scale creation. But the earnings record is not smooth: FY26 revenue fell to ₹1,652 crore and PAT to ₹43.6 crore. FY26 operating margin was approximately 8.2% on Screener's presentation of operating profit, but depreciation and finance costs consumed much of the operating profit, leaving PAT margin at only about 2.6%.

### Balance sheet: better, but not risk-free

At 31 March 2026, consolidated equity was ₹689 crore and borrowings were ₹336 crore, down from approximately ₹427 crore the prior year. Cash and cash equivalents were ₹43 crore, with additional bank balances and current investments. The reduction in borrowings and the ₹235 crore FY26 operating cash flow are positives.

The cash-flow number needs interpretation. A large part came from releasing working capital: inventories fell by ₹78 crore and receivables fell by ₹117 crore, while trade payables fell by ₹109 crore. This is healthy cash conversion in FY26, but it is not equivalent to recurring earnings power. The business may again consume cash if volumes recover and inventory/receivables rebuild.

Credit quality remains investment grade but has weakened at the margin. CRISIL reaffirmed **A** but revised the outlook from Stable to **Negative** in March 2026. It cited roughly 20% revenue decline and a 260 bps operating-margin decline in 9M FY26, 81% average bank-limit utilisation, Middle East supplier concentration and large debtors. The rating agency's key downside trigger is net cash accrual below ₹85 crore or a material rise in working-capital needs/debt-funded capex.

## Management execution and capital allocation

Management has repeatedly guided to 15–20% volume growth, 6–7 lakh MT volumes and roughly ₹4,000–₹4,300 EBITDA per tonne. Those targets were not achieved: FY25 volume was about 5.36 lakh MT, and FY26 volume fell to 3.26 lakh MT. Management attributed shortfalls to dry-docking, cyclones, monsoon effects and geopolitics. Those explanations are credible, but the repeated optimism makes guidance credibility an important monitoring item.

The Mangalore storage project and vessel additions can improve economics if utilisation rises. But they also increase fixed costs and depreciation before the volume recovery is visible. The Karwar acquisition deserves particular scrutiny:

- AICL paid ₹22 crore in cash for 100% of Konkan Storage Systems.
- Identifiable net assets acquired were only ₹1.15 crore.
- The transaction created ₹20.85 crore of goodwill.
- The entity was consolidated for only three months in FY26.

This could be a strategically valuable port asset, but the purchase price embeds a very large premium to book net assets. Investors should demand evidence of incremental throughput, storage income and return on the acquired terminal rather than accept strategic rationale alone.

There are also related funding exposures. The FY26 auditor's report says AICL had ₹94.7 crore of unsecured loan outstanding to its UAE subsidiary and a ₹37 crore guarantee for that subsidiary's bank borrowing. The auditor did not qualify the accounts, but these are material intra-group exposures that should be tracked alongside vessel economics and foreign-exchange risk.

## Industry and external context

The structural demand case is real. PPAC reports India's national highway network expanded materially over the last decade, and bitumen consumption remains linked to road construction. AICL's own annual report highlights Bharatmala, PM Gati Shakti and continued road expansion.

The near-term counterpoint is stronger. PPAC reported FY25 bitumen consumption of 8.33 MMT, down 5.4% from 8.81 MMT. In FY26/FY27, West Asia disruption became a direct supply-chain problem: reporting based on MoPNG data showed April–May 2026 consumption down roughly one-third year-on-year, while FY27 highway construction in the first two months was reported down 45% year-on-year. India is import-dependent for a meaningful portion of bitumen, with imports heavily concentrated in West Asia. This is directly relevant to AICL because CRISIL says 80–85% of its sourcing comes from its top three Middle East suppliers.

The industry can recover when road execution resumes, but recovery may not immediately mean higher profits. Higher bitumen prices can delay projects, squeeze contractors and raise AICL's working-capital requirement. AICL's integrated fleet and terminals are most valuable in a supply-constrained market, yet the same disruption can reduce volumes and vessel utilisation before it creates pricing power.

## Valuation

At ₹432, Screener shows approximately:

- P/E: 15.8x;
- price/book: 0.94x;
- EV/EBITDA: 7.06x;
- dividend yield: 0.76%;
- ROCE: 7.7%; ROE: 6.2%;
- debt/equity: 0.50x.

The valuation is reasonable if FY26 is a trough and PAT can return toward ₹100–₹120 crore. In that recovery case, the stock could rerate. But if FY26–FY27 earnings are closer to ₹40–₹60 crore, the apparent P/E is materially higher than it looks, while low ROCE would limit compounding. Book value is not a sufficient margin of safety because a large asset base must earn acceptable returns and the Karwar purchase added goodwill rather than tangible net worth.

## What could make this a multibagger?

The bull case requires several things to happen together:

1. Bitumen volume returns above 6 lakh MT and ultimately approaches management's FY28 ambition of roughly double FY25 volume.
2. EBITDA per tonne sustains above ₹4,000 without sacrificing working-capital discipline.
3. Vessel utilisation normalises and chartering offsets the lower margin of trading/manufacturing.
4. Mangalore and Karwar terminals produce incremental throughput and returns, rather than simply adding depreciation and goodwill.
5. Net debt stays controlled while capacity expands.
6. Road construction demand recovers and West Asia supply lanes normalise.

If these conditions are met, current valuation could look inexpensive. The problem is that none has been demonstrated after the FY26 shock. The company is better viewed as a cyclical recovery candidate with operating leverage than as a proven high-quality compounder.

## Key risks and disconfirming evidence

- **Commodity and forex risk:** bitumen is a crude derivative; price volatility and currency movements can compress margins before pass-through.
- **Supplier concentration:** 80–85% of sourcing from the top three suppliers, concentrated in the Middle East.
- **Customer/receivable risk:** CRISIL flagged debtors outstanding over six months; a downturn can trap cash in smaller contractors/customers.
- **Shipping and fixed-cost risk:** vessels create strategic control but also depreciation, dry-docking and utilisation risk.
- **Working-capital risk:** FY26 cash generation benefited from working-capital release; a volume recovery can reverse this.
- **Acquisition risk:** Karwar's ₹20.85 crore goodwill must be justified by future earnings and throughput.
- **Governance/related-party monitoring:** material loans and guarantees to the UAE subsidiary merit continuing disclosure review.
- **Guidance credibility:** repeated 15–20% volume-growth guidance has not translated into consistent delivery.

## Final verdict

AICL has a credible integrated platform, strong promoter ownership, a tangible logistics footprint and a balance sheet that remained solvent through a very difficult year. Those are real positives. But the evidence currently describes a capital-intensive commodity/logistics business in an earnings and industry trough, not a durable high-ROCE compounder.

**Investment stance: Watchlist / avoid fresh buying for now.** A contrarian purchase could work if FY27 volumes recover and the market is over-discounting a temporary geopolitical shock. The safer signal is a combination of two or three quarters of volume recovery, EBITDA/tonne near guidance, lower bank utilisation and visible monetisation of the new terminals. Until then, the low price-to-book is more a reflection of weak returns and uncertain asset productivity than an obvious bargain.

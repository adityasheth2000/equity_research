**Down 35% from 52W High**

## What Changed Since the July Verdict

The prior verdict (8 July 2026) flagged the **VAM raw-material spike as the "immediate" risk**. That risk has now materialised in Q1 FY27 results, which is why the stock has fallen from Rs 962 → Rs 870 since.

### Q1 FY27 (Jun 2026) — Standalone

| Metric | Q1 FY26 | Q2 FY26 | Q3 FY26 | Q4 FY26 | **Q1 FY27** | YoY |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Revenue (Cr)** | 75 | 74 | 72 | 93 | **88** | **+17%** |
| **Operating Profit (Cr)** | 21 | 20 | 19 | 25 | **13** | **-38%** |
| **OPM %** | 27% | 28% | 26% | 27% | **14%** | -13pp |
| **Net Profit (Cr)** | 17 | 17 | 15 | 20 | **12** | **-29%** |
| **EPS (Rs)** | 14.48 | 14.30 | 12.81 | 16.73 | **9.79** | -32% |

**The quarter went wrong on margin, not on demand.** Revenue grew 17% (10% volume + 7% price), but operating profit collapsed 38% because VAM (vinyl acetate monomer — ~90% of raw material, 100% imported) spiked from Rs 75–78/kg to Rs 170–180/kg in late March/April on the West Asia conflict. The company runs light on inventory (~30 days), so it absorbed the full spike before its May price hikes (4% April + 11% May) could flow through.

Management is explicit that **Q1 EBITDA of 14.4% is a one-off** (6–7% raw-material hit + 3–4% from a deliberate Rs 4.5 Cr, 54-dealer-meet marketing blitz). VAM has cooled to ~Rs 100/kg, and they guide **22–25% EBITDA from Q2 onwards**.

## Financial Analysis

### Revenue & Profitability

- **FY26 (ended Mar 2026):** Revenue Rs 314 Cr (+11%), PAT Rs 69 Cr (-7% vs FY25's Rs 74 Cr).
- **TTM (Sep'25–Jun'26):** Revenue Rs 327 Cr, PAT Rs 64 Cr → **TTM P/E ~16x**.
- **The VAM shock has reset the earnings base.** Even if Q2–Q4 recover to 22–25% margins, FY27 PAT is unlikely to grow vs FY26's Rs 69 Cr. Consensus case: FY27 PAT ~Rs 62–68 Cr → P/E ~15–17x.

### Balance Sheet & Working Capital

- **Zero debt**, ~Rs 160 Cr cash (Rs 133/share — ~15% of market cap).
- **Trade receivables Rs 145–150 Cr** (vs Rs 125 Cr in FY25, Rs 94 Cr in FY24). Debtor days now >150 (target 120 within 2–3 quarters). Management reiterates bad debt has been <0.5% for 20 years, and receivables are spread across 13,000 direct-to-retail accounts.
- **Employee cost +25% QoQ** (Rs 9 Cr → Rs 11 Cr) — new hires (sales force 520 → 562), appraisals, and the transformation push. Guided to stay at 11–13% of revenue.

### Cash Deployment (unchanged concern)

- Rs 160 Cr earning ~0% real returns. No buyback (internal discussion, "conclusion not reached"). No Big 4 auditor ("too early"; current R Kabra / Ecovis Germany remains). Both were investor asks in prior calls and remain unresolved.

## Strategy & Growth — FY27 Update

1. **Brownfield expansion (3,500 TPM):** ~80% complete, live "in 1–2 months" (Q2 FY27). Capex just Rs 7 Cr. Revenue potential Rs 600–650 Cr from the existing plant.
2. **Greenfield (FY27+):** Rs 45–50 Cr, Phase 1 = 1,500 TPM. Funded by internal accruals.
3. **Volume guidance:** 15–20% YoY target; Q1 delivered 10% (against a 11% price hike — which management frames as brand-strength validation).
4. **Geographic expansion:** 15 states → 100 cities, 65 territories. Entered **Jharkhand**; one more state in Q2. UP at 1,500+ counters. New states still 2–3 years from maturity (structurally higher debtor days + marketing spend).
5. **OEM segment:** 6% of revenue, targeting 10–12% (modular kitchens/furniture, Bangalore/Telangana/Delhi).
6. **Carpenter network:** 210,000 registered (+10,000 in Q1) vs 170,000 a year ago.
7. **FY29 target:** Rs 500 Cr top line (implies ~15% CAGR from Rs 327 Cr TTM) at 22–25% EBITDA.

## Risks

1. **Margin recovery is not yet proven.** Q2 must rebound to 22–25%; another miss would confirm structural pressure, not just a raw-material blip.
2. **VAM volatility persists** — crude-linked, geopolitically driven, ~90% of RM cost with no backward integration at current scale.
3. **Growth deceleration vs. spend escalation:** 3-yr revenue CAGR ~6%, yet ad + sales-promotion spend is being scaled to 6–7% of revenue. ROI remains unproven.
4. **Single product / single plant** (until greenfield): concentration risk.
5. **Working-capital creep** (receivables Rs 150 Cr) while chasing new-state growth.
6. **Governance perception:** no Big 4 auditor, no buyback, confusing carpenter-redemption accounting — a persistent overhang on multiple.
7. **Valuation:** ~16x TTM on *declining* earnings is not cheap if FY27 PAT stagnates; the "cheapness" only appears if 15–20% volume growth + 22–25% margins materialise in FY28–FY29.

---

## Conclusion

**Q1 FY27 was the stress test the prior verdict warned about — and the business passed on demand but failed on margin.** The 17% revenue growth with 10% volume growth *despite* an 11% price hike is genuinely encouraging: it suggests the Euro 7000 brand and carpenter flywheel have pricing power in core markets. But EBITDA at 14.4% (vs 22–25% guidance) shows the company's heavy single-input exposure to imported VAM is a real, recurring vulnerability, not just a theoretical one.

The stock at Rs 870 (P/E ~16x, ex-cash ~14x) is cheaper than the July level, but the cheapness is being earned the wrong way — via earnings compression, not via a re-rating opportunity. The next two quarters are decisive: if Q2/Q3 print 22–25% EBITDA and volume growth holds at 15%+, the VAM episode looks like a temporary shock and the FY29 Rs 500 Cr path re-validates. If margins stay soft and receivables keep climbing, the thesis stalls.

**Verdict: WAIT AND WATCH (unchanged).** The Q1 miss has moved the stock toward the "better entry" zone flagged in July (sub-Rs 800 = ~13–14x), but the margin-recovery evidence isn't in yet. Wait for (a) Q2 FY27 EBITDA ≥ 22% and (b) sustained 15%+ volume growth, ideally with (c) NSE listing + debtor-days normalisation as confirming catalysts, before committing capital.

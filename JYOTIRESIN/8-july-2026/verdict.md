## Company Overview

Jyoti Resins and Adhesives Ltd is a manufacturer of synthetic resin adhesives (white glue / wood adhesives) under the brand **"Euro 7000"**, launched in 2006. It is the **2nd largest wood adhesive brand in India** in the retail segment, with 15-35% market share in its top states. The company operates a single manufacturing plant at Santej, Ahmedabad (2,000 TPM / 24,000 TPA capacity) and serves 14 states through 54 branches, 65 distributors, 13,000+ retailers, and a network of 3.5L+ carpenters.

**Founded:** 1994 by Chairman Jagdish Patel (1st-gen entrepreneur)  
**MD:** Utkarsh Patel (2nd-gen, joined 2005, Chemical Engineer + Management background)  
**Promoter Holding:** 54.4% (increased from 50.83% in Jun 2025 to 50.89% in Sep 2025)  
**Dividend:** 15.4% payout (Rs 9/share, 90% of face value)

## Strategy & Growth

### Capacity Expansion
1. **Brownfield (80% complete):** 2,000 TPM → 3,500 TPM. Capex: Rs 5-7 Cr (very capital efficient). Live before Q2 FY27. Revenue potential: Rs 600-700 Cr.
2. **Greenfield (FY27+):** Phase 1: 1,500 TPM (Rs 45-50 Cr). Scalable to 5,000 TPM in phases. Location: Gujarat. Enables Rs 1,000 Cr+ revenue vision.
3. **Current utilization:** 60-70% average, spiking to near-full in peak months (March).

### Geographic Expansion
- **Mature states (5):** Gujarat, Rajasthan, Maharashtra, MP, Karnataka — 80-85% of revenue. Gujarat alone ~35% market share.
- **Building presence:** UP (650+ counters in 7 cities), Delhi-NCR, Punjab, West Bengal, Telangana, Andhra Pradesh
- **Newly added (May 2026):** Odisha, Chhattisgarh
- **Target:** All India presence within 3 years
- **Key risk:** New states take 2-3 years to break even; higher debtor days and marketing spends are structural during this phase.

### Brand & Marketing
- **Pankaj Tripathi** signed as brand ambassador (Q1 FY26), 3-year contract
- **360° campaign** across TV (AajTak, India TV, CNBC, Zee Business), digital, OOH, print
- **JioHotstar + ICC T20 World Cup** (Feb-Mar 2026) for national visibility during peak season
- **Ad spend trajectory:** 2% (FY25) → 4% (FY26 actual) → targeting 6-7% of revenue
- **Mix:** 70-80% trade marketing (dealer/carpenter meets), 20-25% brand communication
- **Strategy being questioned by investors** for not translating into immediate volume/revenue growth

### Product Strategy
- **Single product focus:** White glue only, no diversification planned until Rs 1,000 Cr milestone
- **OEM segment:** Growing — 6% of revenue currently, targeting 15% within 2-3 years
- **Product range:** 8+ SKUs covering all furniture gluing applications (Extreme 3, WP 2in1, Ultra 5in1, PVC Glue, etc.)

### Revenue Targets
- **Rs 500 Cr** in next 2-3 years (from Rs 315 Cr in FY26 = 17% CAGR needed)
- **Rs 1,000 Cr** long-term aspiration
- **Growth driver:** Exclusively volume-driven from core wood adhesives, no new product lines

## Moat & Competitive Position

### Identified Moats
1. **Carpenter Loyalty / Distribution Density:** 3.5L+ carpenters reached, 2L+ registered on loyalty program, 60% active. Carpenters are the primary decision-makers — dealers and architects follow their recommendation. This creates a **bottom-up influencer flywheel**.
2. **Brand Trust (Regional):** In mature states (especially Gujarat), Euro 7000 has built 20-35% market share over 15+ years of consistent quality. High switching costs due to trust + habit.
3. **Capital Efficiency:** Low-capex model — brownfield expansion of 1,500 TPM additional capacity costs only Rs 5-7 Cr. Fixed asset turnover already 6.3x, expected to hit 8-10x post-expansion.
4. **Asset-Light Distribution:** 54 company branches (stockist model), no distributor middlemen — direct supply to 13,000 retailers. This gives control over credit, pricing, and channel engagement.

### Competitive Reality
- **Pidilite (Fevicol):** Dominant market leader. Unmatched brand recall, distribution depth, and product portfolio (800+ SKUs). Pidilite's adhesive business alone dwarfs Jyoti Resins.
- **Astral:** Recently entered wood adhesives — a credible threat with distribution muscle and deep pockets.
- **Jivanjor:** Setting up capacity in Gujarat — direct threat in Jyoti Resins' home market.
- **Management's counter:** "Competition was there from day one" — and in Gujarat (35% share), they've proven they can compete. But this is easier in home territory vs. new states where the brand has no legacy.

### TAM
- Indian wood adhesive market: Rs 7,500-8,000 Cr
- Jyoti Resins is at ~4% national share but higher in core states
- Large market, underpenetrated — shift from unorganized to branded

## Management Quality

### Positives
- **Utkarsh Patel (MD)** — 20+ years in the business, chemical engineering background, appears focused and disciplined
- **Conservative capital allocation** — zero debt, no wasteful spending, dividend-paying
- **Professionalization underway** — hired Samit Shah as COO, hiring talent from multinationals, building CRM/SOPs
- **Founder still involved** — Jagdish Patel as Chairman provides stability
- **Skin in the game** — promoter holding increased to 54.4%, zero pledged
- **Transparent about challenges** — acknowledges growth misses, explains headwinds

### Concerns
- **Growth guidance credibility:** Promised 20% growth repeatedly (Q1 FY26, Q2 FY26, Q3 FY26 calls), delivered only 8% in FY26. Management acknowledged the miss in Q3 FY26 call and said they'd provide "better planning" going forward, then backed away from giving specific FY27 numbers citing "geopolitical issues."
- **Investor trust deficit:** Multiple investors across calls expressed frustration — questioning auditor quality, calling for buybacks, doubting the reported cash position. The stock's collapse (-39% from highs) reflects this.
- **Auditor quality:** Currently R Kabra & Associates (solo auditor affiliated with Ecovis Germany). Multiple investors have explicitly called for a Big 4/5 auditor to increase credibility. Management says they are "on it" but no timeline given.
- **NSE listing** has been "in process" for multiple quarters with no concrete progress.
- **Redemption accounting** remains confusing — investors repeatedly ask the same question about how carpenters' points impact revenue, requiring management to explain every quarter.

## Risks

1. **Revenue Growth Deceleration:** 3yr sales growth at 6.4% vs 5yr at 25.4%. The growth engine is sputtering. FY26 growth salvaged only by a seasonally strong Q4. FY27 faces tough comps.

2. **VAM Raw Material Risk (IMMEDIATE):** VAM (Vinyl Acetate Monomer) is 90% of raw material cost, 100% imported. Prices spiked 100%+ in Q1 FY27 (Rs 75-80/kg → Rs 160-180/kg) due to geopolitical disruption (West Asia conflict). Management took ~60-70% price pass-through from May 1, 2026. Gross margins will compress in Q1–Q2 FY27 until full pass-through. VAM is crude-linked — sustained high oil prices are a direct margin headwind.

3. **Single Product, Single Plant:** One product (white glue), one plant (Santej). Any disruption — fire, regulatory, supply chain — is catastrophic. No backward integration possible for VAM at current scale.

4. **New State Execution:** 14 states → targeting 20+ states. Each new state takes 2-3 years to mature, burns cash in marketing, and inflates debtors. The risk of spreading too thin is real.

5. **Marketing Spend ROI Unclear:** Significant jump in ad spend (2% → 7-8% of revenue) has NOT translated into proportional revenue growth. FY26 revenue grew 11% while ad spend nearly tripled. If the brand push doesn't convert, margins will remain under pressure.

6. **Competition Intensity Rising:** Astral entering, Jivanjor expanding in Gujarat, Pidilite's unmatched distribution network. As Jyoti Resins expands nationally, it will face the full force of entrenched competitors.

7. **Audit & Governance Perception:** Small auditor, hand-written corrections in transcripts (price rise clarification), confusing redemption accounting, and delayed NSE listing all fuel investor skepticism.

8. **Valuation Risk:** Despite the correction, 16.5x P/E on declining earnings (FY26 PAT down 5.3%) is not a bargain for a company with single-digit revenue growth, margin compression, and rising competitive threats.

While Jyoti Resins trades at a significant discount to Pidilite (and justifiably so given the scale/brand gap), the question is whether 16.5x P/E is cheap enough given:
- PAT declined YoY (FY26: Rs 70 Cr vs FY25: Rs 74 Cr)
- Revenue growth is in single digits on a 3-year basis
- Margins are compressing
- FY27 faces VAM headwinds that will compress gross margins
- Rs 166 Cr in cash/Bank FDs earning low returns

**Ex-cash P/E:** Market cap Rs 1,155 Cr minus cash Rs 166 Cr = Enterprise Value (equity) ~Rs 989 Cr. P/E on deployed capital ~14x. Still not deeply cheap for a business with these headwinds.

**Bull case:** If brownfield expansion ramps up, new states mature, and volumes grow at 15-20%, PAT could reach Rs 90-100 Cr in 2-3 years = 11-12x forward P/E at current price. That would be attractive.

**Bear case:** If VAM stays elevated, margins compress to 22-23%, competition intensifies in new states, and growth stays at 8-10% — PAT could stagnate at Rs 60-70 Cr = 16-17x P/E is rich.

## Conclusion

**Jyoti Resins is a high-quality business in a sticky product category with proven regional dominance, excellent capital efficiency, and a clean balance sheet — but it's in a transition phase where high growth hasn't materialized, margins are compressing, and investor trust is fragile.**

The core business thesis — that consistent quality + carpenter loyalty can replicate Gujarat's success pan-India — is plausible but unproven at scale. The brand push with Pankaj Tripathi is a departure from the company's historically conservative approach, and the payoff timeline is uncertain. FY27 will be a critical year: VAM headwinds will test margin resilience just as the company needs to demonstrate volume acceleration to justify the elevated marketing spend.

At 16.5x P/E on declining earnings, the stock isn't a screaming bargain, but it's also not expensive if one believes in the 15-20% volume growth trajectory. The stock's 39% correction from highs has largely priced in the growth disappointment and margin compression of FY26. The key question for FY27 is whether growth and margins can **inflect upwards**.

**Verdict: WAIT AND WATCH.** The business quality warrants a spot on the watchlist. Better entry points may emerge if VAM-related margin compression or growth disappointment causes further price weakness (sub Rs 800 / P/E ~13x). Catalysts to watch for re-rating: sustained 15%+ volume growth for 2+ quarters, debtor days normalization, auditor upgrade, and successful greenfield execution.

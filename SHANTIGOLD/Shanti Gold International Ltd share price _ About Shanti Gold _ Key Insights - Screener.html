          Concall Summary - May 2026

          Accounting change (material comparability point)

          • The company changed inventory valuation from FIFO to Weighted Average Cost (WAC) with retrospective effect from April 1, 2024. Management said WAC “better reflects the blended inventory cost” amid “significant volatility in gold prices,” improves “cost and revenue matching,” and aligns with “broader industry standard.” They stated the impact is “appropriately reflected in the reported numbers.”

          FY26 financial performance (reported) and key drivers

          Q4 FY26 vs Q4 FY25

          • Revenue: INR 658.93 cr vs INR 297.29 cr (+121.65% YoY) driven by new customers, higher volumes, and elevated gold prices.
          • EBITDA: INR 67.01 cr vs INR 21.12 cr (+217.26% YoY); margin 10.17% vs 7.10% (+306 bps) aided by “better product mix and higher contribution from bridal jewellery.”
          • PAT: INR 51.93 cr vs INR 9.19 cr; margin 7.88% vs 3.09%.

          FY26 vs FY25

          • Revenue: INR 2,018.71 cr vs INR 1,106.41 cr (+82.46% YoY) on “stable ordering trends” and deeper engagement with organized retail customers.
          • EBITDA: INR 199.00 cr vs INR 89.92 cr (+121.31% YoY); margin 9.86% vs 8.13% (+173 bps).
          • PAT: INR 140.15 cr vs INR 54.10 cr; margin 6.94% vs 4.89% (+205 bps).

          Important margin clarification (core vs FY26 uplift)

          • Management repeatedly characterized sustainable PAT margin at ~3.5–4% (called “business margin / core margin”).
          • They attributed the incremental upside in FY26 (PAT margin ~7%) to inventory gains from buying gold outright using IPO proceeds, stating:
            • “whatever extra money… was the borrowing from IPO proceeding. We had purchased gold… So that is why the company has made a higher profit.”
            • CFO: FY26 had “an element of a surge in the gold price… which we are not considering as of now.”
          • Guidance framing: “whatever turnover that we do it in the next year, simply roughly we will get a 4% PAT… inventory gains… will be extra bonus.”

          Demand, volume/value growth outlook, and gold-price sensitivity (as stated)

          • Management indicated the FY26 revenue growth was materially supported by gold prices; on a forward basis they guided:
            • Volume growth: “30% to 40%”
            • Value growth: “60%–70%” (elsewhere “around 60%”)
          • There was inconsistency in how they justified the value growth:
            • In one exchange, MD said value growth was “calculating with current price.”
            • Later, MD acknowledged expecting “some high incremental” in gold and referenced “geopolitical” instability; they mentioned “current price, we are expecting INR 1,50,000” (context: gold price level assumption).
          • Demand resilience view:
            • Despite “elevated gold prices,” demand remained “resilient,” especially “wedding and occasions-led jewellery.”
            • Structural tailwind: organized retailers increasingly outsource manufacturing to focus on “footprint, inventory optimizing, and faster design cycles.”

          Capacity expansion program (scale-up and timelines)

          Current

          • Existing Andheri facility: ~2,700 kg p.a. installed capacity; utilization stated at ~65%.

          Under execution

          1. Marol (Mumbai) new facility
            • Adds ~4,000 kg p.a.
            • “almost ready… in a month or so.”
            • Ramp plan: initially “100 kgs per month” before scaling to the full run-rate (management wording: “reaching very soon we’ll be reaching till 4,000 kilos”).
          2. Jaipur facility (IPO-announced)
            • Adds ~1,200 kg p.a.
            • Expected commissioning: “around September, October.”

          Post expansion

          • Total installed capacity expected: ~7,900 kg p.a.
          • Strategic intent: investments “not speculative,” backed by “customer traction” and “demand visibility.” Management expects scale to improve “operating leverage” and profitability.

          Revenue potential at peak (management math)

          • At full 7,900 kg, MD estimated revenue “around INR 11,850 crores” at a referenced price assumption (“around 150”).
          • Capacity utilization trajectory: management said 3–4 years to reach full utilization (and possibly more capacity beyond that), while expecting FY27 revenue INR 3,000–3,500 cr.

          Product mix strategy and new category launches (growth vectors)

          • Portfolio actions in FY26:
            • Entered machine-made plain gold jewellery (high volume, lower margin).
            • Launched Mangalsutra category; “encouraging customer response.”
            • Developed Turkish jewellery for Marol facility; hired “skilled professionals directly from Turkey” to ensure authenticity and craftsmanship; “initial response… encouraging.”
            • Mentioned additional lines such as “Cuban bracelets,” “plain casting”.
          • Mix disclosure (broad):
            • “70%” from the existing line and “30%” from the newer plain line (as asked in context of studded/plain/22-carat).
            • Margin mix: “bridal jewellery has a higher margin and plain gold has a lesser margin,” blended to “4% plus” core.

          Customer strategy, concentration, and market expansion

          • Customer base: “More than 400” customers.
          • Top customer concentration referenced by an analyst (~7%); management did not quantify share-of-wallet but emphasized:
            • Growth from both “new customers as well as existing.”
            • Increased participation/penetration in North India (Delhi): “North expansion is going very huge.”
          • Not supplying to Titan/Tanishq currently: “No, we don’t supply jewellery to Titan.” Management said with new factories they plan to “approach Titan and lot of new clients… “very soon we are going to be on board” (aspirational; no timeline committed).

          Exports and Dubai subsidiary (strategic intent + delay)

          • Dubai subsidiary incorporation/operations delayed to June 2026 due to “wars” / “geopolitical things are not good.”
          • Status: “All the license and all we’ve got it. Only a couple of license are left”; they have “taken the place” and expect to be “fully operational” once commenced.
          • Export target: increase export contribution from “10% to 20%” (“…or maybe more”).
          • Target markets routed via Dubai: “USA, Singapore, Malaysia, U.K.” (not only Middle East).

          Working capital, leverage, inventory posture, and hedging

          • ROCE: CFO stated ~38%, “very healthy.”
          • Leverage: debt-equity ~0.36; management comfort ceiling “at 1:1” to fund expansion/working capital.
          • Working capital metrics (indicative):
            • Inventory “less than 50” days and receivables “less than 50” days (CFO characterization).
            • Receivable days increased 42 to 48/49; CFO attributed to rapid topline growth and credit support needs in “CZ segment,” calling the change modest in a volatile market.
          • Inventory/positioning amid volatility:
            • They avoided Gold Metal Loan (GML) due to volatility; instead bought gold outright: “we didn’t wanted to take a risk of gold metal loan. So we purchased all the gold on outright.”
            • CFO quantified current “stock and debtors taken together close to 600 kgs.”
          • Hedging policy: “We buy as we sell. Simple.” (implying minimal open price exposure in the normal course, notwithstanding the one-off IPO-funded stocking mentioned for FY26 benefit).

          Regulatory/tax commentary affecting demand (management view)

          • Import duty increase back to 15%: management expects no demand issue, noting it was 15% historically and was reduced to 6% only last year; “I don’t think so there would be any problem in demand.”
          • A query about “Modiji’s statement”: management response—impact expected on “gold bars and gold coins, not on the jewellery.”

          Key actionable investor takeaways (from management statements)

          • Normalize margins: management explicitly guides ~4% PAT as sustainable core, with FY26’s higher margin linked to a non-recurring inventory timing benefit from outright gold purchase using IPO proceeds.
          • Capacity-led growth is imminent: Marol commissioning expected within ~1 month, Jaipur by Sep/Oct; total capacity to ~7,900 kg p.a. supports management’s FY27 revenue aspiration (INR 3,000–3,500 cr) and multi-year scaling to peak utilization.
          • Mix shift risk/opportunity: plain/machine-made jewellery is being added (lower margin, higher volume) while bridal remains margin supportive; the company is explicitly balancing the two to maintain “4% plus” blended economics.
          • Export optionality but execution risk: Dubai setup is strategically important and “ready,” but delayed by geopolitics; export share goal 10% → 20% with broader geography focus.
          • Working capital and leverage: management signals willingness to increase leverage up to 1.0x D/E to support scale-up; current metrics portrayed as controlled despite higher gold prices and receivable day creep.

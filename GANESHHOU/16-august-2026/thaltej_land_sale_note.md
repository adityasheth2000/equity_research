# One 91 Thaltej — Land Sale Investigation Note

**Date:** 16 August 2026 | **Ticker:** GANESHHOU

---

## 1. What Happened in Q1 FY27

After **5 consecutive quarters** of promising an imminent launch of a flagship ~2 mn sq ft, 30+ floor commercial tower with ₹2,100 Cr lifetime revenue, Ganesh Housing **sold the Thaltej land outright** to an undisclosed listed entity.

The sale contributed ~₹250 Cr to Q1 revenue, but the bottom line tells a starkly different story:

| Line | Amount (Rs Cr) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Thaltej land sale (est.) | ~250 | Confirmed by B. Ravi |
| Total Q1 expenses | 170 | Mostly the land's amalgamation cost basis |
| Op Profit | 110 | OPM 39% (vs 80%+ normal) |
| PBT | 105 | After interest + depreciation |
| Tax | 63 | **Effective rate 60%** (vs 25–27% normal) |
| **PAT (entire quarter)** | **42** | |

### Why PAT was so low

1. **High accounting cost basis.** The land entered Ganesh via a 2020 tax-neutral amalgamation at an appraised (high) book value, not Ganesh's typical near-zero historical land cost. The Q1 ₹170 Cr expense line is essentially that high cost.
2. **Punitive one-time tax.** For tax purposes, the cost is the amalgamated entity's original low purchase price. Selling now crystallised the entire deferred gain → 60% effective tax rate.
3. **The buyer is a listed entity**, name withheld pending buyer's disclosure. Management says "He has not yet told us" when asked about disclosure timing.

### Management's justification: NPV

> "The entire ₹2,100 crores... would have been realized over five years... 3 years construction + 2 years sale... We did a calculation saying that when we do a present value of the entire flows... versus what we have been getting immediately, was making far more economical sense." (B. Ravi, Q1 FY27 call)

### The NPV math problem

Management's own Jun 2026 call gave: **₹2,100 Cr sale value − ₹1,500 Cr cost ≈ ₹600 Cr development profit**. Selling as land yielded ~₹80–100 Cr pre-tax, ~₹30–40 Cr net. Even heavily discounted, ₹600 Cr over 5 years dwarfs ₹30–40 Cr now — **unless** development economics were much worse than they'd been telling investors.

### Contradiction on cash need

Prepared remarks: "not a response to liquidity requirements." Q&A (same call): "this is the opportunity we are seeing right now, **for which cash is needed**."

### Key unresolved questions

- **Who is the buyer?** Related party / promoter-linked = serious governance issue. Genuinely independent third party = more defensible.
- **Was full payment received?** B. Ravi: "normally most of the payments come in" — hedged.
- **Why the whiplash after 2 years of planning?** Jun 2026: "yes, definitely" to Q1 clarity on Thaltej. Six weeks later: land sold.

### Specific promises that never materialised

1. **The "premium, brand-building flagship."** Painted as iconic. Jul 2026: brand argument waved off — "projects are anyway being done anywhere else."
2. **It was a sale project, not a lease one** (Feb 2026: "not going to be a lease one"). Plan was to develop and sell — not monetize raw land.
3. **The funding story inverted.** Jun 2026: "enough money from internal accruals to develop that project." Jul 2026: we needed cash on hand to buy land.
4. **"Clarity in Q1 FY27"** became the perpetual promise. When Q1 arrived, the "clarity" was that the project was dead.

## 3. Tone Analysis (Is Management Defensive?)

From the Q1 FY27 transcript, the tone is **consistently defensive**, evidenced by:

1. **Repetition of a rehearsed frame.** The "NPV / five-year risk" and "capital allocation, not liquidity" framing appears verbatim in prepared remarks AND in three separate Q&A answers.

2. **Pre-emptive denial.** "Approvals and all were not the issue. Project by itself was not the issue" — nobody accused them of that. Volunteered denial is a tell.

3. **Deflection.** When asked "why sell a prime asset for ₹42 Cr PAT?", B. Ravi pivots to a technical lecture on the 2020 amalgamation tax basis — accurate, but answers *why the margin is low*, not *why the deal was good for shareholders*.

4. **Hedging on concrete numbers.** "It's completed. It's already completed" sits awkwardly against "normally most of the payments come in" (not all received). Sale value and rate withheld "until the buyer allows us."

5. **"He has not yet told us"** (about the buyer) — an oddly informal phrase signalling the buyer controls the disclosure, and management is comfortable deferring.

6. **Internal contradiction on cash.** Prepared remarks insist "not a response to liquidity requirements"; Q&A reveals "this is the opportunity we are seeing right now, for which cash is needed."

7. **The one candid voice is not the spin doctor.** Neeraj Kalawatia's terse admission — "It is largely land sale and old projects" — contrasts starkly with B. Ravi's multi-paragraph, rehearsed answers. The CFO/VP is blunt; the corporate advisor is managing perception.

8. **The NPV justification is garbled in the prepared remarks.** "In NPV terms, we have realized by developing it" — the transcription is mangled right at the pivotal justification sentence.

## 4. Governance Concerns (Not Proof, but a Pattern Worth Tracking)

1. **Buyer is a listed entity, name withheld.** If related-party or promoter-linked when disclosed → serious escalation. If independent third party paying fair price → narrows to credibility debate.

2. **₹1,600 Cr RPT approval ceiling** sought at the Sept 11, 2026 AGM — unusually large for a ~₹7,000 Cr market cap company.

3. **Gatil Properties amalgamation** (promoter-owned ~71% subsidiary) approved Dec 2025 with a **₹498.56 Cr reserve write-off** — a promoter entity folded in at a value that wiped out reserves.

4. **The 2020 amalgamation** is the seed of the Thaltej problem: promoters injected land at appraised (high) book value, tax-deferred, into the listed entity. Public shareholders now pay the deferred tax on sale. That is a subtle wealth transfer from public shareholders to the promoters (who got equity in exchange for the land at the high appraised value in 2020, without triggering tax).

5. **The narrative whiplash.** 1.5–2 years of approvals, 5 quarters of "imminent launch," then an abrupt reversal — with the Jun 2026 "yes, definitely" to Q1 clarity just six weeks before the sale was announced. Either a buyer materialised very suddenly, or the "imminent launch" messaging was never quite real.

6. **Promoter holding 73.1%,** minimal institutional ownership (FII <1%, DII <1%). Chairman and MD/CEO since 1991/1994.

---

## 5. Summary

The economics are *mechanically* explicable (amalgamation cost + deferred tax explains the thin PAT), but the gap between what was promised (a ₹2,100 Cr flagship development generating ~₹600 Cr profit) and what was delivered (a ₹250 Cr land sale yielding negligible PAT) is not adequately explained by the NPV framing given management's own numbers.

The combination — an undisclosed listed buyer, a ₹1,600 Cr RPT ceiling, a reserve-destroying promoter amalgamation, an earlier 2020 amalgamation that injected the land at inflated book value while deferring tax to public shareholders, and a pattern of deferred/pivoted promises — warrants scrutiny, not dismissal. The single most important data point that would resolve the core concern is **the buyer's identity** when disclosed.

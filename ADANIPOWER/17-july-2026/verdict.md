# Adani Power Ltd (ADANIPOWER) — Analysis Verdict

**Date:** 17 July 2026 | **CMP:** Rs 216.57 | **Mkt Cap:** Rs 4,17,591 Cr | **P/E:** 32.69

---

## What the Company Does

Adani Power is India's largest private thermal power producer. They build, own, and operate coal-based power plants across 8 states, selling electricity to state distribution companies (DISCOMs) under long-term Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) and also selling on the open market (merchant/bilateral). 

Think of them as a massive factory that burns coal to make electricity. They sign 25-year contracts with state electricity boards who guarantee to pay them for making their plants "available" (capacity charge) plus the fuel cost (energy charge). The company operates 18,150 MW across 14 plants — that's enough to power ~15 million Indian homes. They're now building another 23,720 MW (target 41,870 MW by FY32), making it one of the largest thermal capacity expansion plans globally. Key plants: Mundra (4,620 MW in Gujarat — India's largest single-location thermal plant), Tiroda (3,300 MW), Godda (1,600 MW, exclusively supplying Bangladesh), and several acquired stressed assets that they've turned around.

## Key Metrics to Track

### 1. Plant Load Factor (PLF %)
**Why matters:** This is the capacity utilization of their power plants. Higher PLF = more units sold = more energy charge revenue. However, under the new PPA model, capacity charges are paid regardless of dispatch, so PLF's importance is gradually diminishing vs plant availability. FY26 PLF: 67% (down from 71% in FY25 due to extended monsoons). Long-term average: 48-71%.

### 2. EBITDA per MW / EBITDA Margin
**Why matters:** The single best measure of operational efficiency and pricing power. Adani Power's 38-40% EBITDA margin is the highest in India's thermal sector. EBITDA per MW captures both the quality of PPAs (tariff rates) and cost efficiency (fuel sourcing, logistics). FY26 EBITDA: Rs 23,431 Cr (~Rs 1.29 Cr/MW). Management targets Rs 50,000 Cr EBITDA by FY30-31.

### 3. PPA Tie-Up Ratio (% of capacity contracted)
**Why matters:** This determines revenue visibility and cash flow stability. PPAs are 25-year contracts with two-part tariffs (capacity + energy charge with fuel pass-through). Higher tie-up = lower merchant exposure = more predictable earnings. Currently 95% of 18,150 MW under LT/MT PPAs. Target: reduce merchant exposure to 3-4%. For upcoming 23,720 MW, 13,320 MW (56%) already tied up. **Watch closely:** The gap between PPA wins and capacity commissioning.

### 4. Net Debt / EBITDA (Leverage)
**Why matters:** Thermal power is capital-intensive. High leverage nearly bankrupted the company in FY19 (9.75x Net Debt/EBITDA). Dramatic improvement to 2.12x in FY26. But capex of Rs 2 lakh Cr over 7 years will push debt up. Management claims internal accruals cover 70-90% of capex. Track this ratio — anything above 3-4x would be a concern given the aggressive expansion.

### 5. Capacity Addition Progress (MW commissioned per year)
**Why matters:** The entire investment thesis rests on executing 23,720 MW of new capacity on time and on budget. Timeline: FY27: 2.9 GW → FY28: 2.4 GW → FY29: 2.4 GW → FY30: 8 GW → FY31: 5.6 GW → FY32: 2.4 GW. The ramp from 2-3 GW/year to 8 GW in FY30 is aggressive. Any slippage delays EBITDA growth. Korba Phase-II (1,320 MW) is the first test — expected Q2 FY27.

### 6. Realisation Rates (Rs/kWh) — PPA and Merchant
**Why matters:** PPA tariffs drive ~80% of revenue. New PPAs are being signed at capacity charges of Rs 4.11-4.30/kWh (FY26), up from Rs 2.89/kWh in FY21 — this is positive for per-MW profitability. Merchant realizations (Rs 5.30/kWh in FY26) provide upside but are volatile. Track the spread between contracted and merchant rates.

### 7. Free Cash Flow / FFO
**Why matters:** All capex is claimed to be funded from internal accruals. FY26 FFO: Rs 20,248 Cr. FY27 capex guidance: Rs 25,000 Cr. The gap needs bridge financing. If FFO fails to keep pace with capex, either debt rises or expansion slows. 7-year projected FFO: Rs 1,40,000 Cr vs capex of Rs 2,02,000 Cr — gap of Rs 60,000 Cr to be debt-funded.

### 8. Fuel Security (FSA coverage + captive mines)
**Why matters:** Coal is 55-60% of operating cost. 87% of domestic coal capacity has long-term FSAs. The Dhirauli captive mine (6.5 MTPA) is a game-changer — first captive mine, near Mahan plant, significant cost advantage. Commercial mining licenses for 4 mines (14 MTPA total) add further fuel security. Only IPP with in-house mine-to-plant logistics.

## Management Track Record

### Hits (Walked the Talk)

**1. Stressed Asset Turnaround — Exceptional execution**
Management has acquired and revived 7,450 MW of stressed thermal assets (Mahan, Raipur, Raigarh, Korba, Udupi, Dahanu, Butibori/VIPL). Butibori was shut for 10 years — revived within 2 months of acquisition. Mahan generated cumulative EBITDA of ~Rs 6,300 Cr since acquisition. Raipur: cumulative EBITDA of ~Rs 9,690 Cr. Entire acquisition debt prepaid from cash flows. This is arguably their strongest demonstrated capability.

**2. Deleveraging — Historic improvement**
Net Debt/EBITDA from 9.75x (FY19) to 1.44x (FY25). This was not just operational improvement — the Supreme Court ruling (April 2023) allowing recovery of alternate fuel costs unlocked massive one-time regulatory receivables. But management also used the windfall to prepay debt rather than expand aggressively at the time. Credit rating upgraded 8 notches in 6 years (now AA/Stable across all 4 agencies).

**3. Godda — Cross-border project execution**
1,600 MW plant commissioned in 3.5 years from financial closure despite pandemic lockdowns in India, China, and Bangladesh. Dedicated 400kV transmission line to Bangladesh grid. 25-year USD-denominated PPA with Bangladesh Power Development Board. Supplies ~10% of Bangladesh's capacity. Operations continued uninterrupted even during Bangladesh's political turmoil (Aug 2025).

**4. PPA Market Share — Dominance in SHAKTI bids**
Won 13,945 MW out of 20,920 MW of coal-linked PPAs awarded under SHAKTI policy (~67% market share). States awarding PPAs: Assam (3,200 MW), Bihar (2,400 MW), Maharashtra (3,200 MW incl. 1,600 MW recent), UP (1,600 MW), MP (2,920 MW), Karnataka (625 MW).

**5. Capital Cost Advantage — Pre-ordering strategy**
Advance BTG orders placed for entire 23.7 GW pipeline (Rs 2 lakh Cr) before formal PPA signing — a bold, contrarian bet that gives them 2-3 year lead time over competitors on equipment delivery. L&T + BHEL as vendors. Management claims per-MW cost is significantly lower than peers due to scale and brownfield advantages (60% of pipeline).

### Misses / Concerns

**1. Capacity Target Revisions — Moving goalposts**
Guidance trajectory: Q1 FY25: 30 GW by FY30 → Q3 FY25: "30+ GW by 2030" → Q4 FY25: 30.67 GW by FY30 → Q2 FY26: 23.5 GW addition → Q3 FY26: 23.7 GW by FY32 → FY26 AR: 41.87 GW by FY32. The target has grown 40% (30 → 42 GW) and the timeline has shifted. While this reflects growing market opportunity, it also means the initial guidance was conservative. Investors should question whether the 42 GW target will also be revised.

**2. PLF Underperformance in FY26**
FY25 PLF: 71%. FY26 PLF: 67%. Management attributed this to extended monsoons and cooler temperatures. But 9M FY26 PLF was 63% vs 69% in 9M FY25 — a material 6pp decline. While Q4 FY26 saw recovery, the full-year number missed the implied trajectory. Revenue from operations fell 3.5% YoY to Rs 54,241 Cr.

**3. Capex Guidance Creep**
FY25: Guided Rs 8,000 Cr capex for FY25, Rs 13,000 Cr for FY26. FY26 actual: ~Rs 22,000-23,000 Cr (per Q4 FY26 call). FY27 guidance: Rs 25,000 Cr. FY28 guidance: Rs 33,000 Cr. The capex numbers are consistently revised upward as the scope of expansion grows. While this reflects ambition, it also means debt is rising faster than initially indicated — net debt went from Rs 31,023 Cr (FY25) to Rs 45,022 Cr (FY26).

**4. Bangladesh Counterparty Risk — Underappreciated**
Godda plant (1,600 MW) has a 25-year PPA with Bangladesh PDB — a single-counterparty concentration risk in a geopolitically volatile neighbour. Receivables at 1.5-2 months are flagged as "regular" but any payment delay or political disruption could materially impact EBITDA (Godda contributed ~Rs 3,247 Cr EBITDA in 9M FY26). Grid connectivity to India provides a partial hedge (allows sale into Indian grid if Bangladesh defaults).

**5. Free Cash Flow Negative in FY26**
Despite Rs 23,431 Cr EBITDA, Free Cash Flow was Rs -2,817 Cr in FY26 (due to Rs 26,461 Cr investing outflows). FY25 FCF was positive Rs 9,957 Cr. The swing to negative FCF signals the beginning of the heavy capex cycle. Management claims this is transitory and FCF from operating assets covers 70%+ of capex, but investors should monitor FCF trajectory closely.

### Verdict on Management
**Score: 8/10.** The stressed asset turnaround track record is exceptional and hard to replicate. The strategic foresight in pre-ordering BTG equipment and securing land for 23.7 GW before PPAs were signed demonstrates conviction. However, the constantly shifting capacity targets and capex numbers warrant caution — execution at this scale has no precedent in India's private thermal sector. The true test begins now as 2.9 GW commissions over the next 12 months.

## Strategy & Growth Outlook

**The Core Thesis:** India needs 80-97 GW of additional thermal capacity by FY32-35 to meet baseload demand as renewable penetration increases. APL is positioned to capture ~30% of this opportunity with 23.7 GW in the pipeline — all land secured, all BTG equipment ordered, 73% environmental clearances obtained.

**Competitive Advantages:**
1. **Scale + Brownfield:** 60% of expansion at existing sites — lower per-MW cost, faster execution
2. **Fuel logistics moat:** In-house mine-to-plant capability handling 74 MTPA coal, only IPP with this
3. **First-mover in SHAKTI bids:** 67% market share of awarded PPAs, 13.3 GW tied up
4. **Balance sheet ready:** AA rating, access to domestic capital markets, Rs 7,500 Cr NCD raised
5. **New PPA economics:** Capacity charges at Rs 4.11-4.30/kWh (vs Rs 2.89 in FY21) — structurally higher EBITDA/MW

**Growth Levers:**
- 2.9 GW commissioning in FY27 (Korba + partial Mahan)
- PPA pipeline of ~13,180 MW bids ongoing
- Captive coal mine (Dhirauli) operational from CY2026
- International expansion: Bhutan hydro (570 MW), evaluating nuclear
- Potential: 42 GW by FY32 → Rs 50,000 Cr+ EBITDA → debt-free by FY32-33

## Bottom Line

Adani Power is a high-conviction structural growth story riding India's thermal power capex cycle, with genuine competitive advantages in fuel logistics, project execution, and SHAKTI bid dominance. The stressed asset turnaround track record is exceptional and the pre-ordering strategy gives a 2-3 year lead over competitors. However, at 32.7x trailing earnings with negative free cash flow entering a Rs 2 lakh Cr capex cycle, the market is already pricing in flawless execution of the 23.7 GW pipeline. The first test comes in FY27 with 2.9 GW commissioning — delivery here will determine whether the premium multiple is justified or whether the stock has run ahead of fundamentals.

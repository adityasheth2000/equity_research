## Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed the ratings on the bank facilities and debt instruments of Manappuram Finance Limited at ‘Crisil AA/Stable/Crisil A1+’. The rating reflects the company's established market position in the gold finance business while maintaining a strong financial risk profile. These strengths are partially offset by high, though improving, operating costs, geographical concentration in operations, and potential challenges in non-gold product segments.

**Key Highlights:**
*   **AUM Growth:** Assets under management (AUM) on a consolidated basis grew by ~48.3% (YTD) to Rs.63,798 crore as on March 31, 2026, from Rs.43,034 crore as on March 31, 2025.
*   **Portfolio Composition:** Loan against gold jewellery accounts for ~80% of the portfolio, growing at ~99% year-on-year.
*   **Asset Quality:** Gross NPAs on a standalone basis reduced to 1.8% as on March 31, 2026, from 2.8% the previous year.
*   **Capital Infusion:** Bain Capital received regulatory approval for a 41.66% stake. The first tranche of Rs.2,740 crore has been received, with further tranches scheduled for conversion within 4-18 months, aimed at strengthening capital and reducing gearing.
*   **Subsidiary Performance:** The NCDs amounting to Rs.20.47 crore have been withdrawn as these instruments were redeemed.

---

## Key Rating Drivers — Strengths

*   **Established Market Position in Gold Finance:**
    *   Promoter family has 6+ decades of expertise in gold loans.
    *   Strong brand and reputation in South India (Kerala and Tamil Nadu).
    *   Gold loan AUM grew ~99% y-o-y to Rs.50,953 crore as of March 2026.
*   **Sound Capitalisation:**
    *   Consolidated Networth: Rs.16,058 crore.
    *   On-book Gearing: 3.6 times (Consolidated) as on March 31, 2026.
    *   Capital Adequacy Ratio: 21% (Consolidated).
    *   Capital infusion from Bain Capital (~Rs.4,385 crore total planned) will further strengthen the balance sheet.
*   **Stable Funding Profile:**
    *   Diversified funding sources: Banks (62%), ECBs (25%), NCDs (6%), CP (7%).
    *   Incremental cost of borrowing improved to 8.6% in fiscal 2026 from 9.2% in fiscal 2025.
    *   Ability to roll over lines and raise fresh funds from diverse sources.
*   **Profitability:**
    *   Historically, RoMA has been over 4.0% for six fiscals since 2019 driven by the gold loan segment.
    *   Home finance segment profitability increased to Rs.28.3 crore in fiscal 2026.

---

## Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses

*   **Geographical Concentration:**
    *   South India accounted for 63% of total branch level presence as on March 31, 2026.
    *   Revenue concentration risk due to dependence on gold loan financing (78% of revenue).
*   **Non-Gold Segment Challenges:**
    *   Non-gold segments account for 20% of the portfolio and 22% of revenue.
    *   **Microfinance:** Unsecured loans, weak credit risk profiles, susceptibility to local sociopolitical issues.
    *   **Vehicle Finance:** Lending against used/pre-owned commercial vehicles, leading to higher provision costs and elevated NPAs (10.4%).
    *   **Housing Finance:** Caters to unorganized segments with volatile income.
*   **Asset Quality in Non-Gold Segments:**
    *   NPAs within microfinance stood at 4.8% (March 2026).
    *   NPAs within vehicle finance elevated to 10.4% (March 2026).

## Liquidity

*   **Status:** Strong
*   **Liquid Balance:** Rs.3,142 crore as on May 31, 2026.
*   **Components:** Cash and liquid investments (Rs.2,442 crore) + Unutilised CC/WCDL limit (Rs.700 crore).
*   **Debt Cover:** Liquidity cover over 2.1 times against debt obligations due over the next three months.
*   **Rollover:** Company has been able to roll over existing capital lines and raise incremental funds at competitive rates.

---

## Outlook & Rating Sensitivity

**Outlook:** Stable

**Upward Factors**
*   Continued strong market position in gold finance alongside increasing diversity into non-gold business without affecting asset quality.
*   Sustenance of profitability with RoMA above 4% on a steady-state basis.
*   Improvement in asset quality, particularly within non-gold loan segments.

**Downward Factors**
*   Consolidated gearing increasing over 5 times.
*   Steep decline in interest collection in the gold loan business.
*   Deterioration in asset quality or profitability in non-gold loan segments.

## 1. What the Company Does (Business Model in Plain Language)

**Manappuram Finance Ltd (MAFIL)** is a Kerala-based Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC) founded in 1949 by the Nandakumar family. It is India's **#2 gold loan financier** (after Muthoot Finance). In simple terms, here is how it makes money:

### Core Product: Gold Loans
- A customer walks into a Manappuram branch (or uses the Online Gold Loan / OGL app) with household gold jewellery.
- Manappuram's staff appraise the gold, custody it in a secure vault, and lend money against it — typically **57% of the gold's value** (LTV = Loan-to-Value).
- The customer pays interest (currently ~17.3% yield) on the loan for a short tenor (avg 4–5 months, max 1 year) using a bullet repayment structure (principal + interest at the end).
- If the customer doesn't repay, Manappuram can auction the gold to recover the loan.
- Because the loan is fully secured by gold, credit losses are minimal — the business is essentially "spread" income: (Yield on gold loans) − (Cost of borrowing).

### How Manappuram Scales This
- **4,044 gold branches** across India (concentrated in South India: 63% of branches).
- **~63 tonnes of gold** in custody (Q4 FY26) on behalf of **25.2 lakh active gold customers** (standalone).
- **Online Gold Loan (OGL)** — launched Sept 2015, India's first NBFC OGL. Now **92% of the gold book** is sourced online via a multilingual app — a genuine digital moat. Customers can borrow anytime from anywhere against gold already stored in a branch.

### Diversification Attempts (Subsidiaries)
Manappuram has tried to diversify beyond gold loans into multiple underbanked segments, with mixed results:
1. **Asirvad Microfinance (98% owned, subsidiary)** — India's 3rd largest MFI. Unsecured micro-loans to women borrowers (avg ticket Rs 41k). **Severely stressed in FY25–FY26** due to RBI ban (Oct 2024 – Jan 2025) and sector-wide over-leverage. AUM shrank from peak ₹12,310 Cr (Q1 FY25) to ₹6,794 Cr (Q4 FY26). Returned to profit only in Q4 FY26 (₹13 Cr) after 3 quarters of losses.
2. **Manappuram Home Finance (MAHOME HFC, 100% owned)** — Affordable housing loans for self-employed (96% self-employed), avg ticket ₹6L, focus on Tier-3/4 towns. AUM ₹1,852 Cr (Q4 FY26) — small but stable.
3. **Vehicle & Equipment Finance (VEF, standalone)** — Used commercial vehicles, two-wheelers, passenger vehicles. AUM ₹2,991 Cr (Q4 FY26, down 37% YoY) — **stressed; GNPA jumped to 10.4%**.
4. **MSME & Personal Loans (standalone)** — Micro-housing, secured/unsecured PL. AUM ₹3,351 Cr. **Digital Personal Loan disbursals fully stopped** due to stress; GNPA at 7.1%.

### Revenue Model
- Net Interest Income (NII) = Interest income − Finance expenses
- Q4 FY26 Consolidated: NII ₹1,504 Cr (Yield ₹2,614 Cr − Finance Expenses ₹1,110 Cr)
- Minus: Operating expenses (₹737 Cr) → Pre-provision profit ₹779 Cr
- Minus: Provisions (₹216 Cr) → PBT ₹564 Cr → PAT ₹405 Cr (post-tax)

### Customer Base & Reach
- 2.7 million active gold customers (consolidated)
- 5,000+ branches, 42,000+ employees
- Customer base skews to underbanked, semi-urban/rural borrowers; 63% of gold branches in South India

### Capital Providers
- Promoters: 31.77% (Mar 2026) — V.P. Nandakumar family
- Bain Capital (BC Asia Investments XXV): ~9.89% via preferential allotment (March 2026); warrants for additional ~9% conversion in 4–18 months → potential 18%+ stake
- FIIs: 23%, DIIs/MFs: 13%, Public: 32%

## 2. Company Overview & Industry Positioning

### Industry Position
- **#2 gold loan NBFC in India** behind Muthoot Finance
- Gold loan market in India: ~₹15.6 lakh crore (Nov 2025, RBI data), up 42% YoY
- Banks have grown market share to 55% (Dec 2025) from 50% (Mar 2025) — increasing competition
- Only ~8% of household gold is pledged with organised financiers (vs 7% in FY24) — long runway for growth

### Competitive Differentiation
1. **OGL first-mover advantage** — 92% of gold AUM sourced online (vs Muthoot's lower digital share)
2. **Branch network depth** — 4,044 gold branches (Muthoot ~5,000+ but Manappuram is #2)
3. **Cellular vaulting mechanism** — process innovation for gold security
4. **Brand trust in South India** — 63% of branches in Kerala/Tamil Nadu/Karnataka/AP/Telangana
5. **Bain Capital backing** — institutional validation and capital infusion of ~₹4,385 Cr

### Peer Valuation Gap
| Company | P/E | P/B | ROE | AUM/branch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Muthoot Finance | 11.3x | ~3.5x | ~20% | ₹28 Cr |
| Manappuram Fin. | 30.5x | ~1.9x | 7% (FY26) | ~₹14 Cr |
| Discount | – | –45% | – | –50% |

Muthoot commands 45% P/B premium because of: (a) higher ROE (20% vs 7%), (b) better branch productivity (₹28 Cr vs ₹14 Cr AUM/branch), (c) 90% gold focus vs Manappuram's troubled diversification, (d) NIM expansion (Q2 FY26 NIM 11.2% vs Manappuram 9.8%).

## 3. Key Metrics to Track

### 1. Gold Loan AUM Growth
- **Why it matters:** Gold is 80% of consolidated AUM and ~100% of profits (Asirvad/HFC/VEF/MSME collectively drag returns). Gold AUM growth = revenue growth.
- **Current:** ₹50,953 Cr Q4 FY26 (+99.1% YoY, +31.5% QoQ). Management guided FY27 growth "better than FY26."
- **Decomposition:** ~20% gold price + ~7% tonnage + mix shift = 31.5% QoQ AUM growth in Q4 FY26.

### 2. Gold Loan Yield
- **Why it matters:** Direct driver of NIM and profitability. Has been the biggest headwind in FY26.
- **Current:** 17.3% in Q4 FY26 (vs 22.3% FY25, 24.9% FY21). Secular decline due to (a) competition, (b) conscious decision to drop rates to attract higher-ticket customers, (c) bank entry at lower rates.
- **Guidance:** "Will not go down" — expected to stabilize at **17.5–18%** (Nandakumar, Q4 FY26 call).

### 3. Cost of Borrowing
- **Why it matters:** Offsets yield compression. Has been a tailwind in FY26.
- **Current:** 8.80% Q4 FY26 (down from 9.40% Q4 FY25 — 60 bps improvement YoY)
- **Guidance:** "Don't expect much increase" — down 17 bps QoQ in Q4 FY26.

### 4. Loan-to-Value (LTV) Ratio
- **Why it matters:** Risk gauge on collateral coverage. Higher LTV = more risk if gold prices fall.
- **Current:** 57% (stable, conservative) — vs RBI cap of 75% (proposed 85% for small-ticket under new norms).
- **Trend:** 71% (FY21) → 62% (FY22) → 57% (FY26) — tightening risk posture.

### 5. Online Gold Loan (OGL) Share
- **Why it matters:** Digital moat, cost-efficiency, customer acquisition tool.
- **Current:** 92% of gold AUM in Q4 FY26 (vs 54% FY21, 82% FY25).
- **ATS:** ₹1.36 L (OGL) vs ₹1.28 L (overall gold) in Q4 FY26 — higher-ticket digital acquisition.

### 6. Asirvad MFI AUM & Asset Quality
- **Why it matters:** Drag on consolidated PAT. ₹579 Cr loss in FY26 (vs ₹639 Cr loss in FY25) — only Q4 FY26 turned green (₹13 Cr).
- **Current:** AUM ₹6,794 Cr (incl. Gold ₹2,139 Cr); GNPA 4.85%, NNPA 1.6%; collection efficiency 95% (new book 99.4%+).
- **Monitor:** Quarterly PAT, GNPA trajectory, new book % (59% now, target 75%), disbursal growth (₹1,086 Cr Q4 FY26 vs ₹680 Cr Q3 FY26).

### 7. Capital Adequacy (CRAR) & Leverage
- **Why it matters:** Growth headroom and balance sheet resilience.
- **Current:** Consolidated CRAR 21.32% (Mar 26), down from 30.91% (Mar 25) — driven by AUM growth outpacing capital. Standalone CRAR 21%. Asirvad CRAR 20.2%.
- **Gearing:** 3.60x consolidated (Mar 26), up from 2.96x (Mar 25) — watch regulator threshold of 5x.

### 8. Return Ratios (ROE/ROA)
- **Why it matters:** Capital efficiency. Manappuram structurally trails Muthoot here.
- **Current:** Consolidated ROE 7.0% FY26 (vs 10.0% FY25, 20.7% FY24); standalone ROE 12.2% (FY26).
- **Guidance:** Consolidated ROE to stabilize at **13–16% in 1–2 years** (Nandakumar); standalone ROA target **4.25–4.5%** by H2 FY27 (Deepak Reddy, Q3 FY26 call). For FY28: "targeting over 15% for ROE."

## 9. Risks

### A. Business Risks

**1. Gold Price Risk** — A sharp correction in gold prices could (a) reduce AUM growth, (b) push LTV above 57% requiring margin calls, (c) trigger higher auctions. Mitigant: OGL app allows digital LTV management; auctions were minimal in FY26 (₹15–29 Cr).

**2. Competition Intensity** — Banks now hold 55% of gold loan market share (Dec 2025, up from 50% in Mar 2025). New NBFC entrants adding ~935 branches in FY26 YTD. Pressure on yields (already down from 22.3% to 17.3% in 12 months). Mitigant: OGL digital moat, branch network depth, new product launches.

**3. Single-Product Concentration** — Gold = 80% of consolidated AUM. Strategic pivot back to gold (from 59% a year ago) increases dependence on a single product. The "one-stop shop" thesis has effectively been abandoned.

**4. Geographic Concentration** — 63% of gold branches in South India (TN 578, KA 570, KL 480, AP 333, TS 253). Regional slowdowns or regulatory actions (Karnataka/Tamil Nadu MFI ordinances) can impact operations.

### B. Subsidiary / Asset Quality Risks

**5. Asirvad MFI Stress** — AUM down 35.7% YoY (MFI) to ₹4,636 Cr; FY26 loss ₹579 Cr; GNPA 4.85%. Only Q4 FY26 turned green (₹13 Cr), but with one-off ₹96 Cr post-tax MTM + ECL write-back. Adjusted loss was flat QoQ. Recovery is fragile — collection efficiency 95% (overall), 99.4%+ on new book (which is 59% of book). Old book (41%) still has residual stress.

**6. VEF Asset Quality Deterioration** — GNPA worsened from 4.2% (Q2 FY25) → 10.4% (Q4 FY26). One-time write-off of ₹136 Cr in Q4 FY26. AUM contracted -37.3% YoY. Match-bounce collection recoveries improved from 75% to 90%, but the segment is being deliberately shrunk.

**7. MSME Asset Quality** — GNPA 7.1% (Q4 FY26), up from 5.1% (Q4 FY25). Digital PL disbursals fully stopped. Further write-offs possible in FY27.

**8. HFC Stagnation** — AUM flat at ₹1,852 Cr (+1.5% YoY, -2.6% QoQ). Strategy shift to larger-ticket affordable housing yet to play out. New team being assembled.

### C. Financial Risks

**9. Yield Compression** — Gold loan yield compressed from 22.3% (FY25) to 17.3% (Q4 FY26) — ~500 bps erosion in 4 quarters. Management believes this is the bottom ("will not go down"). If competition intensifies or new RBI norms cap LTV-inclusive-of-interest, yields could fall further.

**10. Leverage Rising** — Consolidated gearing up from 2.96x (Mar 25) → 3.60x (Mar 26). CARE/CRISIL downgrade trigger: gearing sustained above 5x. Still comfortable but watchable.

**11. Capital Adequacy Decline** — CRAR down from 30.91% (Mar 25) → 21.32% (Mar 26) — supporting AUM growth but reducing buffer. Bain warrants (~₹1,644 Cr) will help when converted.

**12. ALM Mismatch at Asirvad** — Long-term (>5Y) net deficit of ₹1,908 Cr — duration mismatch risk; needs refinancing.

### D. Regulatory Risks

**13. RBI Gold Lending Norms (Effective April 1, 2026)** — New guidelines require (a) credit assessment for high-value loans, (b) defined LTV/tenor/customer eligibility per product, (c) interest component within LTV cap. Manappuram has launched two new products (consumption + income-generating loans) to comply. Opportunity (no LTV cap for income-generating MSME loans to quality borrowers) but also execution risk.

**14. MFI Regulatory Cycle** — RBI's Oct 2024 ban on Asirvad was lifted Jan 8, 2025, but sector-wide scrutiny continues. State-level ordinances (Karnataka, Tamil Nadu) on MFI collections have caused "initial hiccups" per management.

### E. Governance / Disclosure Risks

**15. Disclosure Inconsistencies** — Q4 FY26 call had multiple discrepancies (auction number, Q3 impairment restatement, Q3 interest income regrouping). Minor but worrying trend.

**16. CEO Vacuum** — Deepak Reddy on medical leave indefinitely; no replacement being considered. Nandakumar (75+ years) continues as MD/Chairman. Key leadership gap at the top.

### F. Valuation Risks

**17. Earnings Quality** — Q4 FY26 PAT of ₹405 Cr included one-off benefits (₹96 Cr post-tax MTM + ECL write-back in Asirvad, ₹84 Cr net vehicle write-off). Adjusted PAT would have been ₹430 Cr. Future PAT sustainability depends on Asirvad continuing without one-off gains.

**18. Re-rating Dependence on ROE** — Stock at P/B 1.9x has re-rated from ~1.25x in 2022, but further re-rating depends on ROE expansion to 13–16%. If ROE stays at 7–10%, multiple could compress.

## 12. Final Verdict

### Investment Thesis (Bull Case)
1. **Gold loan franchise is genuinely strong** — AUM nearly doubled in FY26 (+98% YoY), OGL at 92% is a digital moat, LTV conservative at 57%, cost of funds improving (9.4% → 8.6%)
2. **Asirvad appears to have turned the corner** — Q4 FY26 PAT +₹13 Cr; new book collection efficiency 99.4%+; old book to be ~90% run-off by Q3 FY27
3. **Bain Capital deal closed** — ₹2,740 Cr received; institutional governance and capital backing; warrants worth ₹1,644 Cr pending
4. **Regulatory tailwinds** — RBI removed branch approval requirement; new gold lending norms enable income-generating loans with no LTV cap for quality borrowers
5. **Reasonable forward valuation** — P/E ~16x FY27E, ~12x FY28E; P/B 1.9x vs Muthoot 3.5x (45% discount could narrow)
6. **Operating leverage playing out** — OPEX/AUM down from 6.6% (FY22) → 5.1% (FY26) standalone; AUM/branch up from ₹5.4 Cr (FY21) → ₹14 Cr (Q4 FY26)

### Investment Risks (Bear Case)
1. **ROE is structurally low** — 7% FY26 vs Muthoot's 20%; even guided 13–16% by FY28 is below Muthoot's current level
2. **Yield compression is structural** — from 22.3% (FY25) to 17.3% (Q4 FY26); management says "bottomed" but competition from banks is intensifying
3. **Diversification has been value-destructive** — Asirvad's 4-quarter losses exceed 38-quarter accumulated profit; VEF GNPA 10.4%; MSME GNPA 7.1%; HFC stagnant
4. **CEO vacuum** — Deepak Reddy on indefinite medical leave; Nandakumar 75+ years old
5. **Disclosure inconsistencies** on Q4 FY26 call — minor governance yellow flag
6. **Guidance track record** — ROE 18% (May 2025) walked back to 13–16%; Bain close "before CY2025" actually happened March 2026; VEF turnaround "1–2 quarters" still not delivered
7. **Single-product concentration** — Gold at 80% of AUM; the "one-stop shop" thesis abandoned in practice

### Conclusion
Manappuram is a **turnaround story with strong gold fundamentals but persistent non-gold drag**. The FY26 numbers (PAT down -17.5% YoY despite AUM +48.3%) reflect the tension between strong gold loan growth and weak non-gold execution. The Q4 FY26 print (PAT +₹405 Cr, ROE 12.3% annualized) is the cleanest quarter in 4 quarters and may mark the inflection — but Q4 included ₹96 Cr post-tax one-off benefits in Asirvad.

**Key swing factors for the stock:**
1. Asirvad's ability to deliver sustainable PAT without one-off gains (Q1–Q2 FY27 will be the test)
2. Gold loan yield holding at 17.5–18%
3. ROE progression toward 13–16%
4. Bain Capital's strategic direction post-warrant conversion
5. Deepak Reddy's return and execution on strategic roadmap

**Position sizing consideration:** For an investor comfortable with a turnaround story, Manappuram offers:
- **Asymmetric upside** if Asirvad recovers + ROE expands to 13–16% (P/B could re-rate from 1.9x toward 2.5x)
- **Real downside risk** if gold yield breaks below 17% or Asirvad slips back into losses (P/B could de-rate to 1.3–1.5x)

**Preferred alternative for conservative investors:** Muthoot Finance — same industry, 3x ROE, 2x branch productivity, 90% gold focus, 3.5x P/B (premium but quality rarely comes cheap). Muthoot has delivered consistent execution while Manappuram has been a 5-year story of "one step forward, two steps back" on diversification.

**For Manappuram itself, the investment thesis is best summarized as:** "A gold loan specialist with structural growth tailwinds (gold prices, OGL, RBI norms) but with execution risk in non-gold segments and leadership uncertainty. Buy only if you believe Asirvad has bottomed and ROE will expand to mid-teens by FY28."

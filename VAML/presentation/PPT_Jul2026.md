**Topic: Aluminium Profitability Analysis ($/t) – 1QFY26 vs. 1QFY27**

This slide presents a waterfall-style analysis of aluminium profitability per tonne for Vedanta Aluminium, comparing Q1 FY2026 (base period) to Q1 FY2027 (current period). It breaks down revenue components and cost drivers to show the evolution in EBITDA margin.

---

### **Revenue Components ($/t)**

| Component             | 1QFY26     | 1QFY27     |
|-----------------------|------------|------------|
| LME                   | $2,448     | $3,571     |
| Strategic hedging     | $26        | $352       |
| Premium               | $268       | $416       |
| **Realisation**       | **$2,742** | **$3,635** |

→ Realisation price increased by **$893/t YoY**, driven primarily by higher LME prices (+$1,123/t), partially offset by lower premium contribution growth relative to LME rise.

---

### **Cost Structure ($/t)**

#### Hot Metal Cost Breakdown (1QFY27):
- Alumina: $781
- Power: $465
- Other hot metal: $452  
→ **Total Hot Metal Cost = $1,698/t**

#### Additional Costs:
- Conversion & other: $133/t

→ **Total Cost per tonne (Hot Metal + Conversion) = $1,831/t**

*(Note: In 1QFY26, total costs were $1,765/t — composed of Alumina $877, Power $491, Other hot metal $396, Conversion & other $91)*

---

### **EBITDA Performance ($/t)**

| Period   | EBITDA ($/t) | YoY Change       |
|----------|--------------|------------------|
| 1QFY26   | $887         | —                |
| 1QFY27   | $1,804       | **+103% YoY**    |

→ Despite rising input costs (hot metal cost up ~$100/t), EBITDA nearly doubled due to significantly stronger realisation prices.

---

### **Key Insights for Equity Analysts:**

- **Margin Expansion**: EBITDA margin improved dramatically from $887/t to $1,804/t — an absolute increase of $917/t or 103% YoY.
- **Price Sensitivity**: The company benefits materially from LME price movements; strategic hedging also contributed meaningfully in 1QFY27 ($352/t vs. $26/t prior year).
- **Cost Discipline**: While alumina and power costs rose slightly on a per-tonne basis, conversion costs increased more sharply ($91 → $133), suggesting potential operational inefficiencies or inflationary pressures elsewhere.
- **Profitability Driver**: The primary driver of profitability improvement is top-line realisation strength, not cost reduction — indicating exposure to commodity cycles but strong pricing power when markets are favorable.

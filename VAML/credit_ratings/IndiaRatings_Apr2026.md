# India Ratings Assigns Vedanta Aluminium’s NCDs ‘IND AA-’; Places on Rating Watch with Developing Implications

-

- Apr 30, 2026 | Vedanta Aluminium Metal Limited

- |

- Aluminium

India Ratings and Research (Ind-Ra) has taken the following rating actions on Vedanta Aluminium Metal Limited’s (VAML) non-convertible debentures (NCD):

## Details of Instruments

Instrument Type Date of Issuance Coupon Rate Maturity Date Size of Issue (billion) Rating Assigned with Outlook/Watch Rating Action

Non-convertible debentures*---INR40.89IND AA-/Rating Watch with Developing ImplicationsAssigned; Placed on Rating Watch

*Details in annexure

## Analytical Approach

Ind-Ra has fully consolidated VAML and its group companies, which includes Vedanta Limited (VDL; NCD rated at IND AA-/Rating Watch with Developing Implications), Talwandi Sabo Power Limited, Malco Energy Limited and Vedanta Iron and Steel Limited, to arrive at the ratings on account of strategic, operational and financial linkages among them. The agency has also consolidated the debt of the parent company, Vedanta Resources Limited (VRL), to arrive at the ratings. Ind-Ra will re-assess the approach once the agency receives more clarity on the operational, strategic and cross financial linkages post the completion of the demerger. The entities have jointly been referred to as the Vedanta group.

## Detailed Rationale of the Rating Action

The rating reflects VAML’s strong business profile, backed by its strong market position, its low-cost operations in the aluminium segment, its high product and geographic diversification along with vertical integration, and the rising share of value-added products, as reflected in the improvement in EBITDA per tonne FY25 onwards. VAML completed the capex for setting up its refinery and expansion in smelter in FY26; it is likely to ramp up production in FY27 and maintain high capacity utilisation. VAML’s EBITDA improved over 9MFY26 due to favourable commodity prices as well as a reduction in the cost of production, leading to an improvement in credit metrics.

The rating also reflects the Vedanta group’s improved financial flexibility on account of a reduction in debt refinancing risk at VRL, following the refinancing of a significant portion of its high-cost debt and the elongation of its average maturity. The adjusted consolidated leverage (including VRL's debt) of the group improved over FY25-9MFY26 because of an improvement in the EBIDTA, mainly led by higher commodity prices and a decline in costs. The EBITDA is likely to improve further over FY26-FY27, supported by likely incremental volume growth from the ongoing capex, a likely reduction in costs, and stable commodity prices, with progress towards deleveraging to around 2x. The ratings are, however, constrained by the group’s EBITDA remaining exposed to volatility in commodity prices, regulatory risk, likely high capex and dividend payouts, resulting in sustained consolidated gross debt (including VRL) in the near term.

Ind-Ra has placed the rating on the Rating Watch with Developing Implications in view of insufficient clarity on the debt allocation between the entities and the linkages between entities formed on account of the demerger of VDL’s existing business into five separate listed standalone entities. Furthermore, Ind-Ra is awaiting the details of the final terms and conditions of the revised sanction terms from the lenders and investors. Ind-Ra understands that the demerger will be effective on 1 May 2026. All five demerged entities are likely to be listed. Based on the current understanding, Ind-Ra does not expect the liquidity and credit profile of individual entities, including VAML, to deteriorate post the demerger, and hence, the rating is likely to be maintained or improved from the existing levels. However, a higher-than-expected allocation of debt at VAML or cross linkages between entities, resulting in extraordinary support to any of the other entities, leading to a higher-than-expected leverage, could result in a negative rating action. Ind-Ra will continue to monitor developments on the breakup of assets and liabilities and the linkages between entities, and will evaluate the details of the demerger, which is critical for the resolution of the rating watch.

## Detailed Description of Key Rating Drivers

Strong Business Profile of Aluminium Business: Post the organisational restructuring, VAML (including under Bharat Aluminium Company Limited (BALCO)) will be a vertically integrated facility, with presence across the value chain. The presence of bauxite and coal mines supports raw material availability, while the alumina refinery and aluminium smelter provide value addition. The presence of the captive power plant, bauxite and coal mine helps VAML reduce cost of production. In FY26, VAML completed the capacity expansion of its alumina refinery to 5 million tonnes per annum (MTPA) from 3.5MTPA, and it has also expanded the smelter capacity at BALCO to 3MTPA from 2.8MTPA; the added capacities are likely to ramp up in FY27. Furthermore, the likely commencement of bauxite and coal mines over FY27-FY28 will increase the level of backward integration, which is likely to reduce the cost of production in the near term.

Improved Capital Structure, Lower Refinancing Risk at VRL and Group: The rating reflects a material reduction in refinancing risk at VRL, with stable gross debt (December 2025: USD5.2 billion; March 2025: USD5.1 billion), lower annual maturities of USD302 million in FY27 and USD583 billion in FY28, and annual interest savings owing to a reduction in interest rates. This was supported by bond refinancing by VRL over FY25-9MFY26, with a reduced rate of interest and increased tenor of 3.5-to-seven years. The interest cost and annual maturities at VRL will be serviced through the annual management and brand fee received from VDL along with dividend payouts from VDL, which are likely to reduce from FY26. VRL’s proportionate net leverage will be higher than the consolidated net leverage due to its fragmented holding in VDL and Hindustan Zinc. However, Ind-Ra expects deleveraging of the debt over FY26-FY27, which will remain a key monitorable. As per Ind-Ra’s understanding, VRL’s debtholders do not have any legal recourse to the group’s cash flows.

Improved Profitability in Aluminium Segment: Ind-Ra expects the aluminium segment’s per tonne EBITDA to have remained robust in FY26 (9MFY26: USD1,034/t; FY25: USD870/t, FY24: USD494/t, FY23: USD322/t), backed by elevated commodity prices and higher volumes from capacity addition. The cost of production is likely to improve in the near term owing to the backward integration initiatives, including the capacity expansion of the Lanjigarh alumina refinery to 5MTPA, and the increase in smelter capacity to 3MTPA from 2.4MTPA. The planned increase in the volume share of value-added products to 2.6MTPA from 1.4MTPA, along with the phased ramp up of smelter capacity BALCO over the next two-to-three quarters, will further support operating efficiencies. The timely completion of the ongoing capex and ramp-up of new capacities will be a key monitorable.

The aluminium segment will benefit from better margins and cost competitiveness, resulting from improved raw material (coal) security due to the commissioning of captive coal mines in Radhikapur and Kuraloi blocks, Ghorgharpalli blocks, and its dip extension coal block in Sundargarh, Odisha, totalling to 34MTPA, and the bauxite mine in 1HFY27. According to the management, VAML’s cost of alumina and bauxite is likely to reduce due to higher domestic procurement. The agency expects the high operating leverage in the aluminium segment to ensure higher profitability of USD1,000-1,100/t over the medium term. However, any significant decline in commodity prices could impact the EBITDA per tonne.

Comfortable Credit Metrics: VAML’s credit metrics are likely to remain comfortable in the near term, supported by a likely improvement in EBITDA, on the back of an increase in sales volumes and EBITDA per tonne. The net leverage (net debt/operating EBITDA) stood at 1.45x in FY26 and is likely to remain at similar levels over FY27-FY28; however, the agency’s expectations regarding the net leverage could shift once it receives clarity on the debt allocation between the entities and the linkages between entities, the net leverage could vary. The aluminium segment (including BALCO) reported an EBITDA of INR255 billion in FY26 (FY25: INR178 billion) and EBITDA margin of 39% (30%).

The group’s net adjusted leverage (including VRL’s debt and brand licencing fees) is likely to improve to below 2.0x in FY26 (FY25: 2.51x; FY24: 3.2x). Ind-Ra expects the net leverage to range between 2.0x-2.5x over FY27-FY28, supported by healthy EBITDA, on the back of elevated commodity prices. The net adjusted leverage improved over FY25-9MFY26 primarily because the EBIDTA (including brand fee) increased over this period (9MFY26: INR319 billion; FY25: INR454 billion; FY24: INR375 billion), while the net debt at the group level (including VRL) remained largely stable (9MFY26: around USD11.75 billion; FY25: USD11.11 billion; FYE24: USD12.35 billion). The group has undertaken several transactions such as the qualified institutional placement and offer for sale transactions in FY26, including the recent sale of 1.59% stake in Hindustan Zinc Limited in January 2026 at a floor price of INR685 per share, raising about INR45.9 billion. These proceeds partly contributed to the deleveraging in FY26. Furthermore, the group continues to retain the flexibility to pursue these transactions in case of debt reduction. The management has a clear capital allocation framework that focusses only on high-return capex. However, as per the agency, the total adjusted debt (consolidated) is unlikely to reduce significantly over FY27 and FY28 on account of refinancing, dividend payout, and planned capex of USD1.6 billion-1.7 billion.

Ind-Ra expects the group to achieve consolidated EBITDA (excluding brand fees) of INR550 billion-600 billion over FY26-FY27 owing to an increase in volumes from the added capacities, increased backward integration through increased coal linkages, and cost efficiencies in the aluminium and zinc businesses. The consolidated profitability is likely to be supported by healthy domestic demand over the medium term, along with higher commodity spreads. In a scenario of declining commodity prices, the sustaining of EBITDA levels to support ongoing capex and debt reduction will remain a key monitorable.

Exposure to Commodity Price And Forex Risk: VAML’s and the group’s profitability remains exposed to volatility in commodity prices, as evident from the fact that the EBITDA improved in FY25 and 9MFY26 primarily due to an increase in London Metal Exchange (LME) and favourable forex movements. Any significant adverse changes in these factors are likely to impact the EBITDA.

Proposed Investments for Unrelated Diversification Over Medium Term: The proposed capex in the semi-conductor and display production business will now be executed through VDL (the post-merger entity, which will not include its current subsidiaries), after calling off the joint venture between the Vedanta group and Foxconn. However, the management has stated that the said project is in the nascent stages, and the company does not expect any immediate capital outflow towards the same. The progress of the semiconductor business will depend on VDL (the post-merger entity) identifying a new technology partner and acquiring various regulatory approvals, which will remain a key monitorable. The management also does not foresee any significant near-term outflow towards Konkola Copper Mines Plc business, as the planned capex of USD1 billion is likely to be spread over a period of five years, with VRL infusing USD200 million in FY26 by raising debt. However, further developments on these fronts will remain a key monitorable. Any extraordinary cash support to associate companies of the parent, leading to an increase in leverage or liquidity stress, will be a key monitorable.

## Liquidity

Adequate: The capex plans at Lanjigarh and BALCO have already been concluded, and the company does not have any plans to undertake major growth capex and debt raising towards the same. Furthermore, VAML would have a debt service coverage ratio of above 1.75x over FY27-FY28 with sufficient EBITDA generation to meet the debt servicing obligations of the company. VAML has repayment debt obligation of INR98 billion and INR79 billion in FY27 and FY28, respectively. Being a part of the Vedanta group, VAML would continue to maintain strong relationships with domestic banks. Furthermore, the Vedanta group would continue to support VAML’s liquidity and financial flexibility, if need arise.

VRL’s consolidated debt stood at USD15.1 billion in 9MFY26 (FY25: USD13.8 billion; FY24: USD14.3 billion). The net debt of the group reduced to USD11.7 billion in 9MFY26 (FY25: USD11.1 billion; FY24: USD12.3 billion). VRL’s upcoming maturities will remain important, given the sizeable debt maturities at both entities, alongside continued interest outflows, dividend payouts and ongoing capex requirements. VRL has debt obligations of INR28.4 billion and INR54.8 billion during FY27 and FY28, respectively. Despite high cash generation in FY25 and 9MFY26, VRL’s debt repayments have been minimal because of high capex and dividend payout.

The group has an established track record of refinancing, with access to domestic and international capital markets (20%-40% of borrowing). The group (including VRL) has repayment obligation of INR266.4 billion and INR229.4 billion in FY27 and FY28, respectively. The group’s (including VRL) unencumbered cash and cash equivalents stood at INR230 billion in 9MFY26 (FY25: INR222 billion).

## Rating Sensitivities

The Rating Watch with Developing Implications indicates that the ratings could be upgraded, downgraded or affirmed. Ind-Ra will resolve the Rating Watch after the agency receives clarity on debt allocation between the entities and assesses the cross-financial linkages between the entities formed through the demerger. Ind Ra understands that the company could take another two-to-three quarters to implement the demerger process.

## ESG Issues

ESG Factors Relevant to Rating: The group structure under governance has relevance and moderate impact on VAML’s ratings due to the group’s complexity and significant related-party transactions emanating from inter-company loans, resulting in a higher debt and high dividend leakages. In the past, the group has made efforts towards improving its group structure through increased stake in VDL by VRL. Furthermore, the recent demerger could help simplify the group structure.

Water and wastewater management, and greenhouse gas emission and air quality under environment have relevance and moderate impact on VAML’s ratings. The closure of the copper smelter plant in Tuticorin, Tamil Nadu, due to the directive from the Tamil Nadu Pollution Control Board could result in a delay in volume ramp-up at VDL’s copper unit. Moreover, the company is exposed to regulatory risks in its iron ore operations.

Unless otherwise disclosed in this section, the ESG issues are credit neutral or have only a minimal credit impact on VAML, due to either their nature or the way in which they are being managed by the entity. ## About the Company

VAML is a public company incorporated on 6 October 2023. The company is engaged in the exploring, extracting and processing of aluminium minerals. Its aluminium operations include a refinery and captive power plant at Lanjigarh, smelter and captive power plants at Jharsuguda and coal mines at Jamkhani, all situated in the state of Odisha in eastern India. BALCO's aluminium operations comprise two bauxite mines, two coal mines, power plants, smelting and fabrication facilities in the state of Chhattisgarh in central India.

VDL is a diversified natural resources player, with a significant presence in zinc, oil and gas, copper, aluminium, iron ore, and power. VDL currently generates 90% of its consolidated EBITDA from aluminium, zinc and oil and gas business; this would see a change post the completion of the demerger.

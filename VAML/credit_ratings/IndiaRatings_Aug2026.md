# India Ratings Upgrades Vedanta Aluminium Metal’s NCDs to ‘IND AA+’/Stable; Off Rating Watch with Developing Implications

-

- Aug 13, 2026 | Vedanta Aluminium Metal Limited

- |

- Aluminium

India Ratings and Research (Ind-Ra) has upgraded Vedanta Aluminium Metal Limited’s (VAML) non-convertible debentures (NCDs) to ‘IND AA+’ from ‘IND AA-’ with a Stable Outlook while resolving the Rating Watch with Developing Implications as follows:

## Analytical Approach

Ind-Ra continues to fully consolidate VAML and its group companies - Vedanta Limited (VDL), Vedanta Power Limited, Vedanta Oil and Gas Limited, and Vedanta Iron and Steel Limited, to arrive at the rating, due to common promoters and likely cash fungibility among the entities. The agency has also consolidated the debt of the parent company, Vedanta Resources Limited (VRL), because VRL relies on cash upstreamed from these operating companies for its debt servicing, investments, and provides support to them. The entities have been collectively referred to as the Vedanta Group.

## Detailed Rationale of the Rating Action

The resolution of the Rating Watch with Developing Implications results from the completion of VDL’s demerger into five entities, providing clarity on debt allocation and allowing assessment of linkages among the newly formed standalone entities. Ind-Ra believes the demerger has enhanced VDL’s corporate structure by providing clarity on investments and debt at the individual company level, rather than consolidating them within a single entity. This change prevents strong cash-generating companies extending support to weaker entities. Furthermore, the new structure improves the group’s financial flexibility with the option to monetise stake at individual asset, if required.

The upgrade reflects VAML’s strong business profile backed by its strong market position, low cost of production (COP) in the aluminium industry, and high product and geographic diversification. This is complimented by vertical integration and an increased share of value-added products, as reflected in a higher EBITDA/tonne (t) in FY26, likely sustaining over FY27-FY28. VAML completed its capex for setting up a refinery and expanding its smelter in FY26 to ramp-up production and maintain high-capacity utilisation in FY27. Furthermore, VAML is likely to complete its capex towards a mine development in the next few quarters, subject to clearances, to increase backward integration and reduce COP. VAML’s EBITDA has improved over FY26-1QFY27 due to favourable commodity prices and a lower COP, leading to improved standalone and consolidated credit metrics.

The rating also factors in the group’s enhanced financial flexibility led by a lower debt refinancing risk at VRL, following significant debt refinancing and average maturity extension. The group’s adjusted consolidated leverage (net debt/operating EBITDA; including VRL's debt) improved over FY25-FY26 to below 2.0x (FY24: around 3.5x), due to the improved EBITDA, mainly led by higher commodity prices and cost improvements, primarily in VAML and VDL (the holding company of Hindustan Zinc Limited). Furthermore, VRL’s proportionately consolidated net leverage improved to 2.8x in FY26 (FY25: 3.6x) and is likely to improve further to around 2.0x over FY27-FY28. The gap between VRL’s fully consolidated and proportionate leverage has narrowed, reflecting low cash leakage for debt servicing. EBITDA is likely to improve over FY27-FY28, subject to volume growth from the ongoing capex, cost reductions, and stable commodity prices. Progress towards keeping leverage below 2.0x, despite a high capex plan, will remain a key monitorable.

The rating is, however, constrained by the group’s EBITDA remaining exposed to volatility in commodity prices, regulatory risk, likely high capex and dividend payouts, which may increase the group’s leverage ratio.

## Detailed Description of Key Rating Drivers

Strong Business Profile: Post the organisational restructuring, VAML (including Bharat Aluminium Company Limited (BALCO)) is a vertically-integrated aluminium manufacturing facility with presence across the value chain. The presence of bauxite and coal mines supports raw material availability, while the alumina refinery and aluminium smelter provide value addition. The presence of a captive power plant, bauxite and coal mine reduces COP. In FY26, VAML expanded capacity of its alumina refinery to 5MTPA from 3.5MTPA, and increase overall smelting capacity to 2.8MTPA from 2.4MTPA through the BALCO expansion. These additional capacities are likely to ramp up further in FY27. Debottlenecking initiatives are likely to further increase the smelting capacity to 3MTPA. Furthermore, the likely commencement of bauxite and coal mines over FY27-FY28 will increase the level of backward integration, which is likely to further reduce COP and sustain EBITDA/t in the near term.

Clarity on Linkages Post Demerger: The resolution of the Rating Watch with Developing Implications follows clarity on debt allocation between the entities and assurance of no direct operational linkages or cash fungibility between entities after VDL’s demerger into five listed standalone entities. Ind-Ra understands that all five demerged entities will operate independently; however, given promoter commonality, there could be cash fungibility through promoter entities, potentially resulting in higher cash upstreaming from stronger entities to support others, which remains a key monitorable. The Vedanta group’s (including debt at VRL) consolidated credit profile has improved; however, any higher-than-expected debt at VAML or cash fungibility between entities resulting in an extraordinary support to any of the other entity, leading to a higher-than-expected leverage, could result in a negative rating action. Ind-Ra will continue to monitor linkages among the entities and the combined net leverage at VDL group level.

Improved Capital Structure and Lower Refinancing Risk at VRL: The rating reflects a material reduction in refinancing risk at VRL, with lower net debt (June 2026: USD4.7 billion (including other entities above Vedanta India like Konkola Copper Mines Plc); March 2025: USD5.3 billion; March 2022: USD9.6 billion) as well as lower annual maturities of USD100 million in FY27 and USD628 billion in FY28, along with annual interest savings owing to a reduction in interest rate. The bond refinancing completed over FY25-1QFY27 reduced borrowing costs and extended debt maturities; further refinancing of the remaining debt is underway. VRL’s interest obligations and debt maturities are likely to be serviced through the management and brand fees received from the VDL group, supported by dividend payouts from the same. Dividend upstreaming is likely to moderate in the medium term, considering the elongated repayment schedule. The agency also expects VRL's debt obligations to continue to be refinanced in a timely manner, which remains a key rating sensitivity and monitorable.

As per Ind-Ra’s understanding, VRL’s debtholders do not have any legal recourse to VDL’s cash flows.

Improved Profitability in Aluminium Business: Ind-Ra expects VAML’s robust EBITDA/t to sustain in FY27 (1QFY27: USD1,804/t; FY26: USD1,154/t; FY25: USD870/t, FY24: USD494/t, FY23: USD322/t), backed by elevated commodity prices and higher volumes from capacity addition. COP is also likely to improve in the near term due to the backward integration initiatives, including the aforementioned capacity expansion of the Lanjigarh alumina refinery and the increase in smelter capacity. The planned increase in the volume share of value-added products to around above 90% from 61%, along with the phased ramp-up of smelter capacity under BALCO over the next two-to-three quarters, will further support operating efficiencies. The timely completion of the ongoing capex and ramp-up of new capacities will be a key monitorable.

The aluminium business will benefit from better margins and cost competitiveness, resulting from improved raw material (coal) security due to the commissioning of captive coal mines in Radhikapur and Kuraloi blocks, Ghorgharpalli blocks, and its dip extension coal block in Sundargarh, Odisha, totalling to 34MTPA, and bauxite mine in the next few quarters. According to management, VAML’s cost of alumina and bauxite is likely to reduce due to higher domestic procurement. The agency expects the high operating leverage in the aluminium segment to ensure higher profitability of USD1,100-1,300/t over the medium term. However, any significant decline in commodity prices could impact the EBITDA/t, as VAML is exposed to commodity price fluctuations.

Comfortable Credit Metrics: VAML is likely to have comfortable credit metrics over FY27-FY29, with the net leverage remaining below 1.5x and the interest coverage remaining above 8.0x. Any improvement in credit metrics will be supported by an improvement in the EBITDA on back of increased sales volumes and EBITDA/t in the near term. The interest coverage (operating EBTDA/gross interest) improved to 6.5x in FY26 (FY25: 3.8x) and net leverage to 1.5x (3.1x) due to a higher EBITDA of INR255 billion (INR179 billion) with a margin of 38.7% (30.4%) and a lower net debt of INR375 billion (INR545 billion).

The group’s net adjusted leverage (including VRL’s debt and brand licensing fees) is also likely to sustain below 2.0x in the near term (FY26: 1.9x; FY25: 2.51x; FY24: 3.2x). Ind-Ra expects the net leverage to range between 1.5x and 2.5x over FY27-FY28, supported by healthy EBITDA, led by elevated commodity prices. The net adjusted leverage improved over FY25-FY26 primarily because of EBITDA (including brand fee; FY26: INR600 billion; FY25: INR455 billion; FY24: INR375 billion), and largely stable net debt (including VRL; FY26: USD11.7 billion; FY25: USD13.0 billion; FY24: USD14.1 billion).

The group has undertaken several transactions such as the qualified institutional placement and offer for sale transactions in FY26, including the recent sale of 1.59% stake in Hindustan Zinc in January 2026 at a floor price of INR685 per share, raising about INR45.9 billion. These proceeds partly contributed to the deleveraging in FY26. Furthermore, the group retains the flexibility to pursue these transactions in case of debt reduction. The management has a clear capital allocation framework that focusses only on high-return capex. However, Ind-Ra does not expect the total adjusted debt (consolidated) to reduce significantly over FY27-FY28 on account of refinancing, dividend payout, and planned capex of USD1.6 billion-1.7 billion.

Ind-Ra expects the group to achieve consolidated EBITDA (excluding brand fees) of INR750 billion-800 billion over FY27-FY28 due to an increase in volumes from the added capacities, increased backward integration through increased coal linkages, and cost efficiencies in the aluminium and zinc businesses. The consolidated profitability is likely to be supported by healthy domestic demand over the medium term, along with higher commodity spreads. In case of declining commodity prices, sustaining EBITDA levels to support ongoing capex and a debt reduction will remain a key monitorable.

Commodity Price and Forex Risks: VAML is vulnerable to volatility in commodity prices; its EBITDA improved in FY25 and FY26 primarily due to an increase in London Metal Exchange and favourable forex movements. Any significant adverse changes in these factors is likely to impact EBITDA.

Expected Investments for Unrelated Diversification over Medium Term: The proposed capex in the semi-conductor and display production business will be executed through VDL, after calling off the joint venture between the Vedanta group and Hon Hai Technology (Foxconn). However, management has stated that the said project is at a nascent stage, and the company does not expect any immediate capital outflow towards the same. The progress of the semiconductor business will depend on VDL identifying a new technology partner and acquiring various regulatory approvals, which will remain a monitorable. Management also does not foresee any significant near-term outflow towards Konkola Copper Mines Plc business, as the planned capex of USD1 billion is likely to be spread over a period of five years, with VRL infusing USD200 million in FY26 by raising debt. However, developments on these fronts will remain a key monitorable. Any extraordinary cash support to the parent’s associate company leading to higher leverage or liquidity stress will be a key monitorable.

## Liquidity

Adequate: The capex plans at Lanjigarh and BALCO have already been concluded, and the company does not have any plans to undertake major growth capex and debt raise towards the same. Furthermore, VAML would have a debt service coverage ratio of over 1.75x over FY27-FY28 with sufficient EBITDA generation to meet the company’s debt servicing obligations. VAML has debt obligations of INR98 billion and INR79 billion in FY27 and FY28, respectively. Being a part of the Vedanta group, VAML maintains strong relationships with domestic banks. Also, the Vedanta group would continue to support VAML’s liquidity and financial flexibility, if the need arises.

VRL’s consolidated debt stood at USD15.4 billion in FY26 (FY25: USD16.2 billion; FY24: USD17.1 billion). The group’s net debt reduced to USD11.7 billion in FY26 (FY25: USD13.0 billion; FY24: USD14.1 billion). VRL’s upcoming maturities will remain important, given the sizeable debt maturities at both entities, alongside continued interest outflows, dividend payouts and ongoing capex requirements. As on June 2026, prior to the refinancing of loans, VRL has debt obligations of INR9.4 billion and INR59.0 billion during FY27 and FY28, respectively.

The group has an established track record of refinancing, with access to domestic and international capital markets (20%-40% of borrowing). The group (including VRL) has repayment obligations of INR266.4 billion and INR229.4 billion in FY27 and FY28, respectively. The group’s (including VRL) unencumbered cash and cash equivalents stood at INR295 billion at FYE26 (FYE25: INR222 billion).

## Rating Sensitivities

Positive: The following factors could, individually or collectively, lead to a positive rating action:
- strengthening of the business profile led by increased share of VAP and backward integration, reducing COP and volatility in EBITDA/t on account of commodity price movements;
- sustaining high profitability from ramp-up of additional capacity and net leverage sustaining below 0.5x; and
- further improvements in VDL group (including VRL), leading to the net leverage reducing below 1.0x, along with reduced gap between the consolidated and proportionate net leverage.

Negative: The following factors could, individually or collectively, lead to a negative rating action:
- a significant decline in the profitability and/or debt-led capex, leading to the net leverage exceeding 1.5x on a sustained basis;
- higher-than-expected cash upstreaming in the form of dividend or otherwise to support group liquidity; and
- net leverage at VDL group (including VRL) exceeding 2.0x, along with a widening of gap between the consolidated and proportionate net leverage, on a sustained basis.

## ESG Issues

ESG Factors Relevant to Rating: The group structure under governance has relevance and moderate impact on VAML’s ratings due to the group’s complexity and significant related-party transactions emanating from inter-company loans, resulting in a higher debt and high dividend leakages. In the past, the group has made efforts towards improving its group structure through increased stake in VDL by VRL. Furthermore, the recent demerger helps in simplifying VDL’s group structure.

Water and wastewater management, and greenhouse gas emission and air quality under environment have relevance and moderate impact on VAML’s ratings.

Unless otherwise disclosed in this section, the ESG issues are credit neutral or have only a minimal credit impact on VAML, due to either their nature or the way in which they are being managed by the entity. ## About the Company

VAML is a public company incorporated on 6 October 2023. The company is engaged in exploring, extracting, and processing aluminium minerals. The aluminium operations include a refinery and a captive power plant in Lanjigarh, a smelter and captive power plants in Jharsuguda, and coal mines in Jamkhani, all in Odisha. BALCO's aluminium operations comprise two bauxite mines, two coal mines, power plants, and smelting and fabrication facilities in Chhattisgarh.

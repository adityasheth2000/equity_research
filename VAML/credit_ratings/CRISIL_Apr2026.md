**Date:** April 30, 2026  
**Agency:** Crisil Ratings Limited  
**Location:** Mumbai

---

## Rating Action

*   **Long-Term Rating:** `Crisil AA` (Watch Developing)
*   **Short-Term Rating:** `Crisil A1+`
*   **Outlook:** Watch Developing
*   **Total Facilities Rated:**
    *   Bank Debt: Rs. 5,000 Crore
    *   Non-Convertible Debentures (NCDs): Rs. 6,089 Crore
    *   **Combined Total:** Rs. 11,089 Crore

**Note:** The ratings are assigned to proposed long-term bank facilities and non-convertible debentures of VAML. These facilities are being transferred from existing limits of Vedanta Ltd pursuant to a scheme of arrangement effective May 1, 2026.

---

## Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has assigned a `Crisil AA/Crisil A1+` rating to the debt instruments of Vedanta Aluminium Metal Limited (VAML), a part of the Vedanta group, and placed the long-term rating on 'Watch Developing'. This watch status is due to the pending finalization of the demerger of the Vedanta group's primary business verticals (aluminium, oil & gas, iron & steel) into standalone entities. While the National Company Law Tribunal (NCLT) approved the demerger scheme in December 2025, Crisil awaits critical clarity on the support philosophy for VAML post-demerger. Specifically, the agency needs details on how outstanding debt of the parent company, Vedanta Resources Ltd (VRL), will be managed via dividends, brand fees, and management fees split across demerged entities.

Despite the uncertainty, Crisil expects the likely rating action to be no more than one notch. The rating reflects VAML's strong business risk profile, characterized by its dominant market position, cost-efficient operations, and strong profitability expected to improve further. These strengths are partially offset by sizeable planned capital expenditure and susceptibility to commodity price volatility.

## Key Rating Drivers — Strengths

*   **Dominant Market Position:**
    *   VAML is the largest domestic producer of aluminium and the third-largest globally (excluding China).
    *   **Market Share:** Held 48% of the domestic aluminium market as of FY2026.
    *   **Capacity:** Overall capacity stands at ~2.8 MTPA (after commissioning 435 KTPA at Balco in FY2026), which is more than double that of the second-largest competitor. Capacity is projected to reach 3 MTPA by FY2028 through debottlenecking.

*   **Low-Cost Operational Position:**
    *   Operates in the first quartile of global cost curves.
    *   **Cost Reduction:** Cost of production improved to **$1,848/tonne** in FY2026 (down from $1,915/tonne in FY2025).
    *   **Drivers:** Strong backward integration, including expanded Lanjigarh alumina refinery capacity (2 MTPA to 5 MTPA), improved linkage coal availability, and lower coal prices reducing power costs.

*   **Strong Profitability & Growth Outlook:**
    *   **EBITDA:** Rose to a record **Rs. 25,208 crore** in FY2026 (up 43% YoY), supported by record-high LME aluminium prices ($2,769/tonne).
    *   **Ebitda per Tonne:** Increased to **$1,171** in FY2026 (vs. $872 in FY2025). Expected to sustain above $1,000/tonne in FY2027+.
    *   **Future Drivers:** Volume growth (smelter capacity expansion), higher value-added product (VAP) share (targeting 2.6 MTPA vs. 1.4 MTPA currently), and sustained high LME prices ($2,800–$3,000/tonne).

*   **Strong Financial Risk Profile:**
    *   **Leverage:** Net leverage stood at **1.47 times** in FY2026. Expected to remain below 2.5 times in FY2027+.
    *   **Coverage:** Interest coverage ratio expected to remain above 8 times.
    *   **Cash Generation:** Healthy cash accrual expected to exceed Rs. 30,000 crore over the next three fiscals (FY2027–FY2029), supporting capex requirements.

*   **Reduced Refinancing Risk at Parent (VRL):**
    *   VRL's net debt reduced significantly to ~$4.9 billion (Mar 2026) from $8.9 billion (Mar 2022).
    *   Debt maturities are manageable (~$500–550 million annually in FY2027), largely covered by brand/management fees and reduced dividend outflows.
    *   Group track record of successful stake sales and refinancing provides comfort.

---

## Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses

*   **Sizeable Planned Capital Outlay:**
    *   **Growth Capex:** Rs. 27,712 crore planned for smelter/VAP expansion, captive mines, and refinery expansion.
    *   **Remaining Spend:** Rs. 8,252 crore unspent as of FY2026.
    *   **Future Commitment:** Over Rs. 15,000 crore expected over the next three fiscals (including sustaining capex).
    *   **Greenfield Project:** Announced plan for a Rs. 1.3 lakh crore greenfield smelter in Odisha (3 MTPA); currently at discussion stage with no capital commitment yet. Timely commissioning without overruns is a monitorable factor.

*   **Susceptibility to Price Volatility:**
    *   Exposure to sharp fluctuations in aluminium prices and input costs (coal, coke, pitch).
    *   **Raw Material Security:** While coal linkage security is 60-70%, reliance on Coal India Ltd remains a risk.
    *   **Mitigation:** Ongoing commissioning of captive bauxite and coal mines is expected to improve resilience over the medium term.

## Liquidity

*   **Assessment:** Strong.
*   **Cash Accruals:** Expected annual cash accrual of **Rs. 20,000–22,000 crore** during FY2027–FY2028.
*   **Utilization vs. Needs:** Sufficient to cover:
    *   Planned yearly capex: Rs. 5,000–5,500 crore.
    *   Scheduled term debt repayments: ~Rs. 10,000 crore (FY2027) and ~Rs. 8,000 crore (FY2028).
*   **Liquid Investments:** Consolidated liquid investments were approximately **Rs. 5,000 crore** as of March 31, 2026.
*   **Refinancing:** Healthy financial flexibility supports timely refinancing if required.

## Outlook & Rating Sensitivity

### Outlook Statement
The outlook is **'Watch Developing'** until clarity is received on the post-demerger support philosophy from Vedanta Resources Ltd (VRL) regarding debt management and fund flows.

### Upward Factors
*   Significantly higher-than-expected EBITDA due to volume ramp-up and cost efficiency (sustained EBITDA per tonne > $900–$1,000).
*   Strong operating profitability generating positive free cash flow and maintaining liquidity.
*   Sustained deleveraging leading to a material reduction in the Net Debt/EBITDA ratio.

### Downward Factors
*   Lower-than-expected EBITDA due to higher production costs, slower volume ramp-up, or lower realizations.
*   Higher-than-expected debt-funded capex or excessive dividend/cash outflows.
*   Net Debt/EBITDA ratio sustaining above **2.5 times**.

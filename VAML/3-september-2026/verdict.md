# Vedanta Aluminium Metal Ltd (VAML) — Verdict

**Date:** 3 September 2026
**Ticker:** VAML (NSE) / 544780 (BSE) · **Price:** ₹436 · **Market Cap:** ₹1,70,454 Cr · **Face Value:** ₹1

> **Newly-listed entity.** VAML is the aluminium business of the Vedanta group, carved out of Vedanta Ltd via a demerger (effective 1 May 2026) and trading independently since June 2026. Historical annuals exist because the aluminium business already ran inside Vedanta Ltd, but VAML has **no independent public track record** yet — its first standalone quarter (Q1 FY27) was reported July 2026. There is only **one available concall** (Jul 2026, PPT only — no transcript). Treat the below as a first-look framework, not a seasoned-company review.

---

## 1. What the company does (plain language)

Vedanta Aluminium makes **aluminium** — the lightweight silver metal used in everything from cars and aircraft to power cables, beverage cans, and building panels.

The process works in three steps, and VAML owns all of them (this is the key to its economics):

1. **Mining** — aluminium comes from an ore called **bauxite** (a reddish rock). VAML is developing its own bauxite mines in Odisha.
2. **Refining** — bauxite is cooked into **alumina**, a white powder, at VAML's Lanjigarh refinery. VAML recently expanded this from 3.5 to 5 million tonnes/year.
3. **Smelting** — alumina is dissolved and zapped with huge amounts of electricity to produce pure molten aluminium ("hot metal"). This is why smelters live next to **captive power plants**. VAML has two smelting complexes: **Jharsuguda** (one of the world's largest single-site smelters) and **BALCO** (Bharat Aluminium Company).

The finished molten metal is cast into **value-added products** (VAP) — billets, wire rods, flip coils, rolled products and foundry alloys — which fetch a **premium** over plain aluminium.

**Who buys it and how it makes money:**
- Customers span **60+ countries** across automotive, aerospace, defence, packaging, power transmission, and construction. India consumes roughly half its output; the rest is exported.
- Revenue is priced off the **London Metal Exchange (LME)** aluminium price plus a premium. Profit = (LME price + premium) − (cost of alumina + power + carbon inputs). Because the metal price is a global commodity, VAML's profit is really a **game of cost** — the cheaper you can mine bauxite, refine alumina and generate power, the more you make regardless of whether LME is high or low.

**Why it has an edge:** vertical integration (own bauxite → coal → captive power → alumina → metal → VAP). Captive power and captive alumina strip out the two biggest input costs, putting VAML in the **first quartile of the global cost curve**.

**Scale:** India's largest aluminium producer with ~48% domestic market share (FY26) and the 3rd-largest globally ex-China. Smelting capacity 2.8 MTPA (vs >2x the next Indian competitor), alumina refining 5 MTPA.

## 2. Company overview & industry positioning

- **Demerger result:** VAML now houses the group's aluminium business, **fully consolidating BALCO**. Promoter (Vedanta Resources Ltd, via Vedanta Ltd) holds **56–58%**; FIIs 12.6%, DIIs 13.9%, public ~17%.
- **Peers:** Hindalco (₹2.27 L Cr mcap), NALCO (₹66k Cr). VAML at ₹1.70 L Cr is the largest pure-play aluminium company in India.
- **Positioning:** #1 in India by volume, cost-leader, and rapidly shifting toward value-added products (VAP share target >90% from ~61%) and backward integration (captive bauxite + coal mines).
- **Industry tailwind:** aluminium is the "metal of energy transition" — demand from EVs, solar, power grid, packaging and India's infrastructure/defence build-out.
- **Key concern:** LME aluminium is at a **record ~$3,571/t** — earnings are at a cyclical peak, not a normalised level.

## 3. Key metrics to track (and why)

1. **LME aluminium price ($/t) — the single biggest driver.** Every $100/t move = **~₹2,000 Cr/yr EBITDA** (per company sensitivity). At ~$3,571/t today vs a FY20-era $1,600–1,800/t, most of the current profit is price, not volume. Watch first.
2. **EBITDA per tonne ($/t).** The true unit profit after cost. Q1 FY27 = **$1,804/t** (vs $870 FY25, $494 FY24, $322 FY23). This number collapses fast if LME corrects faster than cost is reduced.
3. **Hot-metal Cost of Production ($/t).** Competitive moat. Q1 FY27 = **$1,698/t**; FY27 guidance **$1,650–1,700/t**. Falling CoP is management's core promise via captive mines/refinery.
4. **Net debt / EBITDA.** Balance-sheet health and dividend capacity. Improved to **0.9x** in Q1 FY27 (from ~1.3–1.5x FY26). Deleveraging frees cash for dividends/buybacks.
5. **Aluminium production volume + VAP mix.** FY27 guidance **2.6–2.7 MnT**; VAP share target **>90%** (VAP earns a premium over commodity metal). Volume growth protects profit when price is flat/down.
6. **Alumina production / refinery ramp.** FY27 guidance **4.0–4.1 MnT**. Captive alumina substitutes expensive imports and lowers smelter CoP.
7. **Mine commissioning milestones.** Kuraloi coal (Q2 FY27), Sijimali bauxite (H2 FY27), Ghogharpalli coal (H2 FY27). These are the *forward* claims that will de-risk CoP — track regulatory clearances and first production.
8. **ROCE (42% TTM) / return metrics + dividend.** Confirms capital allocation quality; maiden dividend ₹8/share (yield ~1.8%) is small, so watch whether gas burns cash on growth capex or reaches shareholders.

## 4. Management track record (hits vs misses on forward-looking claims)

**Track-record caveat:** the management is the same Vedanta team, but VAML has only **one quarter** as an independent company. Judgements below are therefore mostly about *capex delivery* and *Q1 FY27 vs guidance*, not a multi-year pattern.

**Hits (walked the talk so far):**
- **Demerger executed cleanly** — listed June 2026, ratings immediately re-rated after clarity on debt allocation.
- **Record maiden quarter** — Q1 FY27 revenue ₹21,105 Cr (+45% YoY), EBITDA ₹10,499 Cr (+134%), PAT ₹6,597 Cr (+205%), 50% EBITDA margin. Delivered, not promised.
- **Capex ~90%+ delivered on 3 of 4 projects** — Lanjigarh refinery 2→5 MTPA (92% spent), BALCO smelter + VAP (91% spent), Jharsuguda VAP (98% spent). BALCO's 435 KTPA expansion commissioned in FY26 (BALCO output +17% YoY in Q1).
- **Deleveraging delivered** — net debt cut ₹3,481 Cr in one quarter; net debt/EBITDA 0.9x.
- **Credit rating upgrade earned** — CRISIL & ICRA AA+/Stable, India Ratings IND AA+/Stable (upraded from AA- in a single cycle).
- **Maiden dividend ₹8/share** (₹3,129 Cr) — starting shareholder returns early.

**Misses / still-unproven claims (the forward book):**
- **Cost guidance vs actual** — FY27 CoP guidance $1,650–1,700/t vs Q1 actual $1,698/t (top of the range). The Middle-East war added a $50–100/t furnace-oil headwind management did *not* foresee in the base case.
- **Mine commissioning** — Kuraloi/Sijimali/Ghogharpalli still only ~24% spent; nothing produced yet. The backward-integration cost benefit is entirely a *promise* right now.
- **VAP >90% share** — from ~61%; a stated target, not yet achieved.
- **Greenfield 3 MTPA Odisha smelter** — announced at a headline ₹1.3 L Cr, but explicitly "no capital commitment or timelines" (CRISIL). A classic Vedanta "flag-plant" — will watch whether it is ever funded.

**Verdict on management:** credible operators on cost and delivery within a favourable price environment; the genuinely difficult claims (cost reduction through mines, VAP mix, a giant new smelter) are all still in the future.

## 6. Strategy, growth outlook & management quality

- **Strategy:** resource security + operational integration + VAP → "globally competitive, fully integrated aluminium business." CEO Rajesh Kumar; CFO Anup Agarwal.
- **Growth levers:** smelter ramp to 3 MTPA (debottlenecking); alumina to 5 MTPA; VAP >90%; captive bauxite/coal mines; 150 MW RE battery-storage for green power (₹165 Cr).
- **Capex:** ₹27,203 Cr approved projects, ₹7,146 Cr unspent (mostly the mines). CRISIL sees ₹20,000–25,000 Cr capex over FY27–29. Cash accruals ₹20,000–22,000 Cr/yr are expected to fund it without fresh leverage.
- **Management quality:** strong execution in a rising market; discipline vs the parent group (VRL) is the open question — the group's own leverage ($4.7 bn VRL net debt) and possible cash-fungibility upstreaming are flagged by India Ratings as the key non-operating risk.

## 8. Risks (including credit-rating trajectory)

**Credit-rating trajectory — clearly improving:**
- Apr 30 2026: **CRISIL AA / Watch Developing** (demerger uncertainty; support philosophy unclear).
- Jul 16 2026: **CRISIL AA+/Stable** (+ A1+ short-term) — watch resolved post-demerger.
- Aug 13 2026: **India Ratings IND AA+/Stable**, upgraded from IND AA− (NCDs of ₹40,890 mn). ICRA upgraded to AA+/Stable (Jun 2026).

Drivers of the upgrade: clarity on debt allocation post-demerger, VRL refinancing progress (net debt $4.7 bn vs $9.6 bn FY22), EBITDA/t surge, leverage 1.5x→0.9x. Rating sensitivities flagged by agencies: (down) net debt/EBITDA >1.5–2.0x sustained, cash fungibility to weaker group entities, commodity crash; (up) net debt/EBITDA <0.5x, VAP/backward-integration delivery.

**Other risks:**
- **Commodity/forex volatility** — dominant risk; EBITDA is a price-times-spread function.
- **Regulatory** — ED searches re alleged FEMA breaches; no material impact *yet*, but a key sensitivity.
- **Promoter-group leverage & cash fungibility** — VRL's debt service is funded by fees/dividends upstreamed from VAML/VDL.
- **War-related input inflation** — furnace oil via Middle-East conflict.
- **Large future capex** — greenfield smelter (announced, not committed) could consume the free cash flow.
- **New-listing liquidity/complexity** — 1:1 demerger share allocation can create temporary holders (index/passive churn).

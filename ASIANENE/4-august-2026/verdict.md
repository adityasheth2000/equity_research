## What The Company Does

Asian Energy Services is an energy-services contractor. In simple terms, it helps oil, gas and mining companies find, develop and operate energy assets without those customers having to perform every technical and field activity themselves.

The company earns money from four main activities:

1. **Exploration and seismic services:** It collects and processes underground geological data using 2D and 3D seismic surveys. Customers use this information to decide where to drill.
2. **Integrated field development, EPC and O&M:** It can drill wells, build the facilities that connect them, commission the facilities, and then operate and maintain the field. EPC means engineering, procurement and construction; O&M means operations and maintenance.
3. **Mining and material handling:** It designs, builds and operates coal-handling plants and other bulk-material systems used at mines and power stations. Revenue is earned through project execution and, in some cases, longer operating contracts.
4. **International energy manpower services:** Through Kuiper Group, it supplies specialized personnel and related services to drilling, offshore, EPCI and other energy companies, mainly across the Middle East, Southeast Asia and Africa.

The group also has interests in oil and gas fields and mineral assets through Oilmax Energy. These assets can generate revenue from selling produced oil or gas, but the proposed merger of Oilmax into AESL was still pending on the analysis date. Therefore, the current company should be understood as a services business with an additional upstream-asset and minerals strategy, not as a fully merged upstream producer yet. The proposed post-merger platform is intended to cover exploration, development, production and long-term field management.

Customers and counterparties named across the presentations include ONGC, Oil India, Vedanta, Coal India, GAIL, MCL and other mining, drilling and energy-service companies. The presentations show customer logos, but do not disclose revenue or order-book concentration by customer.

## Company Overview And Industry Positioning

AESL is trying to move from a more cyclical, project-led seismic and services model to a broader platform with recurring O&M, integrated field-development contracts, international manpower revenue, mineral infrastructure and upstream production. Management describes the model as asset-light for the services activities, while the Oilmax assets add ownership of fields and mineral resources.

The strategic logic is attractive but not yet fully proven:

- Integrated contracts can increase contract size and make revenue less dependent on individual seismic projects.
- O&M and manpower contracts can provide recurring revenue, although margins and working-capital requirements must be monitored.
- Kuiper adds international reach and customer relationships, but FY26 comparisons include only about seven months of Kuiper consolidation.
- Oilmax can add high-margin production and mineral assets, but the merger, development capex, field execution and dilution remain unresolved.
- The company is expanding beyond Coal India. A Gujarat State Electricity Corp coal-handling contract reported in the Tavily research is a useful external indication of that effort, but the primary company documents reviewed here should remain the basis for financial conclusions.

Industry demand is supported by India's high crude import dependence, domestic exploration policy, expected oil and gas investment and increased outsourcing in mining and material handling. These are industry drivers, not guarantees of AESL revenue. The company remains exposed to oil and gas activity, government policy, project-award cycles, weather and commodity-linked investment decisions.

### Balance Sheet And Cash Flow

At March 2026, Screener reports equity capital of Rs 45 Cr, reserves of Rs 449 Cr, borrowings of Rs 159 Cr, fixed assets of Rs 115 Cr, CWIP of Rs 46 Cr and total assets of Rs 918 Cr. The May presentation's detailed balance sheet shows trade receivables of Rs 347.8 Cr, contract assets/unbilled WIP of Rs 163.9 Cr, cash and cash equivalents of Rs 108.7 Cr, other bank balances of Rs 38.1 Cr, non-current borrowings of Rs 72.2 Cr and current borrowings of Rs 85.9 Cr.

FY26 cash from operations was Rs 53 Cr, but investing cash flow was negative Rs 159 Cr, financing cash flow positive Rs 124 Cr and free cash flow negative Rs 8 Cr. The negative FCF reflects a capital- and working-capital-intensive growth phase. It also means the reported profit improvement has not yet translated into consistently positive free cash flow.

The apparent difference between management's “net-zero debt” description and gross borrowings should be investigated rather than dismissed. Cash and other bank balances of about Rs 146.8 Cr in the May balance sheet are close to, but below, gross borrowings of about Rs 158.1 Cr before considering other financial assets and restricted cash. CRISIL separately reports Rs 157 Cr cash and Rs 92 Cr unencumbered cash for March 2026. These are different presentations and dates, so a lender-level reconciliation is required.

## Strategy, Growth Outlook And Management Quality

Management's growth plan has three layers:

1. Grow the existing Indian services business by 30%-40% in FY27, with 100-200 bps of standalone margin improvement.
2. Grow Kuiper to USD 60-65 Mn revenue in FY27, improve its approximately 7% EBITDA margin by 100-200 bps, and expand into new clients, geographies and marine/offshore services.
3. Complete the Oilmax merger and scale Mewad, Indrora, Tiphuk, Duarmara, CBM and mineral assets. The company targets approximately 1,000 BOPD from the Mewad/Indrora area by FY27 and approximately 10,000 BOEPD across the Oilmax portfolio by FY29/FY30, but these are targets rather than current production.

Management quality is supported by a long operating history, repeat business from major customers, early Kuiper integration progress, reported well performance and a willingness to disclose explicit operating targets. However, the quality assessment is constrained by:

- FY26 standalone guidance miss.
- Lower FY26 full-year margin despite strong revenue growth.
- Weak recent FCF and high receivable/contract-asset balances.
- No quantified synergy, capex-return, order-book phasing or customer-concentration disclosures.
- Repeated reliance on future merger completion and production ramp-up for the higher-margin thesis.

The FY27 guidance is more credible than a pure top-down forecast because management says 90%-95% is covered by existing orders and one L1 tender. It is not fully de-risked because the L1 tender was not yet confirmed as awarded, and the order book excludes Kuiper and taxes.

## Credit Ratings And Risks

### Rating Trajectory

CRISIL's long-term rating is `BBB+` and short-term rating is `A2`, both on **Watch Developing**. The watch was initiated on 9 September 2025 after the Oilmax merger announcement and remained in place in the November 2025, February 2026, April 2026 and July 2026 reports.

| Date | Agency action | Rated facilities |
|---|---|---:|
| 4 Nov 2025 | CRISIL BBB+ / A2 continued on Watch Developing | Rs 282.5 Cr |
| 2 Feb 2026 | CRISIL BBB+ / A2 continued on Watch Developing | Rs 282.5 Cr |
| 30 Apr 2026 | Ratings continued on Watch Developing; facilities enhanced | Rs 317.5 Cr |
| 29 Jul 2026 | Ratings continued on Watch Developing; NCLT outcome still being monitored | Rs 317.5 Cr |

CRISIL describes liquidity as adequate. The July report cites expected net cash accruals above Rs 84 Cr against Rs 24-25 Cr repayment obligations from FY27 onward, fund-based utilization of about 56%, non-fund-based utilization of 76.25%, and Rs 92 Cr of unencumbered cash as of March 2026. The agency's positive triggers include sustained net cash accruals above Rs 80 Cr and working-capital improvement. Negative triggers include sustained accruals below Rs 50 Cr, further receivable stretch or large debt-funded capex.

India Ratings was listed on Screener for April 2026 and October 2025, but both downloaded pages returned only an agency header and `Loading...`. No India Ratings rating, outlook, rationale or facility amount is used here. This is a data limitation, not evidence that India Ratings took no action.

### Principal Risks

- **Merger and dilution:** The Oilmax merger remains subject to NCLT and related approvals. The September presentation showed a proposed increase in fully diluted shares from 4.98 Cr to 9.20 Cr, with promoter ownership moving from 54.81% to 47.30%. The swap ratio was 117 AESL shares for every 10 Oilmax shares. This can add assets and earnings but materially changes the capital structure.
- **Working capital:** Earlier CRISIL reports cited GCA days of 292-293 and debtor days of 177-284, while Screener's FY26 ratio table reports debtor days of 160 and working-capital days of 106. The differences may reflect reporting dates, consolidation, calculation definitions or subsequent collections. The receivable and contract-asset balances remain large enough to require close monitoring.
- **Cash conversion and leverage:** FY26 FCF was negative Rs 8 Cr and gross borrowings rose to about Rs 159 Cr. The company also uses bank guarantees and working-capital lines. Growth should not be funded by a persistent rise in receivables and debt.
- **Execution and weather:** Monsoon conditions materially affected the Mineral segment and Q2 execution. Q4 also faced West Asia-related supply-chain and client delays. The company has not quantified a complete recovery schedule.
- **Upstream development:** Mewad, Indrora, Tiphuk, Duarmara, CBM and other assets depend on drilling, workovers, pipelines, testing, operator performance, environmental approvals and commercial production. Duarmara's commercial production date was specifically not committed in the May call.
- **Oil and gas and policy exposure:** Environmental, safety, royalty, GST and other government-policy changes can affect project economics. Crude price changes affect owned production, even though several service contracts are less directly price-sensitive.
- **Kuiper integration and margin:** Kuiper adds scale but had approximately 7% EBITDA margin in the cited period. The FY27 margin improvement is a target, not a realized result.
- **Order-book quality:** The order book is large relative to revenue, but the disclosures do not give full contract-level margin, customer concentration, cancellation terms, working-capital needs or revenue recognition schedules.
- **Valuation and expectations:** The share price already reflects substantial growth and successful merger execution. Any delay, dilution without sufficient earnings accretion, weaker margins or poor cash conversion can compress the multiple.

The Screener snapshot shows the following market data at approximately Rs 417.60 per share:

- P/E: 35.17x.
- Book value: Rs 110 per share.
- Implied price-to-book: approximately 3.8x, calculated as Rs 417.60 divided by Rs 110.

- Debt/equity: 0.32x.

The Screener peer table reports a five-company median P/E of 24.49x and median ROCE of 16.48%. Asian Energy's P/E is therefore approximately 44% above the displayed peer median, while its ROCE is broadly in line with the median. The peer set is not clean: it includes Deep Industries, Teja Engineering, Oil Country, DHP India and Duke Offshore, with different business mixes and some loss-making or very high-multiple companies. The comparison is a valuation screen, not a like-for-like intrinsic-value estimate.

The premium can be justified only if investors believe the company will deliver the FY27 growth guidance, recover margins toward or above the guided range, convert the order book into cash, and complete the Oilmax merger without disproportionate dilution or execution problems. At 35.17x trailing earnings and roughly 3.8x book value, the current price leaves limited room for a prolonged guidance miss, merger delay or weak cash conversion.

## Overall Assessment

Asian Energy has a credible growth platform with strong reported revenue growth, a large order book, major customers, improving Q4 execution and strategic optionality from Kuiper and Oilmax. The core investment question is whether this platform can convert into durable, higher-quality earnings and cash flow rather than simply larger reported revenue.

The evidence is not yet sufficient to treat the transformation as complete. The standalone FY26 guidance was missed, FY26 full-year EBITDA margin declined, free cash flow remained negative, working-capital disclosures are inconsistent across sources, and the Oilmax merger and production ramp-up are still pending. At the displayed valuation premium to peers, execution quality and cash conversion matter as much as revenue growth. The appropriate stance is therefore **high-growth but high-execution-risk**, with the merger, margins, receivables, debt and field production as the decisive next tests.

## Unit And Data Caveats

- Screener, investor presentations and transcripts generally report rupee financial data in Rs crore. No Rs million financial figures were identified in the reviewed documents, so no Rs Mn-to-Rs Cr conversion was needed.
- USD 60-65 Mn and USD 100 Mn references relate to Kuiper revenue targets or industry context and must not be mixed with rupee crore figures.
- Production statements use BOPD, BOEPD, barrels, gas volume and SCMD in different documents. These are not interchangeable without an explicit conversion.
- Some extracted PPT and Screener rows contain OCR or date-alignment uncertainty. Where sources conflict, the verdict uses the detailed May 2026 presentation, the transcripts and the latest rating report, and flags unresolved differences rather than forcing a reconciliation.

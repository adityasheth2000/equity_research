## Detailed Rationale
Crisil Ratings has revised its outlook on the long-term bank facilities of Balu Forge Industries Limited (BFIL) to **Negative** from 'Stable', while reaffirming the ratings. The outlook revision reflects a moderation in the group's credit risk profile due to the elongation of the working capital cycle amidst an increasing scale of operations.

While debtor cycles have partially corrected as of September 30, 2025 (from high levels on March 31, 2025, where ~39% of debtors were above 6 months), they remain elevated. Significant scale-ups and higher working capital intensity have led to an elevated reliance on bank borrowings; utilization of the packing credit facility averaged **99%** over the six months ended September 2025.

The rating continues to reflect BFIL's established market position in the crankshaft manufacturing industry and a comfortable financial risk profile. However, these strengths are partially offset by a large working capital cycle and susceptibility to volatility in raw material prices. Management's ability to improve debtor collections and enhance bank lines without exerting liquidity pressure remains a key monitorable.

---

## Key Rating Drivers — Strengths
*   **Established Market Position:** The company has a 3-decade promoter experience base (Mr. Chandock), presence in **80 countries**, and strong supplier/customer relationships. This diversity supported Operating Income growth to **Rs. 924 Crores** in FY 2025 (from Rs. 562 Crores in FY 2024).
*   **Increasing Scale of Operations:** Scale of operations has grown for the last three years. Strong demand in CVs, Heavy Engineering, and Defense sectors supported revenue growth.
    *   Estimated revenue growth of **30% to 35% YoY** for FY 2026 over FY 2025.
    *   Group achieved revenues of around **Rs 533 Crores** in H1 FY 2026.
*   **Comfortable Financial Risk Profile:**
    *   **Net Worth:** Improved to **Rs 1,020 Crores** as on March 31, 2025 (led by equity infusion and reserve accretion).
    *   **Capital Structure:** Improved significantly with Gearing and Total Outside Liability to Adjusted Net Worth ratios at **0.10 times** and **0.26 times** respectively (vs 0.20 and 0.41 times a year ago).
    *   **Debt Protection:** Interest coverage stood at **23 times** and NCATD at **2.04 times** for FY 2025 (vs 8 times and 0.91 times a year ago).

---

## Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses
*   **Large Working Capital Cycle:**
    *   Gross Current Assets (net of cash) ranged around **212 days** as on March 31, 2025.
    *   **Debtor Days:** **158 days** as on March 31, 2025 (Improved from 181 days, but still high).
    *   More than 6 months debtors increased significantly during the year ended March 31, 2025 due to extended credit to new customers and 140-180 day terms for existing customers.
*   **Susceptibility to Raw Material Volatility:**
    *   Key raw materials (Steel, Aluminum) are commodities, making margins susceptible to price fluctuations.
    *   Ability to pass on price increases is constrained due to intense competition. Lag in passing on price fluctuations could impact profitability.
*   **Ongoing Capital Expenditure (Capex):**
    *   Aggressive capex plans (forging capacities) currently funded largely from equity/accruals.
    *   Risk factors include delays in completion, ramp-up delays, or cost overruns impacting profitability.

---

## Liquidity
*   **Bank Limits Utilization:** High utilization of the packing credit facility at approximately **99%** for the 12 months ended September 2025.
*   **Cash Accruals:** Net cash accruals expected to be over **Rs 200 Crore**, sufficient against term debt obligations of Rs 1-8 Crore over the medium term.
*   **Bank Balances:** Around **Rs 96 Crore** as on March 31, 2025.
*   **Current Ratio:** **2.5 times** as on March 31, 2025.
*   **Pressure Point:** Despite adequate coverage, accretion to the cash balance remains moderate due to high working capital intensity and aggressive capex.

---

## Outlook & Rating Sensitivity
**Outlook:** **Negative**
Crisil Ratings believes BFIL’s business and liquidity risk profiles may get impacted due to large working capital requirements amidst growing scale of operations.

| Upward Factors | Downward Factors |
| :--- | :--- |
| • Improvement in the working capital cycle, especially debtor days sustained below **90-100 days** driven by sustained improvement in debtors >6 months. | • Sizeable stretch in working capital cycle, especially debtors sustaining above **130 days**. |
| • Sustained double-digit growth in scale of operations and operating profitability leading to higher cash accruals. | • Deterioration in financial risk profile due to **debt-funded capex** or larger dividend payout. |
| | • Significant decline in the scale of operations or operating margins leading to lower cash accruals. |

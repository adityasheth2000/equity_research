**CMP ₹4,822 | Market Cap ₹4,655 Cr | P/E (TTM) 23.7x | P/B 3.9x | Div Yield 1.45% | 52-wk ₹4,692–6,982**

---

## 1. What the Company Does (Plain Language)

ICRA is one of India's big credit-rating agencies, alongside CRISIL and CARE. When a company or government wants to borrow money by issuing a bond or taking a bank loan, investors want to know how likely they are to get their money back. ICRA does that analysis and assigns a **credit rating** (like AAA, AA, A) that investors rely on to decide whether to lend and at what interest rate. It charges a fee for every rating it assigns and every time it reaffirms an existing rating.

On top of that, ICRA has built a second, faster-growing business called **Research & Analytics (R&A)** — effectively an analytics/IT-services arm. This has three parts:
- **KnowTech** — outsourced knowledge/data work (much of it done for its parent, Moody's, which owns 51.86% of ICRA). Analysts support Moody's global research, data operations and ESG analytics.
- **BankTech** — risk-and-regulatory software for banks/NBFCs (regulatory reporting, early-warning systems, expected-credit-loss models). This includes the recently acquired **Fintellix** and **D2K Technologies**.
- **CapTech** — market data, security valuations, mutual-fund research and indices for asset managers.

**Who pays them?** In ratings, the borrower/issuer pays (the "issuer-pays" model). In R&A, the customers are banks, NBFCs, insurers, asset managers and Moody's itself. **How they make money:** fees are recurring (ratings are reaffirmed annually; R&A is increasingly subscription-based). It is a light-capital, high-margin, high-ROE, cash-generative business with no meaningful debt.

**Ownership:** ICRA is 51.86% owned by Moody's Corporation (the global ratings giant). This is both a strength (global methodologies, captive outsourcing work) and a governance consideration (minority-shareholder interests must be watched).

**Market structure:** India's credit-rating market is highly concentrated — CRISIL ~79% share, ICRA ~13%, CARE ~8%. SEBI's multiple-agency norms guarantee ICRA a durable niche. ICRA is effectively the #2 franchise and the only listed "pure-play-plus-analytics" rating agency with a global parent.

**The strategic story** is a deliberate pivot from a pure ratings house to a broader "risk-analytics" enterprise (July 2026 brand refresh: "Insights that Empower"). Ratings is the mature cash cow (~56% of revenue, ~38% segment margin); R&A is the growth engine (~44% of revenue, ~24% margin) being scaled through the Fintellix and D2K acquisitions.

## 3. Key Metrics to Track (and Why)

1. **Ratings revenue growth + credit ratio (upgrades/downgrades).** Ratings is the highest-margin business and tracks the credit cycle. FY26 credit ratio was 3.1 (best in a decade; upgrades 17%, downgrades 5%). A rising downgrade count is the earliest sign of a credit-cycle turn that would hit both volumes and pricing.
2. **R&A revenue split — organic vs Fintellix.** FY26 R&A grew 29.8%, but ex-Fintellix organic growth was only ~7%. The investment case for R&A depends on *sustainable* organic growth, not just the acquisition.
3. **Segment margins (Ratings ~37.6%, R&A ~24.3%).** Every shift of mix toward R&A dilutes blended margin. Watch whether absolute EBITDA still grows and whether R&A margin holds or recovers.
4. **Fintellix integration — EBITDA margin (~26%) and revenue model.** Management explicitly conceded Fintellix is **EPS-dilutive for the first 2–3 years** (intangible amortization + lost ~₹12–14 Cr post-tax treasury income). The payback hinges on converting license revenue to subscription and cross-selling into ICRA's BFSI clients.
5. **Moody's/KnowTech flows.** KnowTech (the largest R&A piece) is moderating because of automation and parent in-sourcing. This is the single biggest structural drag on R&A and is outside ICRA's control.
6. **Net cash & capital allocation.** >₹700 Cr cash, generating ~₹150–200 Cr free cash flow annually. The question is whether management returns it (dividend/buyback) or redeploys it (more M&A). FY26 dividend was hiked to ₹70 + ₹35 special (₹105 total).
7. **ROE/ROCE.** ROE 16.7%, ROCE 23%. These have been stable-to-rising; a sustained dip below ~15% ROE would signal the R&A pivot is value-destructive.
8. **Default rates.** FY26 overall default rate 0.4%, investment-grade 0.04% — near cyclical lows. These can only mean-revert.

## 4. Management Track Record (Hits vs Misses)

**Hits / promises kept:**
- **Fintellix acquisition executed on schedule** — announced 12 June 2025, closed 17 Oct 2025 (98.75% for ₹245.64 Cr), consolidated from Oct 2025, and already contributing (Q3 FY26 ₹24.9 Cr revenue, ₹4.8 Cr EBITDA). D2K remaining 40% bought (₹32 Cr) to make it 100%-owned.
- **Consistent dividend increases** — normal dividend raised ₹60 → ₹70, plus ₹35 special; payout ratio up from 34% (FY25) to 56% (FY26).
- **R&A structural realignment** (KnowTech / BankTech / CapTech) delivered — market-data and risk-management verticals "turned profitable."
- **Ratings quality held up** — credit ratio of 3.1 in FY26 is the strongest in the reported 10-year table; default rates stayed low.

**Misses / cautions:**
- **Fintellix is admitted to be EPS-dilutive for 2–3 years.** Management gave no IRR/payback disclosure despite repeated analyst pressure.
- **KnowTech (Moody's outsourcing) growth is structurally moderating** — automation + parent in-sourcing; management itself guides to "continued moderation, not higher double-digit growth."
- **ESG ratings business was discontinued** (wound down through Q3 FY25) — a small but visible product failure that dragged R&A growth for two quarters.
- **Q4 FY26 PBT actually *declined* -1.7% YoY** (to ₹72.8 Cr) even as revenue rose 28.4% — Labour Codes exceptional charge + acquisition integration costs masked the operating story. Blended margin math is getting messier.
- **Opaque on acquisition economics** — the Board reviews IRR/payback internally but will not disclose, a governance soft spot for minority shareholders.

## 6. Strategy, Growth Outlook & Management Quality

**Strategy:** (1) Defend and grow the ratings franchise through the credit cycle (bank credit, securitisation, eventual bond recovery); (2) scale R&A from ~44% toward a 50%-of-revenue ambition via BankTech/CapTech and Fintellix cross-sell; (3) become a broader "risk-analytics" enterprise rather than a pure rating agency.

**Growth outlook:** Management guides India real GDP to ~6.2% FY27 (down from ~7.5–7.7%), with bank credit expected to remain robust while bond issuance stays subdued near-term on elevated yields. Structural R&A tailwinds: banks' **ECL transition effective 1 April 2027**, SEBI Mutual Fund Regulations 2026 and the new Specialized Investment Funds (SIFs) structure. Consensus models FY27 revenue ~₹720 Cr (+12%) and EPS ~₹225.

**Management quality:** Competent, conservative, execution-focused. The team (Ramnath Krishnan as MD & Group CEO; new ICRA Analytics CEO Shailendra Mruthyunjayappa succeeding the retired Jayanta Chatterjee from Feb 2026) is candid about the margin-mix trade-off and the Fintellix dilution — a positive on honesty, but the refusal to disclose acquisition IRR/payback and the tepid ex-Fintellix R&A growth are legitimate concerns. The Moody's parentage caps ultimate strategic freedom.

## 8. Risks

- **Moody's relationship (structural):** parent in-sourcing/automation of KnowTech work; 51.86% ownership limits minority influence on strategy and capital return.
- **Margin-mix dilution:** R&A (BankTech/CapTech) earns structurally lower margins than ratings/KnowTech; every point of mix shift is a headwind to blended OPM.
- **Fintellix integration & dilution:** EPS dilution for 2–3 years; no public IRR/payback; execution risk in converting to subscription and cross-selling.
- **Credit-cycle risk:** default rates and downgrade ratios are at cyclical lows; mean reversion would hit the highest-margin business.
- **Competition:** CRISIL dominates (~79% share) with pricing power; Regnology/Nasdaq Technologies and system integrators compete in BankTech.
- **Regulatory/legal:** SEBI rating-agency oversight and ECL-transition timing; a **₹19.17 Cr income-tax demand** on ICRA Analytics (FY24-25, under appeal); an **arbitration award against ICRA** (Delhi HC stay granted in 2024 — monitor outcome).
- **AI disruption:** broadly acknowledged in KnowTech/outsourcing and some analytics work.
- **Credit-rating trajectory (own rating):** ICRA is itself a rating agency, so no external agency-rating report is published on screener.in; the relevant "rating" monitorable is ICRA's own business health as captured by the metrics above. Its negligible borrowings (D/E 0.02) make any credit-profile deterioration unlikely in the near term.

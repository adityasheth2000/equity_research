**Topic: Energy Sector Transition – Structural Forces Reshaping India’s Power Markets**

This slide outlines four key structural drivers influencing India’s power sector, with implications for market evolution and potential growth opportunities for exchanges like IEX. While not containing direct financial metrics or company-specific data, it provides strategic context relevant to equity analysts evaluating firms exposed to energy transition dynamics — particularly those in trading, renewables, storage, or digital infrastructure.

---

### 1. Technology Progress
- **Low Carbon Technologies**: Wind, Solar, Hybrid systems gaining traction.
- **Cost Dynamics**: Falling renewable (RE) & battery costs driving higher penetration and integration into grids.
- **Digital Infrastructure**: “India Energy Stack” — a national digital framework aimed at modernizing the power sector.
- **AI & Analytics**: AI-enabled demand-price forecasting and advanced analytics being deployed to optimize grid operations.

→ *Equity Research Implication*: Companies leveraging AI, digital platforms, or enabling technologies for RE integration may benefit from regulatory tailwinds and cost efficiencies.

---

### 2. Changing Preferences
- **PM Surya Ghar Yojana**: Government scheme promoting rooftop solar adoption by households.
- **Shift Toward RE & Storage**: Increasing consumer and industrial preference for clean energy + storage solutions.
- **Electrification of Economy**: EVs, railways, and data centers increasingly powered by electricity → rising load profiles.
- **Distributed Generation**: Decentralized generation models gaining momentum.

→ *Equity Research Implication*: Growth in distributed generation and electrification could expand addressable markets for exchange-traded products (e.g., green tariffs, carbon credits, P2P trading).

---

### 3. Policy and Regulatory Framework
Key upcoming policies shaping the landscape:
- Draft National Electricity Policy 2026
- Draft Electricity (Amendment) Bill, 2025
- Renewable Consumption Obligations (RCO)
- Green Energy Open Access
- Carbon Credit Trading Scheme (CCTS)
- Electricity Derivatives

→ *Equity Research Implication*: These regulations will likely increase liquidity needs, product complexity, and participation diversity — favorable for exchanges offering derivatives, carbon markets, and open access mechanisms.

---

Emerging business models reshaping value chains:
- **Energy as a Service**: Shift from asset ownership to service-based consumption.
- **New Market Designs Driven by RE Penetration**:
  - BESS (Battery Energy Storage Systems) enabling price arbitrage
  - Firm and Dispatchable RE (FDRE)
  - Virtual Power Purchase Agreements (VPPAs), Contracts for Difference (CfDs)
- **Prosumer & P2P Trading**: Generation moving closer to consumers; peer-to-peer energy trading emerging.

→ *Equity Research Implication*: Exchanges that facilitate these new instruments (especially derivatives around FDRE, CfDs, VPPAs) are positioned to capture incremental volume and margin expansion.

---

→ This is a critical insight for equity analysts: All four forces collectively point toward increased market depth, product innovation, and participant engagement — directly benefiting exchange operators through higher transaction volumes, fee income, and diversification of revenue streams.

**Growth Driver 2: Optimisation Potential (Portfolio Optimization by C&I, DISCOMS Replacing Costlier Power)**

**Key Business Metrics & Strategic Initiatives:**
*   **Tariff Structure:** Long-term Power Purchase Agreements (PPAs) for Discoms utilize a two-part structure: Fixed Cost (paid annually for capacity) and Variable Cost (per unit drawn).
*   **Operational Strategy:** Costlier generation plants can be placed on Reserve Shutdown (RSD) when the Exchange Market Clearing Price (MCP) falls below the plant's variable cost.
*   **Solar Arbitrage Opportunity:** Solar hour prices are currently below Rs. 2/unit, allowing for the replacement of costlier generation by backing down to technical minimums.
*   **C&I Segment:** Similar optimization opportunities exist for Commercial & Industrial (C&I) consumers, particularly during solar hours due to low prevailing prices.
*   **Market Trend (Q1 FY'27):** Prices dipped to near-zero levels in specific day-time slots across roughly 31 days.
*   **Cost Savings Potential:** States optimizing procurement through exchanges can achieve up to ~10% cost savings.

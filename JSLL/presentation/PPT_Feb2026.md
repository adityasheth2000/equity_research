*   **Superior Returns:** ROCE of 71% (*based on 3-Year Average).

## Page 26

**JSLL’s Flywheel Effect: Strategic Business Model Positioning**

This slide outlines the company’s self-reinforcing growth engine — a key strategic moat for long-term value creation. The flywheel illustrates how operational excellence drives financial performance and vice versa.

---

**Key Components of the Flywheel:**

1. **Quality Services → Satisfied Patients**  
   - Effective treatment and quality care lead to high patient satisfaction.
   - This is the foundational input that triggers the entire cycle.

2. **Satisfied Patients → Word-of-Mouth Marketing**  
   - Happy patients become brand advocates, driving organic customer acquisition.
   - Reduces marketing spend per new patient over time (implied efficiency gain).

3. **Word-of-Mouth → Higher Patient Volumes**  
   - Increased volume scales operations without proportional cost increases.
   - Enables economies of scale in staffing, procurement, and facility utilization.

4. **Higher Volumes → Larger Scale & Efficiency**  
   - Operational leverage allows cost-effective treatment delivery.
   - Implies improved operating margins as fixed costs are spread over more procedures/patients.

5. **Larger Scale & Efficiency → Better ROCE**  
   - Return on Capital Employed improves due to higher asset turnover and/or margin expansion.
   - Stronger capital efficiency attracts reinvestment or reduces need for external financing.

6. **Better ROCE + Reinvestment → More Facilities, Better Doctors, Higher Operating Efficiencies**  
   - Profits are reinvested into expanding capacity (new facilities), enhancing talent (better doctors), and optimizing processes.
   - Creates feedback loop back to “Quality Services,” reinforcing the cycle.

7. **Result: Higher Growth**  
   - The cumulative effect is sustained top-line and bottom-line growth.
   - Driven internally by the model rather than solely by market tailwinds.

---

**Strategic Implications for Equity Analysts:**

- **Moat Strength**: The flywheel represents a *systemic competitive advantage* — difficult for competitors to replicate quickly because it relies on accumulated trust, scale, and operational maturity.
  
- **Scalability**: The model suggests strong scalability — each iteration should theoretically yield diminishing marginal costs and increasing returns.

- **Capital Allocation Discipline**: Management appears committed to reinvesting profits into growth levers (facilities, talent) that feed back into the flywheel — indicating disciplined capex with clear ROI expectations.

- **ROCE Focus**: Explicit mention of “Better ROCE” signals management prioritizes capital efficiency — critical metric for healthcare businesses with heavy asset bases.

- **Growth Visibility**: If the flywheel is functioning well, future growth may be less dependent on macro conditions and more driven by internal momentum — providing higher predictability.

---

**Risk Considerations (Implied):**

- Breakdown at any node (e.g., loss of quality leading to dissatisfied patients) could stall or reverse the flywheel.
- Scaling too fast without maintaining service quality risks reputational damage.
- Regulatory changes affecting pricing or reimbursement could disrupt the “cost-effective treatment” assumption.

---

**Conclusion:**

This is not just a marketing diagram — it’s a core strategic framework guiding JSLL’s growth trajectory. For equity analysts, this implies:

- Long-term earnings visibility tied to execution of the flywheel.
- Potential for margin expansion and ROCE improvement over time.
- Lower customer acquisition costs via organic growth mechanisms.
- Need to monitor actual metrics (patient NPS, repeat rate, cost per procedure, ROCE trends) to validate whether the flywheel is delivering as promised.

The model positions JSLL as a vertically integrated, operationally efficient provider capable of compounding value through its own ecosystem — a rare trait in fragmented healthcare markets.

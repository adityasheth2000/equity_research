## 1. What the company does (plain language)

Jeena Sikho runs a chain of **Ayurvedic hospitals, clinics and day-care centres**, and also **sells its own Ayurvedic medicines and wellness products**. Think of it as two businesses bolted together:

**Business 1 — Services (hospitals & clinics, ~₹387 Cr, 48% of FY26 revenue).**
Patients come in for *Panchakarma* — traditional Ayurvedic detox and treatment programs (herbal therapies, diet plans, oil massages, steam, etc.). They stay as in-patients (IPD), visit as out-patients (OPD), or use day-care / video consultation. Payment is cash, health insurance (now ~26% of sales), or government employee panels (CGHS, CAPF, etc.). The headline facility is a 612-bed hospital in Meerut, UP; there are 119 active centres across 23 states, 61 hospitals + 58 clinics/day-care centres, ~2,300 operational beds.

**Business 2 — Products (medicines & wellness, ~₹416 Cr, 52% of FY26 revenue).**
They sell ~330+ SKUs of branded Ayurvedic medicines, health supplements, juices, personal-care items — and even a pet-care line ("Pet Shuddhi"). These are manufactured by third parties (outsourced) and sold through their own centres, tele-calling, e-commerce (Zepto/Blinkit, own website) and increasingly OTC pharmacies. Gross margin on products is ~85%.

**How they actually make money / their moat:** customer acquisition is essentially free, driven by the founder **Acharya Manish Ji's** huge social-media following (16M+ followers) plus free 72-hour "health camps" (~30% of camp visitors convert to paid IPD stays) plus word-of-mouth. The unit economics are unusually good because the model is *capital-light*: a new 100-bed hospital costs only ₹3–4 lakh per bed (vs ₹30–50 lakh+ for an allopathic hospital), breaks even at ~35% occupancy, and pays back in under 6 months. The "flywheel" is: social reach → footfalls → Panchakarma admissions → recurring product purchases → word-of-mouth → more footfalls. Inpatient patients become repeat product customers and vice versa.

**Who the customers are:** middle-class Indians (mostly North India) seeking affordable, drug-free treatment for lifestyle/chronic conditions — obesity, joint/back pain, thyroid, diabetes, skin, liver, kidney, sexual-health issues — plus preventive "wellness" seekers. The company is deliberately shifting mix toward private-pay and insured patients (higher margin, faster cash) and away from government panels (slow payers).

## 3. Key metrics to track

1. **Revenue growth + Services/Products mix** — the model needs both engines. FY26 split 48/52; management targets a 45–55% band. *Why:* mix drives blended margin (products ~85% GM vs services ~91% GM) and cash cycle (services = faster cash, products = higher inventory).

2. **EBITDA margin (target 40%+)** — expanded 12% → 44% over FY22–FY26. *Why:* it is the single biggest driver of the multiple; any step-up in ad spend, doctor payroll or lease cost lands here. Q4 FY26 dipped to 36% (one-offs); watch it recover to 40%+.

3. **Beds + occupancy** — 2,300 operational, 59% occupancy now; target 3,000–3,500 beds (FY27) and 75–80% occupancy. *Why:* revenue is occupancy × beds × ₹~8,300/bed/day. Rapid bed additions dilute occupancy and flatter reported growth (Q1 FY27: +100 beds but revenue only +4% QoQ).

4. **IPD volumes & ARPB (₹/bed/day)** — FY26 IPD 40,454 (+65% YoY); ARPB ~₹8,300. *Why:* IPD is the highest-margin, highest-LTV customer; ARPB vs insurance rate (₹8,800–9,200/day) shows pricing headroom and the self-pay discount strategy.

5. **Cash conversion — CFO/OP, FCF, debtor days** — FY26 CFO ₹264 Cr, FCF ₹221 Cr, CFO/OP 94%; debtor days 66 (FY25) → 32 (FY26). *Why:* the biggest historical knock on this company was government receivables; watch that private/insurance mix keeps debtor days low.

6. **ROE/ROCE** — 61%/64%. *Why:* the bull case is "capital-light, high-return compounding." If in-house manufacturing, the Manali resort, or big new hospitals absorb capital, ROCE falls.

7. **Government-panel revenue %** — collapsed from 25% (FY25) to ~4% (FY26) to ~2% (Q1 FY27). *Why:* the entire FY27–FY28 growth case depends on private/insurance/OCT filling this hole; a re-entry into govt/Ayushman at scale is a swing factor both ways.

8. **Product/OTC ramp & distribution (Entero)** — products +93% FY26; OTC +₹3 Cr QoQ run rate; Entero (1.25 lakh pharmacies) repeatedly delayed. *Why:* the ₹3,000 Cr revenue ambition leans heavily on products; distribution execution is the key proof point.

## 4. Management track record — has management walked the talk?

**Hits:**
- **FY26 revenue ₹801 Cr (+71% YoY)** — at Q2 FY26 (Nov 2025) they guided ₹650–700 Cr; they delivered well above the top of the range.
- **Margin delivery** — EBITDA margin 30% → 44% in one year; PAT margin 17% → 27%; ex-one-offs Q4 EBITDA margin would have been 46%.
- **Bed build-out** — the 2,800-bed full-year target was hit within 6 months; 2,300 beds operational by FY26-end; 2,861 built.
- **Governance upgrade executed** — migrated SME→mainboard (Aug 2025), appointed Grant Thornton + Forvis Mazars, Salesforce CRM + Oracle ERP, new board/CS (Jul 2026), ESOP revamp.
- **Consistent dividends** FY23–FY25; final ₹4.50/share recommended for FY26.

**Misses / yellow flags:**
- **FY25 PAT restated down** — originally presented ~₹90.7 Cr (Nov 2025 deck), restated to ~₹79.9 Cr after the Ind AS transition (depreciation up ₹8.7 → ₹29.1 Cr; finance cost up ₹0.5 → ₹10.7 Cr on capitalized leases). A ~12% retrospective cut in reported profit.
- **FY26 PAT slightly below own target** — MD said "₹225 Cr plus" (Feb 2026); actual ₹222 Cr.
- **Entero distribution kept slipping** — "finalized" (Nov 2025) → "did not start" (Jun 2026) → "products live within 15 days" (Aug 2026).
- **Guidance style** — refuses to raise formal guidance while over-delivering, but simultaneously floats aggressive aspirational targets (₹3,000 Cr revenue, ₹1,000 Cr PAT, 7,000–10,000 beds in 3–5 years ≈ 4x current revenue/4.5x PAT) that are far from committed.
- **Government business collapse** — ₹117.8 Cr (FY25) → ₹36 Cr (FY26) → ₹5 Cr (Q1 FY27). Deliberate (receivables/bad-debt), but it removed ~15% of revenue that must be replaced.
- **Communication style** — MD repeatedly deflects quarterly scrutiny ("judge YoY, not QoQ") and cited geopolitics (India-Pakistan, Iran-America) for the Q4 FY26 volume dip — a governance/communication red flag to monitor.

**Net read:** strong operational execution and impressive unit economics, but aggressive targets, an accounting restatement, and repeated delays on promised initiatives mean forward-looking statements should be heavily discounted until proven.

## 6. Strategy, growth outlook & management quality

**Strategy (management's 7-point "way forward"):**
1. Sweat existing assets (occupancy 59% → 75–80%); 2. new centres + bed additions (3,000–3,500 beds FY27; 7,000–10,000 beds in 3–5 yrs); 3. OTC pharmacy channel; 4. new product launches (16 products by Dec 2026); 5. cashless insurance enablement; 6. increased Govt Panel/AYUSH coverage; 7. international expansion (UAE first; centres in Abu Dhabi + 4 Dubai under construction).

**New bets being layered on:**
- **Ultra-luxury wellness "Jeena Sikho Premium" in Manali** — 108 rooms + 22 villas, leased (3+3+3 yr), opens Sep–Oct 2026; Y1 ~50% occupancy, ADR ₹32–35k, EBITDA ~35–40%; Y2 ~60% occupancy, ~7,700 room-nights, ADR ₹35–37k. Management claims "no opex of our own except wages." 3–4 more luxury centres planned (~₹3–5 Cr net profit each).
- **Daycare-centre chain** (new, "execution starting soon"); **in-house manufacturing** (to lift product margin + quality control); **Chandan diagnostics JV** (34 centres live, zero JSLL capex, target ₹5 lakh/day); **Entero/Satkartar distribution**; **medical-college takeovers** (3 tied up, 1 operational).

**Management quality:** strong founder-led marketing machine and real operating leverage, but (a) one accounting restatement already, (b) ambitious, repeatedly-revised guidance, (c) several promised initiatives slipping (Entero, Ayushman, college ramp), and (d) a communication style that deflects scrutiny. Treat it as high-growth/high-execution-risk rather than a defensive compounder. The deliberate board/auditor/Salesforce-Oracle upgrades (Jul 2026 board refresh) are a positive response to earlier governance concerns.

## 8. Risks

1. **No credit rating** — screener lists no credit rating for JSLL; the only CRISIL reference found (BB+/Stable) is for a separate promoter-group entity ("Jeena and Company"), not JSLL. Absence of an independent rating view is itself a gap, especially as the company adds lease liabilities (~₹102–127 Cr) and contemplates capex (resort, manufacturing). *Track:* any fresh bank debt or rating initiation; watch D/E (lease-driven) vs the "debt-free" narrative.
2. **Founder/key-person & reputation concentration** — customer acquisition rides on Acharya Manish Ji's social-media funnel (16M followers) and brand trust in a category with occasional efficacy/regulatory scrutiny.
3. **Accounting/restatement risk** — FY25 PAT already restated down ~12% on Ind AS transition; revenue-recognition change (advances no longer booked early) and lease/ECL provisions are fresh. Audit quality (Grant Thornton) is improving but the history warrants vigilance.
4. **Geographic concentration** — UP/North India (Meerut flagship 612 beds); seasonality in Q3.
5. **Aggressive guidance / execution risk** — ₹3,000 Cr revenue & ₹1,000 Cr PAT in 3–5 yrs is ~4x current scale; multiple simultaneous initiatives (luxury resort, daycare chain, in-house manufacturing, UAE, OTC).
6. **Regulatory & policy** — Drugs (11th Amendment) Rules 2026 (GMP, QR traceability) raise compliance cost; Ayushman Bharat Ayurveda inclusion and insurance cashless-parity remain unresolved (regulator recently rebuked insurers for rejecting Ayurveda claims — a tailwind if enforced, but timing uncertain).
7. **Working-capital/AR quality** — the govt-receivables episode is the main historical scar; debtor days improved to 32 but watch the insurance-led mix.
8. **Valuation & liquidity** — P/E ~26x, P/B ~13x, 4.18x Nifty volatility, thin sell-side coverage, small free float (~31%).

## 9. Valuation context & peer comparison

- **P/E 26.4x TTM** (TTM EPS ₹19.03); **P/B ~13.4x** (BV ₹37.6); **EV/EBITDA ~16.9x** (EV ≈ ₹6,132 Cr after ~₹108 Cr cash; TTM EBITDA ₹363 Cr).
- **History:** the stock ran from ~₹280 (52-wk low area, 2025) to ₹850, then de-rated ~33% to ₹492–502 (near 52-wk low) despite earnings doubling — the market has priced in deceleration, governance concerns, and the govt-revenue exit. P/E compressed from ~40x to ~26x.
- **Peer comparison (screener):** the listed "wellness" set is tiny and loss-making — Kaya (mcap ₹508 Cr, loss-making) and Lloyds Luxuries (mcap ₹145 Cr, loss-making). **There is no true listed comparable** for an integrated Ayurvedic hospital+products chain; JSLL is the only profitable name. On the products side, Dabur trades at ~50x+ but is a very different (FMCG, established-brand) business.
- **Sell-side (thin):** 1 consensus analyst — Strong Buy; Choice (Jun 2026) Buy with ~44% upside; target prices cited ₹900–1,200 (MSN, LinkedIn, Simply Wall St). Edelweiss's older ₹1,650/₹2,150 targets are stale. Treat targets as indicative only given one-analyst coverage.

**Bottom line:** a genuinely fast-growing, high-ROCE, capital-light business with exceptional unit economics — but the multiple already prices in flawless execution, and the record shows restatement + guidance slippage + a founder-key-man model. It's a high-conviction growth bet that must be tracked quarterly, not a set-and-forget compounder.

## Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has continued the long-term bank facilities of BLS International Services Limited (BLS) on 'Rating Watch with Developing Implications'. The rating action follows the company's appeal against a debarment order issued by the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA), which had initially prohibited the company from participating in new tenders for Indian Missions for two years. On October 23, 2025, the rating was placed on watch. Subsequently, the Hon'ble High Court of Delhi set aside the debarment order, quashing the restriction. However, Crisil Ratings continues to monitor the situation to assess any financial impact on the group's ability to bid at competitive pricing and secure new orders. The current rating reflects the group's established market position and robust financial risk profile, partially offset by susceptibility to regulatory changes and risks associated with growth through acquisitions.

---

## Key Rating Drivers — Strengths

*   **Established Market Position:**
    *   Over 30 years of experience with a strong brand presence in the visa, passport, consular, and citizen services sector.
    *   Global presence in **more than 70 countries**, catering to 45-50 government sector clients worldwide.
    *   One of the largest global players in the sector.
*   **Stable Revenue Growth:**
    *   Revenue projected to increase to **Rs 2,200–2,300 crore** during fiscal 2025, up from Rs 1,677 crore in fiscal 2024 (YoY growth of nearly 35%).
    *   Expected business risk improvement driven by tourism growth, digital penetration, and acquisitions (e.g., iData, Aadifidelis Solutions, Citizenship Invests DMCC).
*   **Robust Financial Risk Profile:**
    *   **Adjusted Networth:** Projected at **Rs 1,800–1,900 crore** as on March 31, 2025 (up from Rs 1,314 crore in FY2024).
    *   **Minimal Debt:** Debt capital structure remains minimal, with improving operating margins.
    *   **Flexibility:** Strong financial flexibility with no planned debt-funded capex or acquisition stretch in the working capital cycle.

## Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses

*   **Regulatory Susceptibility:**
    *   Contracts with diplomatic missions are tender-based. Non-renewal at term end or inability to bid for new contracts could weaken the revenue profile.
    *   Risks related to changes in regulations by ministries of various countries.
*   **Acquisition-Related Risks:**
    *   Growth strategy involving inorganic expansion (recently acquired entities in visa and digital services).
    *   Credit profile could be materially altered if acquired businesses fail to ramp up to expected levels.

## Liquidity

*   **Assessment:** Strong
*   **Cash Accruals:** Expected annual accretion of **Rs 500–520 crore**, sufficient to cover working capital expenses, capex, acquisitions, and minimal term debt obligations (Rs 25-28 crore).
*   **Bank Limit Utilisation:** **Nil** (Prudent working capital management).
*   **Cash Reserves:** Sizeable cash reserves estimated at **Rs 950–1,000 crore** as of September 2024.
*   **Net Worth:** Strong adjusted networth enhances overall financial flexibility.

---

## Outlook & Rating Sensitivity

**Outlook:** Watch Developing

### Upward Factors
*   Sustained growth in revenue exceeding **Rs. 3,000 crores** driven by new customer onboarding and timely contract renewal.
*   Steady operating margin of **30-31%**, leading to higher-than-expected net cash accrual.
*   Efficient working capital management ensuring low to nil reliance on external debt.

### Downward Factors
*   Weakening of capital structure due to large, **debt-funded capital expenditure** or acquisitions.
*   Cash accrual falling below **Rs. 250–300 crore** due to decline in operating income, margin, or unfavorable business acquisitions.
*   Any unfavorable/unrelated business acquisition.

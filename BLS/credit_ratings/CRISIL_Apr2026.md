## Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has removed the rating on the long-term bank facilities of BLS International Services Limited (BLS) from ‘Rating Watch with Developing Implications’ and reaffirmed it at ‘Crisil A+’ with a ‘Stable’ outlook. The previous watch was imposed on October 23, 2025, following an order by the Ministry of External Affairs (MEA) debarring the company from new tenders for a two-year period. Although this order was quashed in December 2025, the agency monitored for potential impacts on financial performance and bidding abilities.

Currently, Crisil Ratings believes there is no material impact on the group’s financial performance. For the nine months ended December 2025 (Fiscal 2026), the company reported revenue of Rs. 2184 crores with operating margins of nearly 28%, which outperforms the previous year's corresponding period. Fiscal 2026 full-year revenue is expected to close at Rs. 2900–3000 crores. Business risks are supported by new MEA tenders for China and orders from Spain during January–March 2026, indicating unaffected bidding capabilities.

The ratings reflect BLS's established market position in the visa outsourcing sector and robust financial risk profile. However, these strengths are offset by moderate operating performance (specifically in the digital segment), susceptibility to regulatory changes by foreign ministries, and risks associated with growth through acquisitions.

---

## Key Rating Drivers — Strengths

*   **Established Market Position:**
    *   Promoters have over three decades of experience, building a strong brand.
    *   Present in more than 70 countries, catering to 45-50 government clients worldwide.
    *   One of the largest players globally in visa, passport, consular, and citizen services.
    *   Revenue expected to increase to Rs. 2,900–3,000 crore in FY26 (from Rs. 2,211 crore in FY25), reflecting ~35% year-on-year growth.
    *   Growth supported by tourism, digital penetration, and acquisitions (Aadifidelis Solutions Pvt. Ltd., Citizenship Invests DMCC).
*   **Robust Financial Risk Profile:**
    *   Adjusted networth projected at Rs. 2400–2600 crore as of March 31, 2026 (up from Rs. 1,908 crore as of March 31, 2025).
    *   Minimal debt capital structure and improving operating margins.
    *   Expected to provide strong financial flexibility; no debt-funded capex or acquisition plan currently anticipated.
    *   Prudent working capital management and better accretion to reserves.

---

## Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses

*   **Moderate Operating Performance:**
    *   Scalability relies heavily on the Visa business (60–70%), while the Digital business remains moderate.
    *   Digital segment revenue estimated at ~Rs. 800 crore for 9M-FY26.
    *   Operating profitability in the digital segment remains modest at <10%, potentially constraining group operating profitability.
*   **Susceptibility to Regulatory Changes:**
    *   Diplomatic mission contracts are tender-based with strict prerequisites (experience, IT infrastructure, etc.).
    *   Risk of non-renewal at end of term or inability to bag new contracts if regulatory conditions change by concerned ministries.
*   **Risks Related to Growth Strategy:**
    *   Growth has been both organic and inorganic (acquisitions in visa and digital services).
    *   If acquired businesses are not ramped up as expected, or acquisitions are debt-funded, the credit profile could be materially altered. This is a key rating sensitivity factor.

---

## Liquidity

Liquidity is assessed as **Strong**.

*   **Cash Accrual:** Expected annual cash accrual of Rs. 680–720 crore, sufficient to cover incremental working capital expenses, capex, acquisition plans, and term debt obligations (Rs. 25–28 crore).
*   **Bank Limit Utilisation:** Nil bank limit utilization.
*   **Cash Reserves:** Sizeable cash reserve of nearly Rs. 1,294 crores as of December 2025.
*   **Financial Flexibility:** Enhanced by strong net worth and working capital management.

---

## Outlook & Rating Sensitivity

*   **Outlook:** **Stable**
    *   The BLS International group is expected to benefit from its established market position across diverse segments (passport, consular, citizen services) and continued presence across 70+ countries.

| **Direction** | **Factors** |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Upward Factors** | 1. Significant and sustained ramp-up in operations in both business segments, leading to overall revenue growth of over 20–25% while maintaining operating profitability at over 30%.<br>2. Efficient working capital management ensuring low to nil reliance on external debt, sustaining the robust financial risk profile and liquidity. |
| **Downward Factors** | 1. Weakening of the capital structure because of large, debt-funded capital expenditure or acquisitions.<br>2. Cash accrual falling below Rs. 400 crore, owing to a decline in operating income and/or margin, or any unfavorable/unrelated business acquisition. |

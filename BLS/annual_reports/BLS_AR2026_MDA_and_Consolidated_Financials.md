## 6. Global Tourism Recovery Arc — 2019 to 2025 (p.81)
International tourist arrivals reached 1.465 billion in 2024 (essentially recovering the 2019 peak) before breaking through to a new record of **1.523 billion in 2025, up 4% YoY**. CY2025 marks the first full post-pandemic year conclusively above the 2019 high. Demand is now self-sustaining — driven by normalised mobility, emerging-market outbound growth, and the structural rise in experiential travel, rather than post-pandemic pent-up demand.

Arrivals trajectory: 2019 peak 1,469M → 2020 409M (−72.2%) → 2021 462M (+13.0% crisis year) → 2022 979M (+111.9% rebound) → 2023 1,322M (+35.0% normalisation) → 2024 1,465M (+10.8% recovery complete) → 2025 1,523M (+4.0% new all-time record) → 2026E 1,576M (+3.5% baseline forecast).
*Source: UN Tourism; 2026 baseline estimate anchored to UN Tourism official 3%–4% growth guidance.*

## 8. Global Air Passenger Market (pp.83–84)
Global full-year air passenger demand in 2025 (RPK) rose **5.3% YoY**, while ASK expanded **5.2%** — both at historic highs. Passenger Load Factor reached a record **83.6%**, reflecting structural tightening of supply-demand balance as aerospace supply-chain disruptions delayed new aircraft deliveries.

**Asia-Pacific — the dominant growth engine:** Accounts for **34.5% of global RPK** (largest regional share); 2025 total RPK growth **7.8%**, international RPK **10.9%** (vs global averages of 5.3% and 7.1%). Region's international PLF reached **84.4%** in 2025. Into 2026, Asia-Pacific carriers recorded **11.5%** RPK growth in March 2026 and a record international PLF of **91.2%**. The Europe–Asia corridor surged **29.3%** in March 2026, partly reflecting traffic diverted from disrupted Middle Eastern hubs.

**Africa — the fastest-accelerating market:** Led all regions in total RPK growth in 2025 at **9.4%**; international RPK +7.8%, PLF +0.7pp to 75.3%. African carriers posted **20.6%** RPK growth in March 2026 — more than nine times the global average of 2.1%. International capacity from Africa grew 4.2% against demand growth of 19.2% in March 2026, pushing PLF up nearly 10pp to **77.7%** — a record high. BLS has an established operational presence across Africa.

**Middle East — geopolitical disruption and traffic diversion:** 2025 full-year total RPK +6.8%, international RPK +6.7%, PLF +0.7pp to 81.5%/81.6%. In **March 2026** traffic carried by Middle Eastern airlines contracted **58.6% YoY**, international RPK −60.8%, capacity −54.7%, following airspace closures linked to heightened regional geopolitical tensions. PLF fell to 68.3% — the only region globally to record a load-factor decline. Global capacity expected to partially recover from May 2026, though Middle-East-serving seats are projected to remain **18.3% below prior-year levels**. This disruption compressed near-term demand from applicants transiting Gulf hubs (particularly from the Indian subcontinent) and remains a key variable for the Company's revenue outlook in the current financial year.

**International air passenger market:** Full-year international demand in 2025 grew **7.1%** (vs overall market 5.3%); international routes capacity +6.8%; full-year international PLF reached a record **83.5%**.
*Sources: IATA Air Passenger Market Analysis, December 2025 and March 2026.*

## 11. Challenges & Outlook (p.87)
**Challenges:** Geopolitical uncertainties, fluctuating fuel prices and evolving immigration policies create friction in international travel flows. Persistent aircraft delivery delays and capacity constraints limit airlines' ability to scale operations in line with demand. Rising labour costs and sustainability compliance requirements (particularly around Sustainable Aviation Fuel mandates) add structural cost pressure. Gateway city congestion, accommodation strain and seasonal crowding are emerging policy concerns for destination governments. These introduce near-term volatility into the pace and pattern of growth.

**Outlook:** The long-term outlook has shifted from post-pandemic rebound to structural expansion. WTTC projects the sector's GDP contribution to reach **USD 16.5 trillion by 2035** — equivalent to 11.5% of global GDP — up from USD 11.6 trillion in 2025, implying sustained real growth of ~3%–4% annually over the decade.
- **USD 16.5 Tn** — T&T GDP contribution by 2035 (WTTC long-term projection)
- **460 Mn** — jobs in T&T by 2035 (+91 Mn new roles; 1 in 3 new jobs globally)
- **4.9** — IATA RPK growth forecast CY2026 (record PLF of 83.8% expected)

**2025 quarterly growth pattern — normalisation confirmed:** Q1 2025 +3.7%, Q2 2025 +5.2%, Q3 2025 +4.2%, Q4 2025 +3.1% — all within the 3%–5% guided range, confirming a stable, seasonal expansion cycle replacing the V-shaped rebound.

## 13. Visa & Consular Services Industry (pp.90–94)

The visa and consular outsourcing industry has entered a phase of structural acceleration — governments worldwide are increasingly mandating specialist third-party providers to manage consular operations, reduce operational overhead and deliver a superior applicant experience.

- **USD 3.66 Bn** — Total Global Visa Services Market CY2025 — growing at **14.3% CAGR through 2033**
- **USD 3.2 Bn** — Global Outsourced Visa Market CY2025 — projected **USD 5.1 Bn by 2033**
- **50%** — Outsourced share of total applications projected by CY2029 — up from **38% in CY2024**
- **17%** — BLS market share by value, global visa outsourcing ex-USA — CY2024

The total global visa services market (outsourced application processing, biometrics, document verification, courier, VAS) is ~**USD 3.66 billion in CY2025**, growing at 14.3% CAGR through 2033. The outsourced segment is growing faster — ~USD 3.2 billion in CY2025, projected to reach USD 5.1 billion by 2033. The outsourced share of total applications has risen steadily from 22% in 2010 to 35% in 2021 to 38% in CY2024, and is projected at 50% by CY2029. BLS International holds a **17% share by value and over 10% by volume** in the global visa outsourcing market, excluding the United States.

*Market definition note:* The USD 3.66 Bn / 14.3% CAGR figure represents the total visa services market (all segments). The USD 3.2 Bn / USD 5.1 Bn figure represents the outsourced-only sub-segment, sourced from a separate research series.

### Residency and Investment Migration (p.94)
BLS participates in the investment migration segment through wholly owned subsidiary **Citizenship Invest**, acquired in **October 2024 for ₹260 Crores (~USD 31 million)**. Founded in 2009 and headquartered in Dubai, Citizenship Invest has served more than **1,800 clients across 85 nationalities**, maintaining a **99% application success rate** against an industry average of ~80%.

The global investment migration industry surpassed **USD 30 billion** in annual market value during 2024, maintaining ~12% YoY growth, with over 80 countries offering some form of programme. Henley & Partners' Private Wealth Migration Report 2025 recorded **142,000 millionaire relocations in 2025** — a new high — with forecasts pointing to **165,000 in 2026** (largest migration of wealth on record). The market enters 2026 facing a more uncertain regulatory environment: the EU hardening its stance on citizenship-by-investment programmes and the US imposing travel restrictions on citizens of select nations operating such schemes — reinforcing the importance of jurisdictional diversification.

**Outlook:** The total global visa market is projected to reach **USD 7.7 billion by CY2029**. The outsourced segment is forecast to grow at a **14% CAGR**, with outsourced visa applications reaching **70 million at an 8% CAGR by CY2029**. Shaped further by AI/automation adoption, biometric integration, expansion of e-visa systems, and premium service differentiation. **BLS's strategic position:** 17% market share by value (ex-USA), 10%+ by volume, operations across **66 countries**, plus Citizenship Invest in investment migration.

## 14. Company Overview (pp.96–99)

**Complementary expansions:** opened new visa and consular service centres across Asia, the Middle East, Africa and Latin America. Also expanded Spain and Slovakia visa operations across multiple international markets, strengthened Cyprus visa footprint, and enhanced UAE attestation and document legalisation network through new service locations across India and Russia.

## 16. Strengths, Opportunities & Threats (pp.104–107)

### Strengths (p.104)
- **Global reach and sovereign trust:** presence across 80+ countries, 46 client governments; successful renewal of the MEA India Attestation & Apostille Services contract; new wins such as Indian VACs across China.
- **Long-term contracts and revenue visibility:** most government contracts run **five to ten years on an exclusive basis**, ensuring no overlap with competing vendors in a given region. New long-term mandates in FY26 (5-year Slovak global contract; expanded Cyprus mandate) extend visibility.
- **Asset-light operations and execution speed:** all VACs operate on leased premises, keeping the business capital-light and enabling rapid deployment in response to contract wins.
- **Technology leadership and data security:** AI-led identity verification, liveness and anti-spoofing aligned with ISO/IEC 30107, and GDPR-aligned data handling; increasingly cited as a differentiator in financial bidding processes.

### Opportunities (pp.104–105)
- **Aadhaar and National-ID adjacencies beyond UIDAI:** having proven execution on the UIDAI Aadhaar Seva Kendra order, BLS is positioned to bid for similar state-level and national identity-infrastructure mandates beyond Bihar's PECs.
- **Re-tender wave worth USD 1–2 billion:** a cluster of major visa-outsourcing contracts come up for renewal over the next two years — estimated **USD 1–2 billion** opportunity. BLS starts the renewal cycle from a position of strength (operational incumbency, data history, embassy relationships).
- **Monetising the digital services platform beyond scale:** Digital Services revenue grew 114.4% in FY26, but **EBITDA margin compressed to 7.0% from 11.1%**, reflecting the cost of rapidly scaling touchpoints and integrating new businesses. Opportunity in FY27+ is to convert scale into margin — cross-selling loan distribution, e-governance and assisted e-commerce through the same **1,55,000+ touchpoint network**.
- **Consular and citizen services as a second growth vector:** passport renewal, document attestation, apostille and notarial work are structurally less cyclical than tourist-visa volumes and carry their own renewal economics (evidenced by the MEA Attestation & Apostille contract renewal).
- **Geographic diversification through new embassy wins:** Indian VACs in China, the 5-year Slovak global contract, and the Cyprus mandate spanning Russia, China, South Africa, Kazakhstan and Southeast Asia open multiple new country corridors.
- **AI and automation as a bidding differentiator:** as tenders weight technical and security capability, investment in AI-driven document verification, fraud-resistant waitlisting and automated workflow management can win higher-value mandates rather than competing purely on price.

### Threats (pp.106–107)
- **Margin pressure in the scaling digital segment** — *Risk:* Digital Services EBITDA margin fell to **7.0% in FY26 from 11.1% in FY25** even as revenue more than doubled. If newly acquired and rapidly scaled businesses (Aadifidelis, BC network) continue integrating at lower near-term profitability, blended consolidated margins could come under continued pressure before synergies are realised. *Mitigation:* cross-sell across the combined touchpoint network and centralisation of back-office/technology costs, aiming to lift segment margins as integration matures.
- **Concentration risk from large single contracts** — *Risk:* a meaningful share of incremental FY26 growth is linked to a small number of large mandates — notably the UIDAI Aadhaar Seva Kendra order and the consolidation of Aadifidelis. Any delay, scope reduction or non-renewal of a single large government contract could have an outsized effect. *Mitigation:* diversify government-client base across geographies and service lines; aggressively bid for the next wave of renewals.
- **Working-capital and integration risk from inorganic growth** — *Risk:* continued M&A (e.g., Aadifidelis consolidation in FY26) introduces integration risk: differing data systems, compliance regimes and working-capital cycles across acquired entities (particularly lending and BC operations, which do not enjoy the same upfront-fee, negative-working-capital characteristics as the core visa business) could dilute the Group's historically strong cash conversion. *Mitigation:* evaluate targets for strategic fit; integrate finance/technology centrally; report segment-level financials to allow early identification of subsidiaries under cash or compliance strain.
- **Cybersecurity and data-protection exposure at greater scale** — *Risk:* widening digital footprint (biometric data, Aadhaar enrolment records, loan applicant data, embassy visa data across more countries and subsidiaries) increases attack surface and regulatory exposure under GDPR and India's data-protection regime. *Mitigation:* ISO 27001-aligned information security, GDPR-aligned data handling, AI-led fraud and identity-verification controls.
- **Currency and geopolitical exposure from expanded footprint** — *Risk:* new operations such as Indian VACs in China and the Cyprus contract spanning Russia, South Africa, Kazakhstan and SE Asia extend exposure to jurisdictions with higher geopolitical and currency volatility than established Western European/North American operations. *Mitigation:* asset-light, leased-premises model limits capital at risk in any single country; diversified portfolio contains disruption in any one corridor.

## Note 2 — Basis of Preparation and Material Accounting Policies (pp.306–312+)

### 2.a Basis of preparation of financial statements
**(i) Compliance with Ind AS:** These financial statements comply in all material aspects with Indian Accounting Standards (Ind AS) notified under Section 133 of the Companies Act, 2013 (Companies (Indian Accounting Standards) Rules, 2015, as amended) and other relevant provisions of the Act.

**(ii) Basis of Consolidation:** The Group consolidates all entities it controls (power over entity, exposure/rights to variable returns, ability to affect returns). Entities controlled are consolidated from the date control commences until control ceases. Line-by-line consolidation; intra-group balances, transactions and unrealized gains/losses eliminated. Non-controlling interests (NCI) representing part of net profit/loss and net assets of subsidiaries not owned/controlled are excluded. Associates (significant influence but not control) are accounted using the equity method (initially at cost, adjusted for share of profit/loss). Results of subsidiaries acquired/sold during the year are consolidated from the effective date of acquisition up to effective date of disposal. For foreign subsidiaries, revenue items consolidated at average rate for the year; assets and liabilities converted at closing rates; exchange differences on consolidation recognized in the Foreign Currency Translation Reserve (FCTR). Difference between disposal proceeds and carrying amount of assets less liabilities on disposal is recognized in consolidated P&L. NCI's share of profit/loss and net assets is identified and presented in the consolidated financial statements.

**(iii) Basis of preparation:** Prepared under historical cost convention on accrual basis, except financial assets/liabilities measured at fair value at end of each reporting period. Accounting policies consistently applied except where a newly issued standard is adopted or a revision requires a change. Presented in Indian Rupees, rounded to nearest lakhs except number of shares, face value, earning per share where otherwise indicated.

**(iv) Functional & presentation currency:** Indian Rupee (₹) — company's functional and presentation currency.

**(v) Use of estimates:** Preparation requires management estimates/judgements affecting reported balances, disclosures of contingent liabilities and income/expenses. Estimates and underlying assumptions reviewed on an ongoing basis; revisions recognized in the period revised and future periods affected.

**(vi) Current & non current classification:** Assets/liabilities classified current/non-current as per normal operating cycle and Schedule III criteria. Operating cycle determined as twelve months. Deferred tax assets/liabilities classified as non-current.

### 2.b Material accounting policies
**(a) Revenue recognition:** Revenue from contracts with customers recognized when services are tendered at an amount reflecting expected consideration. Payments from customers for services rendered normally received within 30–60 days.
- *Sale of services and goods:* recognized when performance obligation satisfied by transferring promised service; amount and costs measurable reliably and no significant uncertainty over consideration. Measured at fair value of consideration received/receivable, net of returns, allowances and discounts. Revenue recognized upon transfer of control of promised products/services.
- *Sale of goods:* recognized at point in time when control transferred as per client agreement; measured based on transaction price adjusted for turnover discounts. Where discount level varies with revenue, liability recognized based on estimate of customer's future purchases; if discount criteria likely not met or amount not reliably estimable, discount not recognized until payment probable and estimable. Changes in estimated discount obligations recognized in period change occurs. Revenue excludes taxes collected.
- *Sale of service* includes commission income from online aggregation of financial products based on leads generated from its designated website.
- *Other income:* Interest income recognized on time-proportion basis (amount outstanding × applicable interest rate) under 'Other Income'; dividend income recognized when right to receive established (generally shareholder approval), under Other Income; rent income from sub-let of property recognized on accrual basis per sub-let agreement; profit/loss on sale of assets/investment recognized at time of sale of investment.

**(b) Property, plant and equipment:** Carried at cost of acquisition on current cost basis less accumulated depreciation and accumulated impairment, if any. Cost comprises purchase price and directly attributable cost of bringing the asset to working condition. Derecognized on disposal or when no future economic benefits expected. Gain/loss on derecognition (net disposal proceeds less carrying amount) included in P&L. **Depreciation on written down value (WDV) basis** over estimated useful lives. Useful lives:

| Class of assets | Useful life (years) |
|---|---|
| Building | 30 |
| Computer | 3 |
| Office equipment | 5 |
| Furniture and fixtures | 10 |
| Vehicles | 8 |
| Leasehold improvement | Lease term |

**(c) Intangible assets:** Software acquired or developed carried at cost less accumulated amortisation and impairment losses. Costs of maintaining software programmes expensed as incurred. Development costs directly attributable to design/testing of customised computer software applications recognized as intangible assets under development or intangible assets when ready for intended use, when: (a) technically feasible; (b) ability to use the software; (c) demonstrated probable future economic benefits; (d) adequate technical, financial and other resources to complete development and use available; and (e) expenditure during development reliably measurable. Amortisation period/method for finite-life intangibles reviewed at least at each reporting period end, adjusted prospectively.

**(d) Impairment:** Carrying amount of PPE, intangible assets and investment property reviewed at each balance sheet date. Asset treated as impaired when carrying cost exceeds recoverable value (higher of value in use and net selling price). Impairment loss recognized as expense, reversed if improvement in recoverable amount.

**(e) Financial instruments:** Any contract giving rise to a financial asset of one entity and financial liability or equity instrument of another.
- *Financial assets* include trade receivables, advances, security deposits, cash and cash equivalents. Initially measured at fair value; subsequently classified/measured at amortized cost, FVOCI, or FVTPL. Classification reviewed at each reporting period end.
- *Financial assets at amortized cost:* held to collect contractual cash flows of principal and interest; measured at amortized cost using effective interest rate (EIR) method; EIR amortization included as interest income; impairment losses recognized in P&L.
- *Financial assets at FVOCI:* held to collect contractual cash flows and held for selling; measured at fair value with all fair value movements recognized in OCI; interest income (EIR), impairment gain/loss and foreign exchange gain/loss recognized in P&L; cumulative gain/loss reclassified from OCI to P&L on derecognition.
- *Financial assets at FVTPL:* held for trading or measured neither at amortized cost nor FVOCI; measured at fair value with movements in P&L.
- *Investment in equity shares:* initially at cost. Subsequent fair value gain/loss recognized through P&L if held for trading; all other equity securities' fair value gains/losses recognized in OCI.
- *Investment in mutual fund:* initially at fair value, subsequently at FVTPL.
- *Investments in subsidiaries & joint ventures:* measured at cost less impairment; where indication of impairment exists, assessed and written down immediately to recoverable amount (higher of asset's fair value less costs of disposal and value in use). On disposal, difference between net disposal proceeds and carrying amounts recognized in P&L.
- *Impairment:* assessed at each reporting date (investments, trade receivables, advances, security deposits at amortized cost and financial assets at FVOCI); expected credit losses assessed and loss allowances recognized if credit quality deteriorated significantly since initial recognition.
- *De-recognition:* financial asset derecognized when contractual rights to cash flows expire or the rights to receive cash flows are transferred.
- *Financial liabilities:* recognized when Group becomes a party to contractual provisions; initially at fair value, subsequently at amortized cost. Borrowings, trade payables and other financial liabilities recognized at value of respective contractual obligations; any discount/premium on redemption/settlement recognized in P&L as finance cost using effective interest method. Trade and other payables represent liabilities for goods/services prior to year-end unpaid; unsecured, usually paid per credit period, presented as current unless payment not due within 12 months after reporting period.
- *Offsetting:* Financial assets and liabilities offset and net amount presented where legally enforceable right to offset and intention to settle net/simultaneously.

**(f) Fair value measurement:** Fair value is the price received to sell an asset or paid to transfer a liability in an orderly transaction between market participants at measurement date. Categorized into Level 1, 2, or 3 based on observability of inputs: **Level 1** — quoted prices; **Level 2** — valuation techniques maximizing observable market data, minimal entity-specific estimates; **Level 3** — one or more significant inputs not based on observable market data.

**(g) Leases:**
- *i) Where the Company is the lessee:* Right-of-use asset and corresponding liability recognized at the date the leased asset is available for use. Lease payments apportioned between finance charges and reduction of lease liability to achieve constant rate of interest on remaining balance. Finance charges recognized in finance costs unless directly attributable to qualifying assets (then capitalized). Contingent rents expensed in period incurred. Leased asset depreciated over useful life; if no reasonable certainty of obtaining ownership by end of lease term, depreciated over shorter of useful life and lease term. Right-of-use assets generally depreciated over shorter of useful life and lease term on straight-line basis. If reasonably certain to exercise a purchase option, right-of-use asset depreciated over underlying asset's useful life. Short-term leases (≤12 months) and low-value asset leases recognized on straight-line basis as expense.
- *ii) Where the Company is the lessor:* Leases classified as finance or operating. Finance lease when substantially all risks/rewards incidental to ownership transferred to lessee; all other leases operating. For operating leases, rental income recognized on straight-line basis over relevant term.

**(h) Employee benefit:**
- *i) Short-term obligations:* expensed on basis of amount paid/payable for period services rendered.
- *ii) Post-employment obligations:* defined benefit plans comprise gratuity and provident fund.
  - *a. Provident fund:* contributions to statutory provident fund per EPF & Miscellaneous Provisions Act, 1952 (defined contribution plan); company contributions paid/payable recognized as expense.
  - *b. Gratuity:* net liability/asset = present value of defined benefit obligation at period end less fair value of plan assets. Defined benefit obligation calculated annually by actuaries using projected unit credit method; present value determined by discounting estimated future cash outflows by reference to market yields at period end on government bonds with terms approximating the obligation. Net interest cost calculated by applying discount rate to net balance of obligation and fair value of plan assets; included in employee benefit expense. Remeasurement gains/losses (experience adjustments and changes in actuarial assumptions) recognized in period incurred directly in OCI; included in retained earnings in statement of changes in equity and balance sheet.
  - *iii. Defined benefit plans:* cost determined using actuarial valuations; assumptions include discount rate, future salary increases, mortality rates and attrition rate; sensitive to changes.
- *iv) Share-based payments:* Equity-settled share-based payments to employees measured at fair value of employee stock options at grant date; fair value estimated using the Black-Scholes option pricing model, taking into account terms and conditions. Fair value at grant date amortised over the vesting period, based on estimate of equity instruments that will eventually vest, with a corresponding increase in equity. At each reporting period end, the Company revises its estimate of the number of equity instruments expected to vest.

**(i) Income tax:** Income tax expense is tax payable on current period's taxable income (applicable rate) adjusted by changes in deferred tax assets/liabilities attributable to temporary differences and unused tax losses.
- *Current tax:* calculated on tax laws enacted/substantively enacted at period end. Management periodically evaluates positions in tax returns subject to interpretation and considers whether a taxation authority will accept an uncertain tax treatment; establishes provisions where appropriate on expected amounts payable. Current tax assets/liabilities offset only if the group has a legally enforceable right to set off, and intends to settle net or realize/settle simultaneously.
- *Deferred tax:* provided in full using the liability method on temporary differences between tax bases and carrying amounts. Determined using tax rates enacted/substantively enacted by period end expected to apply when the deferred tax asset is realized/liability settled. Not recognized for: (i) temporary differences on initial recognition of assets/liabilities in a transaction that is not a business combination and affects neither accounting nor taxable profit/loss; and (ii) temporary differences related to investments in subsidiaries, associates and joint arrangements to the extent the group can control timing of reversal and it is probable they will not reverse in the foreseeable future. Deferred tax asset recognized to the extent probable future taxable profits available; reviewed at each reporting date, reduced/reversed as appropriate. Measured at tax rates expected to apply when temporary differences reverse. Offset only if legally enforceable right and relate to income taxes levied by same taxation authority on same taxable entity.

**(j) Cash & cash equivalents:** Cash on hand and balances with banks readily convertible to known amounts of cash, and short-term highly liquid investments with original maturities of three months or less, subject to insignificant risk of changes in value.

**(k) Inventories:** Finished Goods valued at lower of cost and net realisable value (NRV = estimated selling price less estimated completion costs and costs necessary to make the sale). Write-downs to NRV and abnormal losses recognized as expense; reversals reduce the inventory expense in the period of reversal.

**(l) Provisions, contingent assets & contingent liabilities:** Provisions recognized when the Group has a present legal/constructive obligation from past events, probable outflow of resources and reliable estimate. Provision made for onerous contracts where unavoidable costs exceed expected economic benefits. Provisions reviewed at each balance sheet date and adjusted to current best estimate; measured at present value of management's best estimate of expenditure required. Contingent liabilities disclosed where possible obligation or present obligation that may not require outflow or cannot be ascertained with reasonable certainty; no provision/disclosure where likelihood of outflow is remote.

**(m) Foreign currency transactions:** Functional and presentation currency is Indian Rupee. Transactions in foreign currency accounted at exchange rate on transaction date. Gains/losses on settlement and translation of monetary items recognized in P&L. Exchange differences on monetary items forming part of the group's net investment in a foreign operation (functional currency other than INR) accumulated in FCTR.

**(n) Cash flow statements:** Reported using the indirect method; profit adjusted for non-cash transactions, deferrals/accruals of past/future operating cash receipts/payments, and income/expense associated with investing or financing cash flows. Cash flows from operating, investing and financing segregated. All highly liquid investments readily convertible to known amounts of cash treated as cash equivalents.

**(o) Segmental information:** (i) Operating businesses organized/managed separately according to nature of products and services, each segment a strategic business unit. Geographical segments based on areas in which major operating divisions operate. (ii) Unallocated items include general corporate income/expense not allocated to any business segment. (iii) Segment information prepared in conformity with accounting policies adopted for the consolidated financial statements as a whole.

**(p) Business combination:** Per Ind AS 103, accounted using the acquisition method when control is transferred. Consideration measured at fair value at acquisition date; net identifiable assets acquired at fair value. Goodwill tested annually for impairment. Any gain on a bargain purchase recognized in OCI and accumulated in equity as capital reserve if clear evidence of underlying reasons, otherwise directly in equity as capital reserve. Acquisition-related costs expensed as incurred. Contingent consideration measured at fair value at acquisition date; if an obligation meeting the definition of a financial instrument is classified as equity, not remeasured subsequently and settlement accounted within equity; other contingent consideration remeasured at fair value at each reporting date with changes in P&L. Goodwill initially measured at cost (excess of consideration transferred over net identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed); subsequently at cost less accumulated impairment; tested annually or more frequently if indicators. Business combination achieved in stages: previously held equity interest remeasured at acquisition-date fair value with gain/loss in P&L or OCI. **Common control business combinations** accounted using the pooling of interest method; assets/liabilities reflected at carrying amounts, no fair value adjustments, no new assets/liabilities recognized. Property acquisitions and business combinations: management considers substance to determine whether an acquisition represents a business; if not, cost allocated between identifiable assets and liabilities based on relative fair values; no goodwill or deferred tax arises.

**(q) Earning per share:** Basic EPS = net profit attributable to equity holders ÷ weighted average number of equity shares outstanding. Diluted EPS = net profit attributable to equity holders ÷ weighted average shares (including dilutive potential equity shares). Shares and potential dilutive shares adjusted retrospectively for share splits and bonus issues.

## Note 3 — Significant Accounting Judgments, Estimates and Assumptions (pp.314–315)
In applying accounting policies, management made the following estimates/assumptions/judgments with significant effect:
- **(a) Income taxes:** judgment required for provision for income taxes and deferred tax assets/liabilities; carrying amount of deferred tax assets reviewed each balance sheet date; factors used may differ from actual outcome.
- **(b) Contingencies:** judgment required estimating possible outflow of resources, if any, for contingencies/claims/litigations; not possible to predict outcome of pending matters with accuracy.
- **(c) Allowance for uncollected accounts receivable and advances:** trade receivables carry no interest, stated at normal value reduced by allowances for estimated irrecoverable amounts. Individual trade receivables written off when management deems not collectible. Impairment on ECL (present value of cash shortfall over expected life).
- **(d) Fair value measurement of financial instruments:** where fair value cannot be based on quoted prices in active markets, measured using valuation techniques (e.g. discounted cash flow model). Inputs taken from observable markets where possible; degree of judgment required for liquidity risk, credit risk and volatility.
- **(e) Defined benefit plans:** cost and present value determined using actuarial valuations involving assumptions (discount rate, future salary increases, mortality/attrition rates); sensitive to changes.

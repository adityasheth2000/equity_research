# Groww (Billionbrains Garage Ventures Ltd) — Investment Verdict

**Ticker:** NSE: GROWW / BSE: 544603 · **Date:** 28 August 2026 · **CMP:** ~₹192 (52-wk range ₹112–227)

---

## 1. What the company does

Groww (legally **Billionbrains Garage Ventures Ltd**, "BGV") is India's largest **retail-focused digital investing app**. Think of it as a smartphone one-stop-shop where ordinary people — largely first-time investors — put their savings to work. It is not a bank and not an old-style broker; it is a software company that makes money by charging small fees every time a customer buys, sells, holds, or borrows against an investment.

The product suite, in order of revenue contribution:

| Product | What it is | Where it sits |
|---|---|---|
| **Equity derivatives (F&O)** | Options/futures trading on stocks & indices | Broking (GIT) |
| **Equities (cash)** | Buying/selling stocks directly | Broking (GIT) |
| **Commodity derivatives** | Gold, silver, crude, etc. futures/options | Broking (GIT) |
| **Float income** | Interest on idle client cash held with the broker | Broking (GIT) |
| **MTF** | Margin trading — lending clients money to buy stocks | Broking (GIT) |
| **Personal loans + LAS** | Unsecured personal loans & loans against securities | NBFC (Groww CreditServ) |
| **Mutual fund distribution** | Direct-plan MFs & SIPs (the historic core business) | Platform |
| **Groww AMC** | Own mutual funds/ETFs | Asset management |
| **Wealth management** | Fisdom, "W" (HNI), "Prime" (mass-affluent) advisory | Platform |

**Who the customers are:** ~12.75–13 million active NSE clients — more than any other broker — plus ~1.7 crore active platform users, ~81% of whom live in Tier-2/3 cities. Most are first-time retail investors.

**How Groww makes money:** It is a **fee-for-transaction** business, not an asset-management fee business. Brokerage (F&O + cash + commodities) is **~75% of revenue**; the rest is float/interest income, MTF interest, lending, and a small (still loss-making) AMC + wealth piece. The group's broking subsidiary (Groww Invest Tech, "GIT") alone contributed **~94% of FY25 revenue** — this is the core engine. The listed entity BGV is the holding company; the operating value sits in its 100%-owned subsidiaries.

## 2. Company overview & industry positioning

- **Scale leadership:** #1 broker in India by NSE active clients for **11 consecutive quarters (since Q2 FY24)**; ~**29% market share** (Jun 2026), up from ~26% a year earlier. Closest peer Zerodha is ~7.9M clients vs Groww's ~13M.
- **Market-share momentum:** Incremental share is higher than average share across every segment — MF SIP inflows **14.1%**, stocks cash ADTO **15.1%**, equity derivatives premium ADTO **11.0%**, MTF **2.7%** (all rising).
- **Structural tailwind:** India has only ~50M active stock/MF investors out of ~70–80M total — roughly **5% of the adult population**. Management sees **3–4x growth over the next decade** as financialisation deepens.
- **The diversification bet:** The story is shifting from a pure F&O brokerage (equity derivatives were 68% of income in Q2 FY25, now ~52%) toward float income, MTF, commodities, lending, and wealth — each of which is more recurring/less volatility-dependent.
- **Corporate history:** Incorporated 2018 as a subsidiary of Groww Inc. (Delaware); reverse-merged to become the holding company in Q4 FY24; **IPO Nov 2025** (₹100 issue; raised ₹6,632 Cr = ₹1,060 Cr fresh + ₹5,572 Cr OFS; listed 12 Nov 2025).

## 3. Key metrics to track (and why they matter)

| # | Metric | Latest | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **NSE active-client market share** | ~29% | The franchise value. Share gains = future fee revenue with zero marginal cost (users acquired once, monetised for years). A reversal here is the earliest bear signal. |
| 2 | **Revenue mix — % from equity derivatives** | ~52% (was 68%) | Concentration risk. F&O income is volatile and regulatorially exposed. A falling % means a more durable, higher-quality earnings base; a rising % re-introduces cyclicality. |
| 3 | **Total customer assets + net inflows** | ₹3.6 L Cr AUM, ~₹23,000 Cr/quarter net inflows | The "float" and future cross-sell pool. Drives float income today and AMC/wealth AUM tomorrow. |
| 4 | **MTF book & yield** | ₹3,775 Cr, ~14.95% | Highest-margin balance-sheet product; adding ₹600–700 Cr/quarter. Watch the spread between 14.95% and funding cost, plus NPAs. |
| 5 | **Loan book (GCS) & LAS mix** | ₹1,396 Cr; LAS 34% of disbursements | Credit is the swing factor for consolidated ROE — it deploys the IPO cash but adds credit risk. Watch credit cost (~₹70 Cr on ~₹1,250 Cr book in FY25). |
| 6 | **MF SIP inflows + AMC AUM** | SIP ₹13,229 Cr/quarter (14.1% share); AMC AUM ₹5,491 Cr | The structural, recurring revenue engine. AMC + wealth are the "next S-curves" that de-risk the brokerage dependence. |
| 7 | **EBITDA margin / PAT margin** | ~65% / ~47% (Q1 FY27) | Operating leverage. With ~90% fixed costs and variable cost ~10%, margin is a direct function of revenue growth (>15% → margin expands). |
| 8 | **ROE / ROCE & capital deployment** | ROE 28.8%, ROCE 37.3%, D/E 0.03 | Groww has ~₹9,650 Cr net worth and negligible debt; how profitably it deploys this (MTF/LAS vs. idle cash) determines whether ROE stays ~25–30% or drifts down. |

## 4. Management track record — has management walked the talk?

**Founders:** Lalit Keshre (CEO), Ishan Bansal (CFO), Harsh Jain (COO), Neeraj Singh (CTO) — all ex-Flipkart; ~27.8% combined promoter stake.

**Hits (delivered):**
- **Built the #1 broker from scratch in ~9 years** and held the #1 NSE active-client slot for 11 straight quarters — the single most important promise, kept.
- **Navigated brutal regulation** (SEBI true-to-label + Nov 2024 F&O curbs, which cut industry F&O participation ~50%) while *still growing* market share — an outcome most peers failed to match.
- **Commodities:** promised "fair share sooner than MTF"; hit **~28% retail market share (notional ADTO)** in under a year.
- **MTF:** committed to "₹600–700 Cr/quarter additions" — delivered for three+ straight quarters; book grew ~4x YoY.
- **IPO executed cleanly** (Nov 2025) and **credit rating upgraded** AA- → AA (ICRA, Jul 2026) + fresh CARE AA (Apr 2026) — a rare double positive.
- **Float/MTF-driven diversification** — derivatives share fell 68% → 52% exactly as guided ("will go below 50%").

**Misses / unproven (watch these):**
- **No numerical guidance discipline:** management repeatedly declines AUM/wealth/revenue targets ("too early"), giving little to hold them accountable against.
- **Fisdom acquisition (Oct 2025, ~₹929 Cr)** is still in gestation — profitability promised only by **FY28**, not yet evidenced; revenue run-rate actually *fell* (~₹40 Cr → ~₹29 Cr/quarter) during integration.
- **Groww AMC needs AUM to grow 5–6x** to reach profitability — a multi-year, unproven path; State Street's ~23% stake (~₹580 Cr) helps credibility but the outcome is pending.
- **PAT volatility from one-offs:** Q2 FY26 revenue fell 9% YoY (regulatory drag); Q3 FY26 reported PAT fell 28% YoY (provision-reversal base effect) — headline profit has been noisy even as underlying trends improve.
- **CAC is rising** (~₹900 → ₹1,374 → ~₹1,900 per new transacting user), partly IPL-driven, but still an unaddressed trend.

**Net assessment:** strong operators with a proven compounding playbook, but they under-communicate forward numbers — so the burden of proof sits on the *next few quarters of actuals*, not on guidance.

*FY24 loss of ₹805 Cr was largely a one-off (reverse-merger / non-operating) — operating profit was ₹743 Cr. EPS is distorted by the FY25/FY26 capital restructuring and IPO (share count rose to ~627 Cr shares); per-share figures before the IPO are not comparable.*

- **Scale economics are exceptional:** revenue compounded ~59%/yr (3-yr) with OPM expanding from 27% (FY24) to ~62% (FY26/TTM). Variable cost is only ~10% of the base — the rest is fixed — so incremental revenue drops almost straight to EBITDA.
- **Balance sheet is fortress-like:** net worth ~₹9,650 Cr, **debt/equity 0.03**, negligible borrowings, and ~₹3,000+ Cr unencumbered cash/liquid investments. CARE's adjusted tangible net worth ₹7,530 Cr (Dec 2025). This funds MTF/LAS/AMC without leverage.
- **Cash-flow nuance:** CFO is lumpy (₹-962 Cr FY25, ₹-21 Cr FY26) because the business parks growing client float and margin capital with exchanges — a *feature of the model*, not a red flag, but it means reported PAT is not free cash yet.
- **Quality of earnings is improving but still concentrated:** GIT = ~94% of revenue; equity derivatives = ~52% of income; ~75% of revenue is capital-markets-linked. The margin expansion story is real, but the *durability* story is still being built.

## 7. Strategy, growth outlook & management quality

**Four FY27 priorities (management):** (1) scale wealth, (2) compound core broking share, (3) AI inflection (productivity + customer experience), (4) launch new product "S-curves".

- **Wealth (the big bet):** Fisdom (bank partnerships), "W" (affluent/HNI), "Prime" (mass-affluent) launched in H2 FY26. Management calls wealth "a mammoth" product. Genuinely early — combined Fisdom + AMC is **<2% of revenue** today.
- **AMC + State Street:** State Street Global Advisors (~$5.5Tn AUM) to take ~23% of Groww AMC for ~₹580 Cr — SEBI + CCI approved Mar–Apr 2026, completion expected by Sep 2026. Brings global/passive product capability.
- **International:** US-stock trading via GIFT City licensed, launching "soon" (no firm date).
- **New products:** bonds (scaling well), commodity derivatives, LAS, "915" terminal, AI advisory ("MF Prime").
- **Management quality:** disciplined product philosophy ("pull-based", no perverse sales incentives, ~80% organic acquisition), capital-light, and highly profitable at scale. Weakness: aversion to quantitative guidance.

**Outlook synthesis:** The next 2–3 years are a transition from a high-margin but cyclical F&O brokerage to a broader, more recurring wealth + lending + AMC franchise. The bull case is that this raises revenue quality and justifies the premium multiple; the bear case is that the new engines are small, unproven, and will take years (and capital) to move the needle.

## 8. Thesis vs Anti-thesis

| Thesis (bull case) | Anti-thesis (bear case) |
|---|---|
| **NSE active-client share holds >28% or rises** → sustains >15% revenue growth, keeps fixed-cost operating leverage flowing → EBITDA margin stays ≥60%, PAT compounds >30%. *Why it matters:* share is the raw material for every fee line; a drop breaks the compounding engine. | **NSE active-client share stalls below ~27%** as Zerodha/Angel One/Upstox respond → revenue growth falls below ~15%, and with ~90% fixed costs, EBITDA margin stalls/contracts. *Why it matters:* at ~50x earnings the stock is priced for >20% growth; growth below ~15% de-rates it. |
| **MTF book scales ₹600–700 Cr/quarter to >₹6,000 Cr by FY27-end** at ~15% yield with credit costs <1% → adds ~₹600–900 Cr high-margin interest income and lifts float yields +1–2pp/quarter → boosts EBITDA margin and ROE. *Why it matters:* MTF + float are the highest-ROCE use of the ₹9,650 Cr net worth; success reverses ROE dilution. | **MTF/LAS credit losses spike** (a market correction + margin calls) → NPA hit to P&L, slower book growth, and rating agencies flag "prudent credit costs" as the key monitorable. *Why it matters:* a ~2% credit loss on a ₹6,000 Cr MTF book is ~₹120 Cr — ~5% of PAT — and would cap ROE expansion. |
| **Wealth + AMC become visible:** Fisdom turns breakeven by FY28 and Groww AMC AUM 5–6x's to ~₹25,000+ Cr → a durable, recurring second engine that cuts derivatives share below ~45%. *Why it matters:* diversifies away from cyclical brokerage and justifies a structural (not cyclical) multiple. | **New businesses stay sub-scale and dilutive:** Fisdom misses FY28 breakeven; AMC stalls; combined contribution stays <3% of revenue → the stock remains a one-trick (brokerage) story priced like a platform. *Why it matters:* every quarter the AMC/wealth drag (₹200–300 Cr annual losses) directly trims consolidated EBITDA margin. |
| **Regulatory tailwind/stability:** no new SEBI F&O curbs and RBI's Jul 2026 capital-market-lending rules raise the barrier for undercapitalised rivals → Groww's scale + capital become a moat. *Why it matters:* regulation has historically consolidated share toward the strongest capitalised players — Groww. | **Regulatory shock:** a fresh SEBI curb on derivatives (e.g., expiry-day margin hikes, position limits) or restrictions on broking-float/lending economics → immediate hit to the ~75% capital-markets revenue base. *Why it matters:* the Nov 2024 changes cut F&O penetration from ~18% to ~10%; a repeat would hit the top line directly. |
| **Float income keeps compounding** as client assets grow (₹3.6 L Cr and ~₹23,000 Cr/quarter inflows) and rate/yield tailwind persists → a rising, annuity-like income stream. *Why it matters:* float is near-pure margin (no credit risk) and reduces earnings cyclicality. | **Capital-markets downturn / sustained FII selling** → client-asset mark-to-market losses and weak inflows compress float, MTF demand, and fee volume simultaneously. *Why it matters:* ~75% of revenue is market-linked; a prolonged bear market reverses the operating leverage as sharply as the bull market built it. |

### What would change my mind

- **Bull case invalidation:** NSE active-client market share declining for **two consecutive quarters**, or revenue growth dropping below ~15% for two quarters — that breaks the operating-leverage thesis underpinning the ~50x multiple.
- **Bear case invalidation:** derivatives revenue share falling **below ~45%** while total revenue keeps growing >20% (proving the diversification is real and self-sustaining), and/or MTF + LAS scaling with credit costs <1% — that converts the story from cyclical broker to durable platform.

## 9. Risks (including credit-rating trajectory)

**Credit-rating trajectory (positive):**
- ICRA: **[ICRA]AA- (Stable) Nov 2025 → [ICRA]AA (Stable) Jul 2026** (upgrade on sustained market leadership, ~29% share, strong capitalisation, healthy profitability).
- CARE: **CARE AA; Stable** (assigned Apr 2026).
- Subsidiaries: GIT **[ICRA]AA (Stable)/A1+**; GCS (NBFC) **[ICRA]A (Stable)/A1** (CARE A+).
- Rating agencies' stated **downgrade triggers**: consolidated gearing >2.5x (sustained), material market-share deterioration, asset-quality deterioration, or an adverse capital-markets/regulatory event. None are active — but they are the tripwires to monitor.

**Key risks:**
1. **Revenue concentration:** ~75% of income is capital-markets-linked; GIT = 94% of revenue. A market downturn hits the top line and operating leverage simultaneously.
2. **Regulatory risk (SEBI/RBI):** F&O curbs, the RBI Feb-2026 capital-market-intermediary lending directions (effective Jul 2026 — fully collateralised lending, higher haircuts, restricted proprietary funding), and any new derivatives rules.
3. **Credit risk:** rapid MTF (₹3,775 Cr, 4x YoY) and LAS scaling into what could be a late-cycle market.
4. **Competition:** Zerodha, Angel One, Upstox, Paytm Money; discount-broker price wars.
5. **Valuation:** trailing P/E ~49–50x and P/B ~12x vs peers at ~10–39x and sector P/B ~4–5x — a de-rating risk if growth falters.
6. **Overhang:** early VC investors (Peak XV, Ribbit, Sequoia) selling large block deals (₹5,326 Cr + ₹5,637 Cr in May 2026; Ribbit ₹1,914 Cr more recently) — persistent secondary supply; anchor lock-up expiry Feb 2026.
7. **Execution breadth:** many simultaneous new ventures (commodities, wealth, AMC, US stocks, bonds, LAS) with a flat ~1,800 headcount.

## 11. Key monitorables (next 2–4 quarters)

1. **Q2/Q3 FY27 revenue growth** — does it stay >15–20% (needed for margin expansion), or does the Q1 FY27 plateau extend?
2. **Derivatives revenue share** — does it break below ~45–50% as guided, proving diversification?
3. **MTF book run-rate** — is ₹600–700 Cr/quarter sustained, and do credit costs stay <1%?
4. **LAS + personal-loan credit quality** — any uptick in NPAs or provisioning as the book matures?
5. **State Street AMC deal completion (by Sep 2026)** and post-deal AUM trajectory.
6. **Fisdom progress toward FY28 breakeven** (watch the quarterly revenue run-rate).
7. **NSE active-client share** — any quarterly share loss is the earliest leading indicator.
8. **Block-deal overhang** — further VC/secondary selling pressure.
9. **Regulatory developments** — SEBI derivatives rules and the practical impact of RBI's Jul-2026 lending directions.
10. **AMC AUM + MF SIP market share** — the leading indicators of the "next S-curve" actually working.

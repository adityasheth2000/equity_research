## Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its long-term rating on the bank facilities of OSEL Devices Limited (OSEL) at 'Crisil BBB' while revising the outlook to 'Positive' from 'Stable'. The revision reflects the expected improvement in the business risk profile. Revenue grew by 41% year-on-year in fiscal 2025 to Rs 186 crore, driven by volume growth and strong diversified clientele. Operating margins remained stable at 17-18% in fiscal 2025, driven by better absorption of fixed costs. The rating also factors in a strengthened financial risk profile following the IPO in September 2024 and a subsequent preferential issue in September 2025, which improved networth to Rs 108 crore as of March 31, 2025. However, the profile is partially offset by exposure to technological obsolescence, raw material price fluctuations, and a large working capital requirement, evidenced by receivables increasing to 128 days in fiscal 2025.

## Key Rating Drivers — Strengths

*   **Extensive promoter experience and improving scale:** Promoters have ~20 years of experience in the healthcare equipment industry. Operating income has grown at a healthy CAGR of 42% over the three fiscals through 2025.
*   **Product diversification and partnerships:** The company has a mix of hearing aids (~54% revenue) and LED display systems (~46% revenue). A partnership with Philips for feature phones is expected to strengthen the business risk profile.
*   **Strong clientele:** Base includes both private and government customers, supporting revenue growth.
*   **Healthy financial risk profile:** Networth is expected to improve to Rs 190-195 crore by March 31, 2026, driven by reserve accretion and warrant issuance. Gearing is expected to remain below 0.5 times.
*   **Strong interest coverage:** Interest coverage ratio is expected at 7-8 times in fiscal 2026.
*   **Capital expenditure discipline:** No major debt-funded capital expenditure is expected, contributing to financial risk improvement.

## Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses

*   **Technology obsolescence and raw material risk:** Intense competition requires rapid product innovation, creating obsolescence risk. Key raw materials are procured from China, exposing the company to price volatility and supply chain disruptions.
*   **Large working capital requirement:** Operations are working capital intensive. Gross Current Assets (GCAs) stood at 350 days as of March 31, 2025 (up from 219 days the previous year). Receivables increased to 128 days from 73 days. GCAs are expected at 370-380 days as of March 31, 2026.

## Liquidity

*   **Assessment:** Adequate.
*   **Cash Accruals:** Expected net cash accrual of Rs 30-35 crore per annum, sufficient to cover working capital requirements and yearly term debt obligations (Rs 0.5-1.0 crore).
*   **Cash & Bank Balance:** Rs 32 crore as on September 30, 2025.
*   **Bank Limit Utilisation:** Averaged 77% for the 12 months through September 2025.
*   **Current Ratio:** Expected at 2.1 times as on March 31, 2026.

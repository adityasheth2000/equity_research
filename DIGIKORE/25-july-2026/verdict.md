# Digikore Studios Ltd (DIGIKORE) — Verdict

**Date:** 25 July 2026 | **CMP:** ₹50.60 | **Market Cap:** ₹64 Cr | **P/E:** 5.07 | **BV:** ₹37.6

---

## What the Company Does

Digikore Studios is a visual effects (VFX) studio based in Pune, India. Think of it as a digital special-effects workshop for Hollywood. When you watch a Marvel movie, a Netflix series, or a big-budget film and see explosions, alien worlds, or impossible stunts — someone had to create those shots frame by frame on a computer. Digikore is one of those "someones."

**Core VFX Services:** The company takes raw footage from film studios (Marvel, Netflix, Amazon, Warner Bros., CBS, HBO, Hulu) and adds computer-generated visual effects — compositing, rotoscopy, match-moving, clean-up, camera tracking. This is 98% export-driven, with clients almost entirely in the US and Europe.

**How they make money:** Hollywood studios outsource the labor-intensive VFX work to lower-cost locations like India. Digikore bids for projects, assigns a team of artists, and gets paid on a per-project or milestone basis. Think of it like a specialised IT services company, but for film effects instead of software code.

**Beyond VFX:** The company has been building three other lines of business, though none are material yet:
- **Branded Content:** Producing and owning original shows (*Kaise Banta Hai*, *Heroes*) for OTT platforms (JioHotstar) — this is an IP-ownership bet where they could earn recurring revenue from syndication and YouTube monetisation.
- **AI SaaS:** Products like *iMadeASong* (AI brand jingles), *SelfSync.ai* (AI hiring assessments), and *MaxiPitch* (AI outbound sales for MSMEs). Currently generating peanuts — jingles at ₹3-3.5 lakhs/week, self-serve tools at early stages.
- **Virtual Production Assets:** Selling 3D assets on Unreal Engine's `fab.com` marketplace. Run-rate: ₹5-6 lakhs/month.

## Key Metrics to Track

1. **Revenue Growth & Guidance Credibility:** Management has a track record of setting ambitious targets and missing badly. FY25 guided at ₹70 Cr → delivered ₹36 Cr. FY26 originally projected at ₹125 Cr in Nov 2024, revised to ₹60-65 Cr by Jun 2025, actual ₹66 Cr. Watch H1 FY27 closely — guidance is ₹75-100 Cr but base effect from FY26 H1 (₹36 Cr) is easily comparable.

2. **Debtor Days & Cash Flow:** The single biggest red flag. Debtor days exploded from 20 (FY22) → 190 (FY24) → 401 (FY25) → 370 (FY26). Operating cash flow has been negative for 3 straight years: -₹17 Cr (FY24), -₹22 Cr (FY25), -₹8 Cr (FY26). Free cash flow: -₹24 Cr, -₹30 Cr, -₹10 Cr. The company is making accounting profits but bleeding cash — trade receivables at ₹46 Cr (Sep 2025) against FY26 full-year revenue of ₹66 Cr means nearly 70% of annual revenue is tied up in receivables.

3. **Pledged Promoter Shares:** 86.5% of promoter holding (58.46% stake) is pledged. This is an extreme level and a major governance red flag. Management says QIP proceeds will reduce this by Feb 2026, but as of screen date, it hasn't happened yet.

4. **Borrowing Levels:** Total borrowings rose from ₹9 Cr (FY24) → ₹40 Cr (FY25) → ₹55 Cr (FY26). Debt/Equity at 1.16. Interest cost doubled YoY in FY25 (₹3 Cr) and doubled again in FY26 (₹7 Cr) — now consuming nearly half of operating profit. On a full-year FY26 profit of ₹15 Cr PBT, interest alone is ₹7 Cr.

5. **Direct vs Outsourced Revenue Mix:** Direct work (billing studios directly) offers better margins and faster collections than outsourced/intermediated work. Direct share was 20% in FY25, rose to 50% in H1 FY26. Target 80% by FY27. This is the single most important operational metric — it drives both margins and cash conversion.

6. **Operating Margin (OPM %):** Has been volatile: 8% (FY22) → 16% (FY23) → 28% (FY24) → -13% (FY25) → 33% (FY26). The FY26 OPM of 33% appears to be an outlier driven by the post-strike demand surge. Normalised PAT margins on VFX work are 16-18% per management.

7. **Segment Diversification Progress:** The AI/content/IP bets are interesting but currently tiny. Branded content contributed ~₹10-12 Cr in FY26 (15-18% of revenue). AI SaaS and VP assets combined under ₹2 Cr. The core VFX business is still 80%+ of revenue. Track whether these new verticals actually scale or remain PPT slideware.

## Management Track Record

**Misses:**
- **FY25 Revenue Guidance:** Guided ₹70 Cr in Nov 2024 investor presentation (with detailed CMAs projecting ₹75 Cr for FY26). Actual: ₹36 Cr. Miss of 49%. Blamed on Hollywood strikes — but management was making ₹70 Cr guidance *after* the strikes had already ended (Nov 2024).
- **FY26 Revenue Guidance:** Originally ₹125 Cr (Oct 2024 PPT) → cut to ₹125 Cr still (Nov 2024) → vaguely maintained → ₹60-65 Cr by Jun 2025 (down 50%). Actual: ₹66 Cr. Ballpark met the *revised* guidance, but the original was a gross overstatement.
- **Jio Cinema Deal:** Announced as 58 episodes, ~₹30 Cr revenue by Mar 2025. Shrunk to ~40 episodes (1-2 shows cancelled during Jio-Hotstar merger). Revenue pushed to FY26. Actual branded content contribution ~₹10-12 Cr — far below initial claim.
- **Rights Issue:** Announced ₹25 Cr rights issue, board meeting scheduled, then cancelled abruptly. Management's explanation: "We didn't want to fool investors and take money before announcing a loss." Now replaced with QIP ₹45 Cr + promoter warrants ₹20 Cr.
- **"100% Capacity" Statements:** Management told investors the company was running at 100% capacity in Feb 2025. Later admitted this was true only on a *reduced base* — the company had laid off ~100 people, so 100% of a smaller team.
- **Transparency:** Multiple investors directly confronted management on the Jun 2025 concall about lack of updates between reporting periods. Management acknowledged and committed to quarterly reporting and more regular updates. To their credit, they did start quarterly reporting from FY26.

**Hits:**
- **Survived the strike:** ~150 VFX studios globally declared bankruptcy during the strike downturn (including Technicolor, one of the world's largest). Digikore survived — though not without significant pain.
- **FY26 turnaround delivered:** After a disastrous FY25 (₹-7 Cr PAT loss), FY26 delivered ₹13 Cr PAT on ₹66 Cr revenue — a genuine recovery.
- **Direct work share up:** From 20% to 50% in one year. This is structural improvement if sustained.
- **Cost optimisation:** Monthly salary bill reduced from ₹3 Cr to ₹2 Cr with same output — genuine operational improvement.
- **Bonus issue completed:** 1:1 bonus, signalling confidence (though this is cosmetic, not fundamental).

**Verdict on Management:** Abhishek More (MD) comes across as a passionate entrepreneur who genuinely understands the VFX business and the AI threat. However, he has a clear pattern of overpromising — projecting hockey-stick revenue growth that doesn't materialise. His investor communication oscillates between defensive ("only listed companies in India are making profits in VFX") and candid ("we've become more grounded... taking baby steps"). The 86.5% pledged promoter shares is the most serious trust deficit. Approach guidance with heavy scepticism.

## Strategy, Growth Outlook & Management Quality

**Near-term Thesis (6-12 months):** The post-strike VFX recovery is tangible. Order book at ₹20 Cr+, bidding pipeline ₹35 Cr+. Europe expansion is the key incremental lever — historically US-heavy (~100%), targeting Europe = US in revenue mix. New York subsidiary being established. If FY27 delivers ₹75-100 Cr with improving cash conversion, the stock at 5x earnings and 1.36x book could re-rate.

**Medium-term Thesis (1-3 years):** The diversification into branded content IP, AI SaaS, and VP assets is directionally smart — VFX services will face existential AI disruption. But there is almost no evidence yet that these new verticals can scale. Brand jingles at ₹3-4 lakhs/week and fab.com at ₹5-6 lakhs/month are rounding errors on a ₹66 Cr revenue base. The "VFX-to-Tech pivot" narrative is currently more story than substance.

**Long-term Risk:** AI disruption to VFX is real and acknowledged by management. If AI tools reduce the labour required per VFX shot by 90% (management's own estimate: 50-artist tasks becoming 5-artist tasks), the entire outsourcing model — Digikore's core competitive advantage of lower-cost Indian labour — gets compressed. US studios are already contractually banning generative AI use, buying time, but the clock is ticking.

## Risks

1. **Pledged Shares & Governance:** 86.5% of promoter holding pledged is the single biggest risk. Even if management says QIP proceeds will clear this, until it happens, it's a severe overhang. A stock price decline could trigger margin calls.

2. **Cash Flow / Receivables Impairment:** ₹46 Cr in trade receivables against a ₹66 Cr revenue base. Debtor days at 370. If even 15-20% of these turn bad (post-strike, mid-tier studios did go bankrupt), it could wipe out a full year of PAT. Management says they don't foresee bad debts — but they also didn't foresee FY25's 100% loss.

3. **Guidance Credibility:** Management has a demonstrated pattern of overpromising and underdelivering. FY25 miss was 49%. FY26 original guidance was cut by 50%. FY27 guidance of ₹75-100 Cr should be discounted heavily.

4. **AI Disruption to Core VFX:** This is existential for the VFX outsourcing model. Management is building AI tools (self-disruption), but there's no guarantee they succeed. The graveyard of companies that saw disruption coming but couldn't pivot is deep.

5. **Client Concentration:** Top 5 clients = 30% of revenue (FY25), down from 45% in FY23 — improving but still significant for a small company. Loss of even one major studio client would be material.

6. **Borrowing & Interest Burden:** ₹55 Cr borrowings, ₹7 Cr interest in FY26. Interest coverage ratio: 3.36x on PBT basis, but on a cash basis, far weaker. If rates stay high or revenue dips, debt service becomes challenging.

7. **Credit Ratings:** No credit rating reports available for Digikore — consistent with an SME company with high pledged shares and recent losses. The absence of formal ratings is itself a data point.

## Summary

Digikore is a genuine VFX company with real client relationships (Disney, Marvel, Netflix, CBS, HBO) and an improving post-strike trajectory. FY26 was a solid recovery year — ₹66 Cr revenue, ₹13 Cr PAT, 33% OPM. FY27 H1 should be at least comparable to H1 FY26 (₹34-36 Cr).

**However**, the investment case has serious structural concerns:

1. **Cash flow is terrible.** Three years of negative CFO. Cumulative -₹64 Cr FCF against ₹16 Cr PAT. Trade receivables at 70% of annual revenue. This is not a company that converts profit to cash.

2. **Promoters have pledged 86.5%** of their holding. Governance is the single biggest risk factor — until this is reduced, everything else is secondary.

3. **Management has burned investor trust** with repeated guidance failures, selective disclosures, and defensive responses to tough questions. The "we've become more grounded" narrative is only as good as the next few quarters' delivery.

4. **AI disruption to core VFX** is a known unknown. Management is aware and building hedges (AI SaaS, branded content IP), but these are tiny today and unproven at scale.

At ₹50.6 (P/E 5, P/B 1.35), the stock is cheap on reported earnings but expensive on free cash flow (which is negative). The market is pricing in: (a) risk of receivables turning bad, (b) governance discount for pledged shares, and (c) a "show me" premium post repeated guidance misses.

This is a high-risk, potential-high-reward situation suited only for investors who can (a) track it closely quarter by quarter, (b) stomach extreme volatility, and (c) believe the VFX recovery + diversification story will eventually convert to cash. For most investors, it's a "wait for proof of cash generation + pledge reduction" before committing capital.

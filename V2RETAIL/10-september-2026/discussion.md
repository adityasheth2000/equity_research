# Discussion Points

## Can V2 Retail Fund Its Hyper-Aggressive Store Expansion?

Management's funding plan is plausible, but the next phase of expansion is not currently funded purely by free cash flow.

### Guidance and cash requirement

- Store-opening guidance has accelerated from 100 stores in FY26 to 170-200 stores in FY27. Management has also discussed 250 stores in the following year if store-level returns remain strong.
- Store investment has increased from approximately ₹2.3-2.5 crore per store in August 2025 to ₹2.7-2.8 crore in May 2026, including fit-out capex and inventory. At that level, 170-200 stores imply gross investment of roughly ₹460-560 crore before additional warehouse and infrastructure spending.
- Inventory must be procured around two months before a store opens. Management indicated that opening 50-55 stores requires ₹100-150 crore of inventory to be held in advance.

### Management's funding stack

1. **Internal accruals and EBITDA:** Management says FY27 store additions can be funded through internal accruals and existing cash.
2. **Release of vendor prepayments:** The ₹400 crore QIP allowed V2 to prepay vendors, including approximately ₹300 crore mentioned on the February 2026 call. Management now expects to restore creditor days from the unusually low level created by prepayments, releasing approximately ₹150-200 crore of cash.
3. **Bank working-capital limits:** Management is negotiating higher bank limits and has said additional debt would be its first preference for future expansion rather than another QIP.
4. **QIP bridge capital:** The QIP was allocated approximately ₹135 crore to debt repayment, ₹165 crore to working capital and ₹100 crore to general corporate purposes, including expansion, warehouses and technology.

### The key concern

The latest investor presentation shows that FY26 operating cash flow was **negative ₹103 crore**, investing cash flow was **negative ₹219 crore**, and inventory rose to **₹1,142 crore** from ₹558 crore. Financing cash flow was positive ₹319 crore, meaning the expansion and working-capital build were funded through external capital and balance-sheet facilities despite reported profitability.

Therefore, management's plan depends on three assumptions working together:

- stores continue to become EBITDA-positive from the first month;
- inventory normalises toward 90-100 days; and
- creditor days can be restored toward 45-60 days without materially weakening supplier relationships or gross margins.

The most important funding lever is the reversal of vendor prepayments, not current free cash generation. This can release ₹150-200 crore, but it also removes some of the gross-margin benefit from early-payment discounts. In short, V2 can probably fund the next phase without another equity raise if operating accruals remain strong and banks expand working-capital limits, but the 170-200-store plan is not yet proven to be self-funded on a clean free-cash-flow basis.

**Sources:** V2 Retail concall transcripts dated May 2025, August 2025, November 2025, February 2026, May 2026 and August 2026; Q1 FY27 investor presentation dated August 2026.

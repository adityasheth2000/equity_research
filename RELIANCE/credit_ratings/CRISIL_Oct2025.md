## Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its Long-Term 'Crisil AAA/Stable' and Short-Term 'Crisil A1+' ratings on the existing debt instruments and bank loan facilities of Reliance Industries Limited (RIL). The ratings factor RIL's leadership position and strong competitiveness in the Oil-to-Chemical (O2C) business, alongside its leadership in the telecom (Reliance Jio Infocomm Ltd) and retail segments (Reliance Retail Ventures Ltd). Exceptional financial flexibility supports these strengths.

In Fiscal 2025, operating profit was driven by strong growth in digital services and retail, offset by challenges in the O2C segment due to falling product cracks. However, the O2C business showed a strong rebound in the first six months of Fiscal 2026 due to favorable margins on domestic fuel retail and improvements in polypropylene and PVC deltas. The company reported ~15% year-on-year growth in operating profits and 8% revenue growth in the first half of FY'26. Consolidated EBITDA for the six months ended September 2025 stood at Rs 99,467 crore. The ratings are constrained by intense competition in retail, technological changes in telecom, and cyclicality in the O2C/E&P segments.

---

## Key Rating Drivers — Strengths

*   **Leadership in O2C Business:**  
    *   RIL is amongst the top global petrochemical manufacturers.  
    *   World's largest integrated producer of polyester, 3rd largest paraxylene (PX), and top five in PTA and polypropylene.  
    *   Accounts for nearly **50%** of total domestic cracker (ethylene) capacity and >50% share in polymers (PE, PP, PVC) market in India.  
    *   World's largest single-site refinery (**1.4 mbpd**) with a Complexity Index of 21.1.  
    *   Feedstock flexibility via ethane transportation facilities and RoGC projects.  

*   **Strong Market Position in Retail:**  
    *   India's largest retailer by revenue, scale, and profits.  
    *   Network expanded to **19,821 stores** as on September 30, 2025 (strong tier-2 and tier-3 presence).  
    *   JioMart serves over 3,000 stores across 1,000+ cities (5,000 pin codes).  
    *   Over **4 million merchant partner base** following integration of Metro Cash and Carry.  

*   **Sustained Telecom Leadership:**  
    *   Wireless subscriber base of **47.9 crore** (Market share ~41% as of Aug 31, 2025).  
    *   Highest spectrum footprint in India: **26,801 MHz** (Uplink + Downlink).  
    *   Extensive network of telecom towers and optical fibre.  
    *   Growing ARPU driven by 5G adoption and data usage.  

*   **Exceptional Financial Flexibility:**  
    *   Ability to raise resources from capital markets at competitive rates.  
    *   Strong debt protection metrics and stable profitability.  
    *   Supported by unutilised bank lines and robust cash flow.  

---

## Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses

*   **Susceptibility to Crude Oil Price Volatility:**  
    *   Feedstock prices and product prices can be adversely affected by geopolitical tensions, tariffs, and supply chain disruptions.  
    *   O2C segment profitability is cyclic and vulnerable to global demand fluctuations.  

*   **Regulatory and Technological Risks in Telecom:**  
    *   Structurally dynamic industry susceptible to regulatory/policy changes.  
    *   High Capex cycles (8-10 years) for technology upgrades (e.g., 5G network investment).  

*   **Intense Competition in Retail Sector:**  
    *   Competition from organized retailers and unorganized sector.  
    *   Susceptible to adverse macroeconomic environments and the rise of "quick commerce" participants.  

*   **Gestation and Technology Risk in New Energy:**  
    *   Significant investments in new energy (Green Giga Complex, 100 GW target by 2030).  
    *   Exposed to technology risks as a relatively new entrant; no dominant technology platform has emerged.  

---

## Liquidity

*   **Liquidity Assessment:** Superior and robust.  
*   **Cash & Liquid Investments:** Consolidated cash and liquid investments stood at **Rs 229,685 Crore** as on September 30, 2025.  
*   **Bank Lines:** Sizeable bank lines which usually remain moderately utilised.  
*   **Coverage:** Cash accruals are adequate to meet maturing debt and a portion of the Capex requirement.  
*   **Operational Cash Flow:** Strong cash flow from operations aids in meeting Capex requirements.

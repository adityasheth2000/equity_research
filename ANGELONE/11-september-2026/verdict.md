# Angel One Ltd (NSE: ANGELONE) — Investment Verdict
**Date of analysis:** 11 September 2026 | **CMP:** ₹297 | **Market Cap:** ₹27,138 Cr | **P/E:** 26.3x | **Book Value:** ₹67.2 | **ROE (TTM):** 15.6% (5yr avg 29.4%)

*All post-split figures (1:10 split, record date 26 Feb 2026, face value ₹10→₹1) unless noted. Sources: FY25 & FY26 Annual Reports, Q3FY26/Q4FY26/Q1FY27 investor decks and concall transcripts, Screener.in financials, and web research on analyst/industry/regulatory context. Page/slide references are preserved from source review where useful for verification.*

## 1. What the business actually is

Angel One is India's largest listed pure-play discount stockbroker (38.6M platform users, 20.2% retail equity turnover share, 16.7% demat market share as of June 2026), built on a "broking-as-a-front-door" flywheel: acquire retail investors cheaply at scale, then cross-sell margin trading (MTF), mutual fund distribution, wealth management (Ionic Wealth), asset management (Angel One AMC), lending distribution, and — most recently — life insurance (JV with LivWell Holdings). Management's own framing, repeated across every quarter reviewed, is that non-broking revenue is now ~40% of the business and rising, explicitly designed to reduce dependence on volatile F&O brokerage income.

This is a real and measurable strategic shift, not just marketing: gross broking revenue share has fallen from 68% (Q4FY24) to ~60% (Q1FY27), while interest income (from the MTF book) has risen from ~19% to ~33% of gross revenue over the same period. The company is transitioning from a pure transaction-fee model to one increasingly reliant on a levered balance sheet (client funding book grew from ₹14.8Bn in FY24 to ₹51.3Bn in FY26 — a 3.5x increase in two years).

## 2. Financial track record — a long compounding history now inflecting

| Metric | FY15 | FY20 | FY23 | FY25 | FY26 | TTM (Jun'26) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sales (₹Cr) | 450 | 748 | 3,002 | 5,239 | 5,138 | 5,426 |
| Net Profit (₹Cr) | 47 | 82 | 625 | 1,172 | 915 | 1,032 |
| OPM % | 25% | 24% | 43% | 38% | 35% | 37% |
| ROCE % | – | 14% | 44% | 26% | 15% | – |

- **10-year sales CAGR 28%, profit CAGR 40%** — a genuine long-run compounder by the numbers.
- But the **trend has clearly inflected**: 3-year profit CAGR is just **1%**, TTM profit growth **4%**, and ROCE has fallen from a **44% peak (FY23) to 15% (FY26)** — the sharpest deterioration in the company's listed history, driven by (a) regulatory compression of F&O revenue and (b) a shift of the balance sheet toward a lower-return, more capital-intensive lending model (MTF book).
- **ROE**: 5yr average 29.4%, but last year (FY26) just **15.6%** — again reflecting the trough.
- **Leverage has roughly doubled**: Debt/Equity 0.60x (FY25) → 1.28-1.30x (FY26); gearing ratio 31.75%→55.65%. This funds MTF book growth but structurally changes the risk profile of a business that was previously close to debt-free.
- **Operating cash flow turned sharply negative**: -₹4,142Cr in FY26 (CFO/OP -209%), vs -₹2,023Cr in FY25 — driven by MTF book and client-margin float growth, not underlying cash burn, but it means reported PAT is no longer a good proxy for owner cash flow while the lending book scales.
- **Dividend policy**: Consistent high payout (~35-45% historically, spiking to 246% payout in FY26 on a mix of interim dividends + lower base-year EPS); FY26 saw a 1:10 stock split (Feb 2026) plus continued quarterly-ish interim dividends (₹23, ₹1.75, ₹1/share across recent quarters) — a genuine, sustained capital-return signal even through a down-earnings year.

**Read:** the reported 10-year growth story is real, but an investor today is buying at an inflection point where the old, high-ROCE, asset-light broking annuity is being diluted by (a) regulation eating into F&O economics and (b) a deliberate pivot toward a more capital-intensive, lower-margin lending and wealth-management model whose payoff is multi-year out.

## 3. The core competitive problem: losing share to Groww, not just an industry-wide slowdown

This is the single most important fact surfaced by web research that is almost entirely absent from Angel One's own investor communications:

- **NSE active-client market share, FY26**: Angel One fell from **15.4% to 14.8%**; Zerodha fell from 16.0% to 15.1%; **Groww rose from 26.3% to 28.3%** and is now the largest player by active clients. Total NSE active clients industry-wide *fell* ~7% (4.92Cr → 4.57Cr) — the first industry contraction in years.
- **Groww IPO'd in November 2025** at a ~$8.9Bn valuation (₹79,547 Cr) — roughly **3x Angel One's entire market cap** — with FY25 revenue ₹3,900Cr and PAT ₹1,800Cr, i.e., already larger and more profitable than Angel One on a standalone basis, and growing faster with reportedly lower marketing spend per client.
- On Angel One's own Q3FY26 earnings call, an analyst directly challenged management: *"one of our competitors, which was recently listed... [is] gaining market share at a much faster pace and also spending less on marketing... do you see any gaps in our strategy?"* — management's answer cited long history and modest YoY cash-share gains but **did not engage with the "spending less, growing faster" framing**, nor name the competitor. This pattern of soft, non-quantified answers to direct competitive challenges recurs across multiple quarters (Q4FY26: an analyst called flat market share "stagnation"; Q1FY27: another analyst noted "the leader has become more active" in active-client terms).
- A telling anecdotal data point from the Q1FY27 customer-service segment of the earnings call: a long-time retail customer complained on the call itself that Angel One imposes its own "Restricted Basket" trading limits beyond SEBI requirements — unlike Zerodha, Upstox, or Groww — and said this drove him to move holdings to Zerodha. Management apologized but did not commit to removing the restriction.
- MTF pricing: a Citi analyst noted "players with deep pockets" (implicitly Groww) undercutting Angel One on margin-funding rates; management's response was that it is "at the right pricing point" with no plan to reprice — a confident, possibly complacent stance given a named competitive threat.

**This is the central bear-case data point for the multibagger thesis**: Angel One's own materials frame its trajectory almost entirely around regulation and industry-wide volume softness, but the more uncomfortable truth in the data is that a well-funded, listed competitor (Groww) is taking share in the core client-acquisition metric, and management's public responses to this have been consistently soft rather than a clear, numbers-backed rebuttal.

## 4. Regulatory headwinds — real, quantified, and only partly cyclical

Multiple, compounding regulatory changes have structurally reduced F&O brokerage economics since mid-2024:
- **STT hike (Jul 2024)**: F&O sell-side transaction tax raised ~60%.
- **Expiry-day rationalization**: NSE/BSE moved to single weekly index-options expiries, estimated to cut F&O-linked broker earnings 15-30%.
- **"True-to-label" charge disclosure mandate (Oct 2024)**: eliminated a revenue line that contributed 4.3% of FY25 total income, "fully extinguished" per the FY26 annual report (p.85) — a real, already-realized, permanent headwind, not transient.
- **Jane Street ban (Jul 2025)** and the broader SEBI crackdown on index-options manipulation: index options volumes on NSE+BSE fell >50% in FY26; Angel One's Q1FY26 (Jun 2025) revenue fell 19% YoY and PAT fell 61% YoY as a direct result — the single sharpest quarterly hit in the review period.
- **New cash-margin upstreaming rule (effective Oct 2025)**: forced brokers to upstream client margin cash to clearing corporations, increasing working-capital borrowing; quantified net EBDAT impact of **₹70mn in Q3FY26 alone**, management repeatedly calling it "transient" pending a "software fix" — worth checking whether this actually resolved (a promise made but not obviously fulfilled in later quarters reviewed).
- **RBI capital-market lending curbs (effective Jul 2026)**: banks must now lend to brokers on a fully secured basis with a 40% minimum haircut on equity collateral, and are barred from funding proprietary trading — an early July 2026 data point showed a ~40% fall in MCX options premium ADTV in the first days of implementation. This is a fresh, live risk not yet fully reflected in the Q1FY27 print (quarter ended June 2026, just before implementation) and is one of the more under-discussed forward risks in the company's own materials.

Management's standing narrative — that FY26 was the trough and each of these shocks is "transient" or already absorbed — is *partially* supported by the sequential EBDAT margin recovery (21.8% → 44.4% normalized margin, Q1FY26 to Q1FY27), but the RBI curb lands after that recovery was reported, so the "normalization is complete" story is not yet fully tested against the newest regulatory shock.

## 5. The diversification bet: real progress, but still a net drag, and uneven

Per-entity FY26 consolidated profit contribution (from the annual report, Note 55, Schedule III):

| Entity | FY26 PAT (₹mn) | FY25 PAT (₹mn) | Trend |
|---|---|---|---|
| Angel One Ltd (standalone broking) | +10,227 | +12,160 | Core engine, still >100% of consolidated profit |
| Angel Fincap + Angel Financial Advisors (lending/NBFC) | +176 | +52 | Small but scaling profitably |
| Angel One Wealth Ltd (Ionic Wealth) | (121) | (214) | Loss narrowing YoY, but structurally still 3-4 yrs from breakeven per management's own guidance |
| Angel One AMC | (128) | (124) | Loss essentially flat — AUM only ₹6.2Bn after ~2 years, stalled by management's own admission |
| AOWL step-down subsidiaries (Investment Services, Investment Managers & Advisors) | (642) + (259) = **(901)** | (96) + (163) = (259) | **Losses more than tripled YoY** — the single largest and fastest-growing drag in the consolidated P&L |

**Net effect:** the AMC + Wealth complex (including its step-down subsidiaries) lost roughly **₹1,150mn in FY26**, up from ~₹1,196mn combined the prior year but with a much worse trajectory at the AOWL step-down entities specifically. This is being funded entirely by the highly profitable core broking business. Management is explicit that Wealth's breakeven is 3-3.5 years out from launch (not from now) and has committed **up to ₹1.5Bn of fresh capital each** into Wealth and the NBFC/credit platform.

The AMC business drew unusually pointed, unresolved analyst skepticism in the Q1FY27 call — a Motilal Oswal analyst explicitly noted "massive churn in top management" (the AMC CEO, Hemen Bhatia, resigned June 2026; a Chief Product Officer group-wide, Ankit Rastogi, also departed August 2026) and that AUM has "hardly scaled" — management's response (products only fully rolled out in Q4FY26, "too early to judge") did not address the leadership-churn point directly.

Meanwhile, **credit distribution (personal loans via partner NBFCs) has declined for three consecutive quarters** (₹7.1Bn → ₹6.1Bn → ₹5.3Bn, Q3FY26 to Q1FY27), candidly attributed by management to "lender underwriting and pricing" and funnel friction — a genuine admission that this specific growth engine isn't working smoothly, even as the company's own MTF (on-balance-sheet margin funding, a different product) continues to grow strongly.

**Bottom line on diversification:** it is real and directionally supports the long-term thesis (interest income and distribution income are structurally larger shares of revenue than 2-3 years ago), but the newer, equity-story-defining pieces (AMC, Wealth, credit distribution) are collectively a drag today, with mixed and in some cases worsening trends, not a clean "flywheel already working" narrative.

## 6. Governance — mostly sound, with a recurring pattern of small compliance lapses

**Positives:** Independent-majority board (5 of 9 directors) with full attendance; zero promoter share pledge; unqualified audit opinion in both FY25 and FY26 (auditor: S.R. Batliboi & Co./EY network, single Key Audit Matter — IT systems controls, no red flags); very low related-party transaction volumes (<0.3% of purchases/sales); zero disclosed data breaches; declining customer complaint volumes; clean secretarial audit.

**Flags worth carrying into ongoing monitoring:**
- **Recurring SEBI/NSE enforcement actions**: at least 4-5 separate matters across FY25-FY26 — NSE penalty (₹5.74 lakh, margin-reporting errors), SEBI settlement (₹34.57 lakh, algo-platform/API "guaranteed returns" association), SEBI adjudication (₹3 lakh, unauthorized terminals/AP supervision), a further SEBI settlement (₹4.28 Cr, May 2026, AP-monitoring lapses — the largest of these, per web research), and a 2025 SEBI settlement scheme payment. Individually immaterial in rupee terms, but the *frequency* across authorized-person supervision, margin/collateral reporting, and terminal management suggests recurring operational-control gaps in a business where regulatory trust is close to the core moat.
- **A reversed corporate restructuring**: the Board approved (Aug 2023), then withdrew (Feb 2025) after NSE returned the application, a scheme to transfer the core broking business to subsidiaries — roughly 18 months of effort abandoned. A second restructuring attempt (a new BTA approved May 2025) was also **withdrawn again in January 2026**. Two consecutive reversed restructuring attempts is a genuine execution-quality/strategic-clarity flag worth probing with management directly.
- **Large, volatile executive compensation via ESOPs**: Group CEO Ambarish Kenghe's FY26 total pay was ₹682mn, driven almost entirely by a large one-time ESOP charge (₹555mn) tied to his CEO appointment — a significant outlier against a year of declining consolidated profit and multiple regulatory settlements. Overall ESOP expense has risen from ₹628mn (FY24) to ₹973mn (FY25) to ~₹1,680mn (FY26, standalone) — a rising dilution/comp cost trend materially outpacing revenue growth.
- **Leadership churn**: CEO transition completed only in March 2025 (Kenghe appointed after a ~22-month gap following the prior CEO's May 2023 resignation); AMC CEO resigned June 2026; group CPO resigned effective August 2026. For a company mid-pivot into new business lines, this level of senior turnover in exactly the units driving the diversification thesis (AMC, product) is a real monitorable.
- **Rising attrition**: overall permanent employee turnover rose from 9.3% (FY24) to 16.0% (FY26).
- **Gender pay gap**: KMP-level median female remuneration is less than a quarter of median male remuneration despite a stated "gender pay parity assessment" — outcome not quantitatively disclosed.

None of these are solvency- or fraud-level concerns — audit opinions are clean and contingent liabilities are dominated by routine exchange-margin bank guarantees, not real litigation exposure — but collectively they describe a company with real, if modest, control-maturity friction, not the "few remaining nits" typically seen in a best-in-class governance profile.

## 7. Valuation and peer context

- CMP ₹297, P/E 26.3x trailing, P/B ~4.4x (CMP/book value ₹67.2).
- Peer comparison (Screener, Sep 2026): **Billionbrains (Groww)** trades at P/E 50x with market cap ₹1,22,021 Cr (4.5x Angel One's) and stronger recent quarterly sales growth (66% QoQ vs Angel One's low-single-digit/negative QoQ prints in recent quarters) — the market is already pricing Groww at a large premium for its growth and share-gain trajectory, which is the flip side of the market-share risk in Section 3.
- Sell-side (public web sources only — could not independently verify Jefferies/CLSA/Nomura/Kotak/ICICI Securities/Nuvama-specific notes, a genuine research gap): consensus target ~₹352-369 (implying ~20-25% upside from CMP), predominantly "Buy"-rated (Motilal Oswal reaffirmed Buy, ₹280 post-split target after Q1FY27 results, based on a 21x FY28E EPS framework). One tracked service noted analysts cutting FY26 revenue estimates by ~24% over the review period and lowering targets, reflecting the regulatory/volume headwinds above.
- At 26x trailing earnings for a business whose 3-year profit CAGR is 1% and whose core client-acquisition metric is losing share to a larger, faster-growing, better-capitalized listed peer, valuation is not obviously cheap — it is pricing in a recovery and successful diversification that the last two years of data only partially support.

## 8. Multibagger potential — what would have to be true

For Angel One to be a genuine long-term multibagger from here, several things would need to happen simultaneously:
1. **F&O/regulatory headwinds genuinely stabilize** (plausible — True-to-Label and expiry-rationalization impacts are now largely lapped; the RBI curb from July 2026 is the newest unknown).
2. **Active-client/market-share erosion versus Groww halts or reverses** — the single most important, least-addressed risk in management's own communications. Nothing in the documents reviewed shows this happening yet.
3. **Wealth, AMC, and credit distribution move from cash-burning/stalled to profitably scaling** within the 3-4 year horizon management itself has set — plausible given the underlying addressable market (India's wealth-management and MF industries are genuinely large and growing), but AMC in particular looks stalled today and credit distribution is actively declining.
4. **Leverage stabilizes rather than continuing to compound** — the MTF-funded balance sheet model works well while collateral values hold and funding costs stay contained, but converts Angel One from an asset-light annuity business into something closer to a leveraged NBFC-like risk profile; a sharp equity-market drawdown would hit collateral value and funding costs simultaneously.
5. **Governance/compliance friction (SEBI/NSE actions, leadership churn) doesn't escalate** into something that damages the regulatory trust the business depends on.

None of these are implausible, and the underlying secular tailwind — rising Indian retail participation in financial markets, still-low household financial-asset penetration, growing SIP/MF culture — is genuine and well-documented in the FY26 MD&A. But the base rate here looks more like "a structurally advantaged incumbent defending share and re-rating modestly if execution goes well" than "a company with a clear, underappreciated path to 5-10x," given that its most direct listed comparable (Groww) is already larger, growing faster, and commanding a materially higher multiple for doing so.

## 9. Verdict

**Not a clear multibagger case today; better characterized as a fair-to-moderately-attractively-priced compounder-in-transition, with a real and currently unresolved competitive threat.**

The bull case (diversified revenue, huge SIP/demat/financialization tailwind, disciplined capital return, clean audit history, cheaper-than-peer valuation) is genuine and evidence-backed. But the bear case is not a one-line regulatory story that management's "transient" framing suggests — it is a combination of (a) demonstrable, multi-quarter active-client share loss to a larger and faster-growing listed competitor that management has not directly rebutted with data, (b) a diversification strategy where the newest, most equity-story-relevant pieces (AMC, wealth step-downs, credit distribution) are losing more money or declining, not less, (c) leverage and cash-flow characteristics that have changed materially and unfavorably in just two years, and (d) a pattern of recurring, if individually small, compliance lapses and reversed strategic decisions that raise modest execution-quality questions.

**Recommended posture:** Watchlist / small starter position rather than high-conviction multibagger allocation. The next 2-3 quarters of data — specifically (i) whether NSE active-client share stabilizes relative to Groww, (ii) whether AMC AUM and credit distribution volumes inflect positively, (iii) how the RBI capital-market lending curb (effective July 2026) flows through Q2FY27 results, and (iv) whether leverage/gearing growth decelerates — should meaningfully clarify which of the two narratives (structural re-rating vs. slow share erosion in a maturing, increasingly capital-intensive business) is playing out.

## 10. Key open questions / monitorables for the next review
1. Angel One's NSE active-client market share trend vs. Groww and Zerodha over the next 2-3 quarters — the single most important number missing from a clean bull case.
2. Whether the "transient" cash-upstreaming finance-cost drag (flagged Q3FY26) and the "transient" MII-related client reimbursement (flagged Q4FY26) actually resolved, or recurred.
3. AMC AUM trajectory and whether a permanent CEO is appointed following Hemen Bhatia's June 2026 resignation.
4. Credit distribution volumes — a fourth consecutive quarterly decline would meaningfully weaken the "AI-driven underwriting" narrative management has offered.
5. Q2FY27 (Sep 2026 quarter) results as the first full quarter reflecting the RBI capital-market lending curb (effective July 2026).
6. Whether debt/equity and gearing continue to rise, and at what point (if any) management signals a ceiling on balance-sheet leverage for the MTF book.
7. Progress (or further reversal) of the twice-abandoned corporate restructuring, and whether it signals unresolved tax/regulatory/strategic uncertainty.
8. Independent confirmation of institutional (Jefferies/CLSA/Nomura/Kotak/ICICI Securities/Nuvama) target prices and theses — not available via public web search in this review and worth a terminal-based pull for a fuller valuation cross-check.

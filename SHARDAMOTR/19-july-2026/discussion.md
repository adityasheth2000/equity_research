## 1. Donghee Subframe RFQ Wins — In Simple Terms

**Who is Donghee?**
Donghee Industries Co. Ltd. is a large Korean Tier-1 auto parts manufacturer with $2 billion in revenue (CY2024), 17 plants, and 6 R&D centres across South Korea, Europe, China, USA, and Mexico. They supply to global OEMs including Hyundai, Kia, Volkswagen, and Stellantis.

**What is the Donghee-Sharda deal?**
In October 2025, Sharda Motor signed a **Technology Licensing Agreement (TLA)** with Donghee. Under this agreement, Donghee transfers design drawings, process know-how, and validation protocols to Sharda. Sharda will locally manufacture **control arms, torsion beams, and subframes** for Indian and global OEM platforms.

**What is a subframe?**
A subframe is a rigid metal frame bolted underneath the car body. It holds the engine, suspension, and steering components together. Think of it as the "skeleton" beneath the car. It is larger, heavier, and pricier than the control arms Sharda currently makes.

**What does this mean for content per vehicle?**
- Current Sharda content per vehicle (suspension): Rs 2,000 - 8,000
- Post-Donghee target content per vehicle: Rs 6,000 - 18,000
- Additional content from subframes + torsion beams alone: Rs 4,000 - 10,000 per vehicle

This is a potential 3x expansion in revenue per car from the suspension portfolio.

**What is an RFQ?**
RFQ = "Request for Quote." When an OEM (like Maruti, Tata, Mahindra, Hyundai) needs a new part, they ask qualified suppliers to submit price quotes. The supplier who wins the RFQ gets the business for that platform — typically for 5-7 years (the life of the vehicle platform).

**Has Sharda won any Donghee subframe RFQ yet?**
No. As of the Q4 FY26 concall (May 2026), management confirmed they are in the "business development and engagement process" with OEMs. These are longer-gestation products with higher engineering and validation requirements. Commercial scale-up will happen "progressively over multiple quarters."

**Why is this the most important catalyst to watch?**
- First subframe RFQ win would validate the Donghee TLA thesis
- It would unlock 2-3x content per vehicle potential
- It would prove Sharda can move beyond control arms into higher-value structural components
- It would signal that OEMs are accepting Sharda as a full-suspension-system partner, not just a control-arm supplier

**Timeline:** Management has not given a specific date. Their language suggests 2-4 quarters of business development before first wins materialise. Watch FY27 concalls closely.

## 2. BS7 Notification — In Simple Terms

**What is BS7?**
BS = "Bharat Stage" — India's emission norms for vehicles. Currently India is at BS6 (equivalent to Euro 6). BS7 is the next stage — tighter pollution limits that vehicles must meet to be sold legally.

**Has BS7 been announced?**
Not yet. The government has not officially notified BS7. Sharda's management said in the Q4 FY26 concall: "We have started readiness work by benchmarking European developments and engaging with customers on future system requirements." They expect the notification "soon" but no firm date.

**What changes with BS7?**
- **More content per engine:** Exhaust systems become more complex — additional catalysts, more sensors, larger after-treatment systems. Every exhaust Sharda sells becomes pricier.
- **Market consolidation:** Smaller suppliers without R&D and testing capabilities will drop out. Tier-1 suppliers like Sharda (with in-house R&D, 125+ engineers, patented technology) gain share.
- **Mirrors Euro 7:** India typically follows European emission norms with a lag. Euro 7 standards provide a template for what BS7 might look like.

**Why does it matter for Sharda?**
BS7 is a revenue catalyst. Every exhaust system Sharda manufactures will carry higher content (more revenue per vehicle) without proportional cost increase. The market consolidation aspect also widens Sharda's share of a growing pie.

**How big is the opportunity?**
Management has not quantified it because the exact BS7 norms are not yet notified. They said the quantum of content increase "depends on Indian OEM engine strategies." European experience suggests meaningful step-up in content per engine.

**Timeline:** Unknown. Watch for government gazette notification. Once notified, OEMs and suppliers typically get 2-3 years to comply.

## 3. CAFE III Implementation — In Simple Terms

**What is CAFE?**
CAFE = "Corporate Average Fuel Efficiency." Instead of regulating tailpipe emissions from individual vehicles (which is what BS norms do), CAFE regulates the **average fuel consumption of all the vehicles a manufacturer sells in a year.**

If Maruti sells 1 lakh cars in a year, the average CO2 emissions across all those cars (small, big, petrol, diesel, CNG, EV) must be below a government-set target. If the average is too high, the manufacturer pays penalties or cannot sell certain models.

**What is CAFE III?**
- Phase: April 2027 to March 2032 (FY28 to FY32)
- Target: ~91.7 g/km CO2 (down from ~130 g/km in Phase 1, ~113 g/km in Phase 2)
- After CAFE III: CAFE IV (post-2032) targets 70 g/km — even tighter

**How do OEMs comply with CAFE III?**
OEMs have multiple levers:
1. Sell more EVs (highest credits)
2. Sell more hybrids (partial credits)
3. Sell more CNG, ethanol, flex-fuel vehicles
4. **Make vehicles lighter** (less weight = less fuel = lower CO2)
5. Improve engine efficiency

**Why is CAFE III a structural tailwind for Sharda?**
- **Lightweighting demand:** OEMs *must* reduce vehicle weight to hit CAFE III targets. Sharda's core lightweighting products (control arms, subframes, torsion beams, links) directly enable weight reduction.
- **Multi-fuel framework:** CAFE III supports hybrids, CNG, ethanol, flex-fuel, and lightweighting technologies. Management noted: "Except for pure EVs, all other powertrains continue to require engineered emission systems." So Sharda wins on both sides — lightweighting for everyone, exhausts for non-EVs.
- **Platform standardisation:** OEMs will standardise platforms across multiple powertrains (ICE, hybrid, CNG). This creates scale advantages for suppliers like Sharda who can supply across platforms.

**How big is the content-per-vehicle impact?**
Management has said they intend to add approximately Rs 4,000 to Rs 10,000 per vehicle in content increase due to portfolio enhancement (control arms + subframes + torsion beams together). This is on top of the current Rs 2,000-8,000 range.

**Timeline:** CAFE III becomes effective April 1, 2027. The demand for lightweighting components will accelerate from FY28 onwards.

## 6. The Single Most Important Variable

**Does OPM stabilise at 12-12.5% in FY27?**

- If YES: The base case or bull case plays out. Margin stabilisation confirms the compression was transitory (startup costs, catalyst pass-through optics, new vertical investments). Re-rating follows.
- If NO (OPM falls below 11%): The bear case becomes more likely. Margin compression is structural (competitive pricing pressure, raw material squeeze, lower-margin lightweighting mix growing faster than high-margin exhausts).

**First read:** Q1 FY27 results, expected around August 2026. This will be the most important data point for the stock in the near term.

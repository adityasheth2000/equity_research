# Credit Rating Report: Power Finance Corporation Limited (PFC)

## Rating Action
*   **Agency:** Crisil Ratings Limited
*   **Date of Issue:** July 06, 2026
*   **Subject:** Update on Power Finance Corporation Limited (PFC) and REC Limited (REC)
*   **Key Event:** Merger of REC into PFC by way of absorption.
*   **Rating Impact:** No impact on the credit quality of PFC. The ratings continue to factor in support from the Government of India (GoI).
*   **Total Facilities Rated:** Not explicitly quantified in this text; however, the report covers all instruments including bank loans, bonds, debentures, commercial paper, and other debt securities as per the regulatory annexure.

## Detailed Rationale
On June 28, 2026, the Boards of Directors of Power Finance Corporation Limited (PFC) and REC Limited approved a merger where REC will be absorbed into PFC with a share exchange ratio of 88 equity shares of PFC for every 100 equity shares of REC. This strategic move aims to create India's largest power sector financier, enhancing balance sheet strength, capital efficiency, and operational synergies. As of March 31, 2026, the consolidated advances of both entities stood at Rs 11.6 lakh crore.

Crisil Ratings has determined that the merged entity will remain a Government Company with the GoI retaining majority voting rights and control. Consequently, the agency will continue to factor in sovereign support, resulting in no downgrade or upgrade to the current credit quality of PFC. The rating rationale considers the strong market position, adequate capitalization, diversified resource profile, and inherent vulnerability regarding asset quality due to borrower credit risk profiles and sectoral concentration.

## Key Rating Drivers — Strengths
*   **Sovereign Support:** The entities act as nodal agencies for several Government of India (GoI) backed schemes, playing a critical role in financing India's power sector. The GoI retains majority voting rights and control in the merged entity, ensuring continued government backing.
*   **Market Position:** Post-merger, the entity is positioned as the **largest power sector financer in India**.
*   **Scale and Capitalization:** On a standalone basis (as of March 31, 2026), gross advances for both PFC and REC were approximately Rs 5.8 lakh crore each. Consolidated advances reached Rs 11.6 lakh crore.
*   **Operational Synergies:** The merger is expected to improve capital efficiency and enable large-scale funding across the entire power sector value chain.
*   **Diversified Resource Profile:** Both entities possess a diversified mix of resources and a strong track record in the financial sector.

## Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses
*   **Asset Quality Vulnerability:** Inherent risks associated with the weak credit risk profiles of borrowers in the power sector.
*   **Concentration Risk:** Significant concentration in revenue derived from specific sectors and customers.
*   **Regulatory Dependency:** The success of the merger remains subject to statutory, regulatory, shareholder, and creditor approvals.

## Liquidity
*   **Assessment:** The merged entity is expected to achieve enhanced balance sheet strength and improved capital efficiency, which supports liquidity.
*   **Cash Accruals & Bank Limits:** Specific figures for cash accruals or bank limit utilization are not detailed in this specific update. However, the entity's status as a nodal agency for GoI-backed schemes implies robust access to funding sources.
*   **Future Outlook:** The consolidation is anticipated to provide improved credit flow and funding capabilities, suggesting stable liquidity post-merger.

## Outlook & Rating Sensitivity
*   **Outlook:** **Stable** (Implied by "no impact on credit quality" and continued sovereign support).
*   **Upward Factors:**
    *   Further strengthening of government support or increased capital infusion.
    *   Successful execution of the merger leading to significant cost synergies and improved asset quality metrics.
*   **Downward Factors:**
    *   Deterioration in the credit quality of borrowers within the power sector.
    *   Failure to secure requisite regulatory or shareholder approvals for the merger.
    *   Reduction in government voting rights or control over the merged entity.

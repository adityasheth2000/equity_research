## Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has assigned a **'Crisil AAA/Stable'** rating to the long-term borrowing programme and a **'Crisil A1+'** rating to the short-term borrowing programme of Power Finance Corporation Limited (PFC). The agency has also reaffirmed ratings on existing debt instruments and bank facilities at these levels.

The ratings reflect PFC's strategic importance to the Government of India (GoI), given its role as the nodal agency for financing the Indian power sector, particularly state power utilities (SPUs). The company benefits from majority government ownership (56% as of Dec 31, 2025) and strong support mechanisms. While PFC maintains a dominant market position (~40% combined share with REC) and adequate capitalization, these strengths are partially offset by inherent vulnerabilities in asset quality due to exposure to weak credit profiles of SPUs and significant concentration in the power sector.

Notably, Crisil is monitoring the proposed merger between PFC and REC Limited. The merged entity is expected to remain a 'Government Company' under the Companies Act, 2013, continuing to receive sovereign support.

---

## Key Rating Drivers — Strengths

*   **Strategic Importance & Majority Ownership by GoI:**
    *   PFC plays a critical role in implementing government policies for the power sector, with ~80% of its consolidated loan book (as of Dec 31, 2025) comprising government sector power utilities.
    *   It serves as the nodal agency for ultra-mega power projects, integrated power development schemes, and rural electrification programs (Deendayal Upadhyaya Gram Jyoti Yojana, Saubhagya).
    *   PFC and REC are lending partners for the announced discom package and nodal agencies for the Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS).
    *   GoI holds a **56%** stake as of Dec 31, 2025, providing a moral obligation and financial backing in times of distress.
*   **Dominant Market Position:**
    *   PFC is the largest lender to the power sector in India.
    *   Combined market share with REC is approximately **40%** in power financing.
    *   Gross consolidated loan book stood at **Rs. 11.5 lakh crore** as of Dec 31, 2025, up from Rs. 10.7 lakh crore the previous year.
    *   Diversifying portfolio into renewable energy and infrastructure/logistics; transmission/distribution segment comprises ~46% of the standalone loan book.
*   **Adequate Capitalisation:**
    *   Networth reported at **Rs. 1,00,737 crore** (standalone) as of Dec 31, 2025.
    *   Gearing ratio stands at **4.7 times** (standalone) vs 5.7 times (consolidated).
    *   Networth to Net NPA ratio is robust at **68.3 times** (standalone).
    *   Capital Adequacy Ratio (CAR) and Tier 1 CAR are sufficient at **22.4%** and **20.66%** respectively.
    *   Return on Assets (RoA) and Return on Equity (RoE) for nine months of FY26 were **3.1%** and **19.1%** respectively.
*   **Sufficient Resource Profile:**
    *   Competitive borrowing costs with an average cost of borrowing of **7.0%** (9 months ended Dec 31, 2025).
    *   Diversified resource base: Bonds (59%), Term Loans (20%), Other Borrowings (1%).
    *   Access to foreign currency borrowing (20% of total), with **98%** hedged against exchange rate risk.
    *   Ability to raise tax-free bonds under Section 54 EC.

---

## Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses

*   **Inherent Vulnerability in Asset Quality:**
    *   Primary exposure (~80%) to State Power Utilities (SPUs), which have historically weak financial risk profiles.
    *   Significant sectoral and customer concentration in revenue.
    *   Private sector exposure (~20%) faces risks regarding fuel availability and price pass-through, though this is mitigated by increasing renewable energy share.
*   **Asset Quality Trends:**
    *   NPAs (Stage III assets) remained **nil** for the SPU portfolio as of Dec 31, 2025, supported by government dispensation schemes (RDSS, LPS).
    *   Consolidated Gross NPA improved to **1.3%** as of Dec 31, 2025 (down from 3.0% in Mar 2024), entirely attributable to the private sector book.
    *   Provision coverage on stage III assets in the private sector book is **82%**.

---

## Liquidity

*   **Assessment:** **Superior**
*   **Asset Liability Management (ALM):** As of February 28, 2026, the ALM profile shows positive cumulative mismatches across all periods up to the 1-year bucket.
*   **Liquidity Cushion:** Standalone liquidity cushion of **Rs. 18,287 crore** as of Feb 28, 2026.
    *   Cash and Equivalents: **Rs. 10,937 crore**.
    *   Unutilised Cash Credit Lines: **Rs. 7,350 crore**.
*   **Debt Obligations:** Outstanding debt obligations over the next month stand at **Rs. 9,090 crore**, well-covered by the available cushion.

---

## Outlook & Rating Sensitivity

*   **Outlook:** **Stable**
    *   Crisil expects the GoI to retain majority stake and continue supporting PFC's critical role in power sector financing.
    *   The company is likely to maintain its strong market position and healthy capitalization.
*   **Upward Factors:**
    *   Enhanced implementation of RDSS leading to improved asset quality of SPUs.
    *   Successful execution of the PFC-REC merger resulting in a stronger balance sheet.
*   **Downward Factors:**
    *   Government shareholding declining below 50%.
    *   Diminution of government support.
    *   Significant deterioration in asset quality impacting profitability and capitalization.
*   **Rating Sensitivity:**
    *   The rating on **perpetual bonds** is sensitive to the capital buffer maintained over regulatory requirements; rating transitions on these instruments could be sharper than other debt instruments.

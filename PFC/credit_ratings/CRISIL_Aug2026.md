## ## Detailed Rationale

Crisil Ratings has reaffirmed its **'Crisil AAA/Stable'** ratings on the bank facilities and debt instruments of **Power Finance Corporation Limited (PFC)** and its subsidiary **REC Limited**. The rating reflects PFC's strategic importance to the Government of India (GoI), dominant market position in power financing, adequate capitalization, and strong resource profile. These strengths are partially offset by inherent vulnerabilities in asset quality due to exposure to weak credit profiles of State Power Utilities (SPUs) and significant sectoral/customer concentration.

The agency has also noted the proposed merger between PFC and REC, under which REC will merge into PFC via a share exchange ratio of 88 PFC shares for every 100 REC shares. The merged entity is expected to retain majority government ownership and continue to qualify as a Government Company. The ratings factor in the GoI's strong strategic reasons and moral obligation to support PFC, given its role in implementing national power sector policies.

## ## Key Rating Drivers — Strengths

### Strategic Importance to and Majority Ownership by GoI
*   **Policy Implementation:** PFC acts as the nodal agency for ultra-mega power projects, integrated power development schemes, and rural electrification programs (Deendayal Upadhyaya Gram Jyoti Yojana, Saubhagya).
*   **Government Support:** GoI holds a **56% stake** (as of March 31, 2026). The company plays a critical role in channeling finance to State Power Utilities (SPUs), with ~76% of the standalone loan book dedicated to them.
*   **Discom Package:** PFC and REC are lending partners for the discom package and nodal agencies for the Revamped Distribution Sector Scheme (RDSS), which aims to improve discom sustainability.
*   **Financial Assistance:** Extended Rs. 62,528 crore to discoms under the Late Payment Surcharge (LPS) scheme in FY2022.

### Dominant Market Position
*   **Market Share:** PFC and REC combined hold a market share of **~40%** in power financing.
*   **Loan Book Size:** Gross consolidated loan book stood at **Rs. 11.6 lakh crore** (March 31, 2026), up from Rs. 11.1 lakh crore the previous year.
*   **Portfolio Diversification:** While SPUs dominate (~81% of consolidated book), PFC has diversified into transmission/distribution (~41%), renewable energy (16%), infrastructure (9%), and conventional generation (32%).

### Adequate Capitalisation
*   **Net Worth:** Reported net worth of **Rs. 1,02,532 crore** (standalone) as of March 31, 2026.
*   **Gearing:** Standalone gearing at **4.8 times**; Consolidated gearing at 5.7 times.
*   **Coverage:** Net worth to Net NPA ratio stands at **117.3 times** (standalone).
*   **Profitability:** Return on Equity (ROE) of **20.7%** and Return on Assets (ROA) of **3.4%** for FY2026.
*   **Capital Adequacy:** Overall CAR at **23.4%** and Tier 1 CAR at **21.9%**.

### Sufficient Resource Profile
*   **Cost of Borrowing:** Competitive average cost of borrowing at **7.0%** for FY2026.
*   **Diversification:** Resource base comprises 59% bonds, 19% term loans, and 2% other borrowings.
*   **FX Hedging:** Access to foreign currency borrowing (20% of total) with **97% hedged** against exchange rate risks.
*   **Tax Benefits:** Holds approval to raise Capital Gains Tax Exemption Bonds under Section 54 EC.

---

## ## Key Rating Drivers — Weaknesses

### Asset Quality Vulnerability & Concentration
*   **SPU Exposure:** ~81% of the consolidated loan book is exposed to State Power Utilities (SPUs), which face inherent financial stress due to poor operational efficiencies and tariff issues.
*   **Private Sector Risk:** ~19% exposure to private sector players, historically vulnerable to fuel availability and pricing challenges.
*   **NPA Trends:** While consolidated Gross NPAs improved to **0.7%** (from 3.0% in FY2024), they remain concentrated in the private sector book. ~3% of private sector loans are Stage III assets with ~80% provision coverage.
*   **Reform Dependency:** Asset quality improvement relies heavily on effective execution of reform measures and political consensus on tariff hikes.

## ## Liquidity

*   **Assessment:** **Superior**.
*   **ALM Profile:** As of June 30, 2026, the Asset Liability Maturity profile shows few mismatches in buckets up to 1 year; however, cumulative inflows match outflows when unutilized bank lines are included.
*   **Liquidity Cushion (Standalone):**
    *   Total Cash & Equivalents: **Rs. 286 crore**.
    *   Unutilised Cash Credit Lines: **Rs. 11,440 crore**.
    *   **Total Cushion:** **Rs. 11,726 crore**.
*   **Near-Term Obligations:** Debt obligations over the next month stand at **Rs. 10,553 crore**, well-covered by the liquidity cushion.

## ## Outlook & Rating Sensitivity

### Outlook: Stable
Crisil expects the GoI to retain majority stakeholding, ensuring continued policy implementation support. The company is projected to maintain its strong market position and healthy capitalization.

### Rating Sensitivity Factors

| **Upward Factors** | **Downward Factors** |
| :--- | :--- |
| • Sustained or increased government support.<br>• Significant improvement in asset quality of SPUs.<br>• Successful integration post-merger with REC leading to synergies. | • **Government shareholding declining below 50%.**<br>• Diminution of government support.<br>• **Significant deterioration in asset quality** impacting profitability and capitalization.<br>• Failure to implement necessary tariff reforms. |

*Note: Perpetual bonds are sensitive to the capital buffer maintained over regulatory requirements.*

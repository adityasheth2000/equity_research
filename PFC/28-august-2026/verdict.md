# PFC — Power Finance Corporation Ltd — Verdict

**Date:** 28 August 2026 · **CMP:** ₹352 (BSE ~₹350.90) · **Market cap:** ₹1,16,147 Cr
**Valuation snapshot:** P/E 4.46 | P/B ~0.81 (consolidated) / ~1.07 (standalone) | Div yield 5.27% | 52-wk ₹486 / ₹330

---

## Why is it falling despite a low P/E and P/B?

The short answer: **the market is pricing PFC as a low-growth value trap, not a cheap compounding story.** A low multiple on a financial stock only *matters* if earnings and the loan book can still grow. Right now the market doubts both. Four concrete pressures are behind the ~28% fall from the ₹486 high (and ~13% decline in August alone):

1. **Loan growth has stalled.** Management guided 10–11% FY26 growth but delivered only ~7% (standalone book ₹5.8 lakh Cr), and Q1 FY27 disbursements collapsed to ₹20,176 Cr from ₹36,152 Cr a year ago (**–44% YoY**). The cause is not demand — it is **prepayment/refinancing**: in the falling-rate cycle, PSU banks are aggressively refinancing PFC's *commissioned* and *renewable* assets (short-gestation, sure cash flows). Management admits that without these prepayments, growth would have hit the 10–11% range. This is exactly what triggered **Morgan Stanley's downgrade to Equal-weight (target cut ₹510 → ₹410)** on "sharp moderation in loan-book growth relative to peers."

2. **Margins are compressing.** Standalone yield has slipped from 10.01% (Q1 FY26) → 9.56% (Q1 FY27); spread from 2.61% → 2.49%; NIM 3.68% → 3.54%. Management has cut FY27 spread guidance to **2.40–2.50%**. The same competitive intensity that steals growth also steals price.

3. **FY26 earnings were flattered by one-offs.** ~₹1,800 Cr of FY26 standalone profit came from provision reversals (Sinnar Thermal ~₹670 Cr, TRN ~₹160 Cr, ~₹1,000 Cr DISCOM ECL reversal) that will **not recur**. Underlying profit growth is far thinner than the headline ~16% suggests.

4. **Merger overhang.** The announced PFC–REC merger (REC absorbed into PFC; 88:100 swap) creates genuine uncertainty: government stake mechanically drops to ~41–42% (below 50%), the merged group's bank-borrowing limit falls to 20%, dividends are only declared post-merger, and the deal needs MCA/RBI/SEBI/Cabinet nods by the Apr-2027 target. Uncertainty over a change in the sovereign-support optics is bearish for a stock whose thesis rests on that support.

So the market is not "misreading" a cheap stock; it is **re-rating a former compounding PSU into a low-growth, spread-compressing, ex-growth utility** — and paying only ~4.5x for it because ~21% ROE without growth doesn't command a growth multiple. Bulls will note the 5.3% yield, the AAA balance sheet, GNPA at 1.1%/0.66% and sovereign backstop; bears will note that a falling knife of a PSU NBFC has historically stayed cheap for years (PFC traded near these multiples for much of 2016–2020).

## What the company does

PFC (Power Finance Corporation) is a **Maharatna public-sector NBFC** — essentially a government-owned **wholesale bank for the electricity industry**. It is the nodal financier appointed by the Government of India to fund the power value chain.

- **What it sells:** long-term rupee term loans (and foreign-currency loans) to build power plants, transmission lines and distribution networks; short-term working-capital/loan-equipment finance; guarantees and letters of credit; and fee-based advisory via subsidiary PFC Consulting.
- **Who its customers are:** ~76–80% state-government power utilities (state gencos and DISCOMs — "State Power Utilities"/SPUs) and ~20–24% private power producers. Government-sector exposure is essentially the reasons its NPA is so low.
- **How it makes money:** it borrows cheaply (AAA-rated bond market 59%, banks 19–20%, foreign currency 20%) and lends at a spread — net interest margin ~3.5% on a ₹5.7 lakh Cr standalone book (₹11.6 lakh Cr consolidated including subsidiary REC). It takes a margin between its ~7% cost of funds and ~9.5–10% lending yield, and keeps the difference after credit costs.
- **Who owns it:** the President of India holds ~56%. It acquired a 52.6% stake in REC Ltd in 2019, and the two are in the process of merging.

In plain terms: **the government funnels money for India's power build-out through PFC, which earns a thin, low-risk spread on a very large balance sheet.**

## Company overview & industry positioning

- **Largest power-sector financier in India**; with REC, ~40% combined share of power financing. Largest NBFC group in India by loan book and profit.
- **Consolidated:** loan book ₹11.64 lakh Cr, PAT ₹33,625 Cr, balance sheet ~₹11.8 lakh Cr. **Standalone:** loan book ₹5.70 lakh Cr (+3.7% YoY), PAT ₹20,051 Cr (+16%), net worth ₹1,02,532 Cr.
- Loan mix (standalone, Q1 FY27): Generation 47% (conv. 32.5%, renewable+large hydro 15%), Distribution ~33%, Transmission ~8%, Infra/logistics ~9%, others ~3%. Government ₹4.37 lakh Cr (77%) vs private ₹1.33 lakh Cr (23%).
- Credit ratings: **CRISIL AAA/Stable** (reaffirmed Aug 2026, covering ₹1,39,762 Cr FY27 programme + ₹20,000 Cr CP + ₹11,000 Cr sub-debt), **CRISIL A1+** (short term). ICRA/CARE also rate it investment-grade.

## Management track record

**Hits**
- **Credit repair is real and large.** GNPA was taken from 8–9% (FY19–21) down to 1.09% standalone / 0.66% consolidated (FY26) while keeping ROE ~21%. 80% of the peak NPA book was resolved. KSK Mahanadi (₹3,300 Cr) was recovered at **>100% of principal** plus ₹1,192 Cr interest; Sinnar Thermal (₹3,001 Cr) resolved with a 42% principal recovery and ~₹670 Cr write-back.
- **Renewable pivot executed.** RE loan book grew ~35% YoY (FY25) to ₹81,031 Cr → ₹84,588 Cr (Q1 FY27); PFC is now India's largest RE financier, with a ₹16,000 Cr early-mover storage book.
- **Capital discipline.** Net worth crossed ₹1 lakh Cr; CRAR held above 23% even while returning dividends (₹18.55/share FY26).

**Misses**
- **Repeatedly missed its own growth guidance.** FY26 guided 10–11%, delivered ~7% (blamed on prepayments). FY27 re-guided to only ~10% — the third consecutive year of guidance being walked lower (12–13% → 10–11% → ~10%).
- **Spread guidance drifting down.** Held "~2.5%" for two years then conceded 2.40–2.50% — the first explicit admission of compression.
- **One-off reversals flattered profitability** — ₹1,800 Cr of FY26 profit will not repeat, and management has been explicit about that, yet is *not* giving a normalized (ex-reversal) profit target.
- **Unresolved merger mechanics** — government stake dropping below 50% in a share swap was seemingly not pre-solved before announcing; management is still working out how to preserve "government company" status.

## Financial analysis (₹ Cr)

- **Revenue** (consolidated total income): FY24 ₹91,508 → FY25 ₹1,06,502 → FY26 ₹1,15,450 (TTM ₹1,15,431). Total income is essentially interest income plus fee income; operating margin ("financing margin") ~37%.
- **Profit:** Consolidated PAT FY24 ₹26,461 → FY25 ₹30,514 → FY26 ₹33,625 (TTM ₹33,642). **Standalone** PAT FY26 ₹20,051 (+16%). Note the gap: ~₹13,500 Cr of consolidated PAT is REC + NCI — a key point, because **PFC shareholders only own 52.6% of REC** (and hence capture REC's earnings mainly via dividend, not full consolidation).
- **NII (standalone):** FY26 ₹21,896 Cr (+13% YoY), on a ₹5.8 lakh Cr book.
- **Margins:** spread 2.58% (FY25) → 2.46% (FY26) → 2.49% (Q1 FY27); NIM 3.64% → 3.55% → 3.54%. Yield trending 10.02% → 9.96% → 9.56%; cost of funds 7.44% → 7.50% → 7.07%.
- **Balance sheet:** standalone net worth ₹1,02,532 Cr (gearing 4.8x, consolidated 5.7x), borrowings ₹10.13 lakh Cr (consolidated). Solvency is not the question — **growth and spread are.**
- **Ratios:** ROE 21%, ROA 3.4% (CRISIL), net-worth-to-net-NPA 117x standalone.

## Quarterly progression & trends

- **Disbursements (standalone):** Q4 FY25 ₹67,968 Cr → FY26 quarterly run-rate slid → **Q1 FY27 ₹20,176 Cr (–44% YoY)**. Distribution's share of disbursement fell to 49% (from 63% YoY) as the LPS/RDSS one-off programmes wind down; generation and transmission rose in mix.
- **Loan book:** 30.06.2025 ₹5,49,786 Cr → 30.06.2026 ₹5,70,045 Cr (+3.7%). Transmission +15.7% YoY is the bright spot; distribution –2.1% YoY is the drag.
- **Asset quality (standalone GNPA):** FY25 1.94% → FY26 1.09% → Q1 FY27 1.11% (a small uptick; NNPA flat at 0.15%).
- **Stage-3 residual:** ₹6,315 Cr across 19 projects, of which ₹5,461 Cr under NCLT (6 projects / ₹2,603 Cr in liquidation, not expected to recover much). 16 of 19 projects already 100%-provisioned.

## Strategy, growth outlook & management quality

- Strategy is to hold ~40% power-financing share, pivot the mix from ~75% conventional generation toward ~50% generation (16% renewable) plus distribution/infrastructure, and monetize the power-sector capex cycle (80 GW thermal, 100 GW nuclear by 2047, storage).
- **Growth outlook is the crux.** FY27 guidance is ~10% loan growth and 2.40–2.50% spread; management argues prepayments will moderate. The power-sector capex pipeline (530 GW installed, 55 GW non-fossil added in FY26, storage mandates) is genuinely large — but PFC's ability to *keep* assets on its book (vs banks refinancing them after commissioning) is the open question.
- **Management quality:** competent on credit (asset quality transformation is genuinely well-executed) but **weak on growth delivery and candid target-setting.** The slippage is less about competence and more about the structural handicap: PFC is a wholesale lender whose best assets get poached by cheaper bank balance sheets the moment they de-risk.

Yield doesn't compound. *Moves: total shareholder return stays low single-digit unless multiple re-rates.* |

**What would change my mind (bull → bear):** a quarter where standalone **disbursements stay below ~₹25,000 Cr AND spread prints <2.40%** — that combination confirms the structural ex-growth + compression thesis and I'd stop waiting for a re-rate.

**What would change my mind (bear → bull):** two consecutive quarters of **≥10% standalone loan-book growth with NNPA ≤0.2%** — that would prove prepayment pressure is fading and the sanctions pipeline is genuinely replenishing the book. The single data point to watch is the **quarterly disbursement print.**

## Risks

1. **Prepayment/refinancing risk** — concentrated in renewable + commissioned assets; structurally caps book growth in rate-cut cycles.
2. **Competitive intensity** — PSU banks (clean balance sheets, cheap CASA/G-sec) undercutting on the best assets.
3. **Margin compression** — spread guided down, plus FX/hedging cost inflation as the rupee weakens (₹3,400 Cr hedging reserve vs ₹100 Cr in FY25).
4. **Credit-cost normalization** — ₹1,800 Cr of reversals don't recur; residual ₹2,603 Cr in liquidation unlikely to recover.
5. **Merger execution** — CRISIL's explicit downside triggers: *government shareholding below 50%* and *diminution of support*. This is the biggest tail risk to the AAA/sovereign-support anchor.
6. **DISCOM fiscal health** — 81% of the consolidated book is SPUs; tariff-reform slippage or subsidy arrears would erode the nil-government-NPA base.
7. **Concentration** — sector + single-counterparty (GoI) concentration is flagged by every rating agency.
8. **Credit rating trajectory:** no negative outlook (AAA/Stable across CRISIL/ICRA/CARE), but the merger's effect on government ownership is being watched — any movement of the stake below 50% would be the single most-material adverse event.

PFC and REC are the two **cheapest** power financiers precisely because the market now treats them as **mature, low-growth distributors of the government's power capex** rather than growth companies. IREDA and IRFC trade at 2–3x P/B and 8–16x P/E because their loan books are *still* compounding (renewables and railways, respectively) — the multiple is essentially a **growth premium that PFC no longer earns.** Analyst consensus is still "Buy" (avg target ~₹494, ICICI ₹560, Motilal/Emkay ~₹500; Morgan Stanley ₹410), but consensus targets assume a re-rating that the last three quarters of slipping growth have not delivered.

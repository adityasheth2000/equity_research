## Page 20

**Topic: Robust Asset Quality (FY19–FY25)**

The slide highlights a consistent and significant improvement in asset quality for the company (referred to as PFC), evidenced by declining trends in both Net NPA and Gross NPA ratios over the past seven fiscal years.

**Key Financial Metrics:**

| Metric             | FY 19   | FY 20   | FY 21   | FY 22   | FY 23   | FY 24   | FY 25   |
|--------------------|---------|---------|---------|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| **Net NPA Ratio**  | 4.55%   | 3.80%   | 2.09%   | 1.76%   | 1.07%   | 0.85%   | **0.39%** |
| **Gross NPA Ratio**| 9.39%   | 8.08%   | 5.70%   | 5.61%   | 3.91%   | 3.34%   | 1.94%    |

*Note: The slide explicitly states “Net NPA Ratio at 0.39%” for FY25, indicating this is the most recent or projected figure.*

**Management Commentary & Strategic Implication:**

- Management attributes the improving asset quality to “successful resolution efforts.”
- The decreasing trend in NPAs is framed as evidence of effective risk management and portfolio cleanup.
- This suggests strong operational execution in recovering bad loans or restructuring non-performing assets — a critical metric for financial institutions like NBFCs/banks.

**Investor Relevance:**

- A falling NPA ratio directly supports profitability (lower provisioning) and capital adequacy.
- The sharp drop in Net NPA from 4.55% (FY19) to 0.39% (FY25) represents an ~91% reduction — highly favorable for valuation multiples and credit ratings.
- Gross NPA also declined significantly (~79% over same period), though still higher than net NPA, suggesting some recoveries are being realized but not fully written off yet.

**Risk Factor / Watchpoint:**

- While improving, Gross NPA remains elevated relative to Net NPA — investors should monitor whether this gap reflects ongoing recovery potential or lingering hidden risks.
- FY25 figures may be estimates/projections; confirm actuals if available in subsequent reports.

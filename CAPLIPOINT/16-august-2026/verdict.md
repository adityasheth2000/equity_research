## 1. What the Company Does (In Plain Language)

Caplin Point Laboratories is a **generic pharmaceuticals exporter**. It makes and sells copies of off-patent medicines — tablets, capsules, injections, eye drops, syrups — at prices far below what branded companies charge. Its products are the kind of everyday, essential drugs (antibiotics, painkillers, diabetes medicines, blood-pressure pills) that cover more than 65% of the World Health Organization's "essential medicines" list.

**Who buys its products?** Almost entirely **developing-country customers in Latin America and Africa** (plus a growing US business). It does not sell much in India. The company's core market is the "bottom of the pyramid" — people in Guatemala, El Salvador, Honduras, Nicaragua, Ecuador, Colombia, Chile, Mexico and other countries who need affordable medicines. It sells through three channels: wholesale distributors (45%), retail pharmacies (35%), and government/institutional tenders (20%).

**How does it make money?** It has an unusual, vertically-integrated "**stock-near-the-customer**" model: it registers its products in each country (a slow, moat-building regulatory process), manufactures or outsources the drugs (60% in-house, 40% outsourced — much from China), ships inventory to its own warehouses close to customers, and sells directly to pharmacies and distributors through its own local subsidiaries and a 700+ person LatAm sales force. Because it controls the front end (its own distribution) and the back end (its own API and formulation plants), it captures margins most pure-play exporters cannot.

**Product scale:** 4,000–5,000+ registered product licenses, 650+ formulations, 36 therapeutic areas, operating in 23 countries. Roughly 75% of its emerging-market sales are plain generics; 25% are "branded generics" (its own brand names), a mix management is deliberately shifting toward branded for better margins.

**Two engines:**
- **Emerging markets (LatAm + Africa)** — ~78% of revenue in FY26. This is the mature, cash-generating core.
- **Caplin Steriles (US)** — ~18–22% of revenue. Sterile injectables/ophthalmics sold into the US via approved ANDAs, both B2B (supplying wholesalers) and a fast-growing own-label business. This is the higher-margin growth engine.

In Nov 2025 Ashok and Vivek Partheeban were elevated to Vice-Chairmen (LatAm and US/regulated respectively). Multiple USFDA approvals disclosed via exchange filings in a timely manner.

## 3. Key Metrics to Track (5–8 KPIs)

1. **US (Caplin Steriles) revenue growth** — the single biggest determinant of re-rating. Grew ~25–30% in FY26; management targets 25–30% again in FY27. Watch whether new ANDA launches (60 approved, 12 planned launches in FY27) convert to revenue.
2. **Own-label US ("CSU") revenue** — scaled from zero to ~₹100 Cr in its first full year; management targets ~₹200 Cr in FY27. This is the highest-margin piece and validates the B2B→B2C shift.
3. **ANDAs / product approvals & filings** — pipeline visibility. 60 US ANDAs approved, 55+ in pipeline; 110+ non-US filings. Approval velocity (14–15 mo avg, first-cycle) is a genuine edge.
4. **LatAm tender wins & Chile ramp** — Chile is the newest market (~$10M over 18 mo, targeting top-5 = $10–15M/yr in 2–3 yrs); Central America tender wins ($7M + $12M recently) drive near-term revenue spikes but also receivables.
5. **EBITDA / PAT margin** — FY26: 38.1% / 28.2%. Management argues ~38–39% EBITDA and 27–28% PAT are sustainable and could "inch up" in 18–24 months; a 40% op margin is cited as possible in ~2 years.
6. **Receivables days / cash conversion** — climbed from ~98 days (FY23) to 121–138 days; CFO/OP is ~88%. This is the key quality watch-item (FX + tender timing).
7. **Free cash flow & liquid assets** — FY26 FCF ₹275 Cr; liquid assets ₹2,875 Cr, free cash reserves ₹1,502 Cr. The ~₹1,000 Cr capex program must be funded from internal accruals without levering up.
8. **Capex delivery timelines** — Oncology API (Thervoy, Q4 FY27), OSD (Puducherry, Q1 FY28), COL injectable (Gummidipoondi, Q1 FY28), Mexico plant (commissioning FY27-28). Delays here (already slipped Q1→Q3 FY27 on oncology API) push out the FY28 growth inflection.

## 4. Management Track Record — Walked the Talk?

**Hits:**
- **5-year financial delivery:** Revenue ~₹1,085 Cr → ~₹2,300 Cr (FY21→FY26), PAT up ~2.5x, EBITDA/PBT/PAT up ~2.5x, while *staying debt-free throughout*. 10-yr compounded sales growth 25%, profit growth 30%.
- **Margin execution:** Gross margin held ~60%, EBITDA margin expanded 36.5% → 38.1% over FY25–26, PAT margin 26.6% → 28.2% — at the top of the industry.
- **US regulatory execution:** From first ANDA approval in 2018 to 60 approvals, 38 launched, with multiple **first-cycle** approvals (methylprednisolone suspension, emulsions) and no major USFDA inspection blemishes; EU GMP and Saudi FDA audits passed with nil critical observations.
- **Own-label profitability ahead of plan:** CSU turned profitable in its first quarter vs a 2-year breakeven target.
- **Cash discipline:** ~80% cash conversion of PAT (FY26 CFO ₹523 Cr vs PAT ₹650 Cr); liquid assets now exceed annual revenue; no promoter pledge; regular dividends (₹8/share for FY26, 400% of FV).

**Misses / caution flags:**
- **Slowing top line vs history:** FY26 revenue +12.9%, 9M FY26 +10–11% — below the ~15% 3-yr and 16% 5-yr CAGR. Growth has decelerated while fixed assets roughly doubled in ~3 years.
- **Oncology API facility delayed** (Q1 FY27 → Q3 FY27), partly attributed to Tamil Nadu talent/ecosystem constraints — a recurring "wait-and-watch" pattern on capex location/timing.
- **Growth inflection repeatedly pushed out:** Management initially framed Mexico/Chile/Colombia scaling as "next 2–3 years," then "FY28+." The 20–25% growth target has been qualified as "after FY2028-29" in some calls. Forward guidance is qualitative, not numeric.
- **Working capital drift:** Receivables have trended up (98 → 138 days over 3 years); management attributes this to tender timing and FX, but it's worth monitoring against the ~120-day target.

**Overall:** Management has delivered on the *core* (margins, cash, debt-free, US regulatory execution) but has been consistently conservative — and occasionally late — on *growth and capex timing*. Treat aggressive growth timelines with skepticism; treat margin/cash claims as credible.

## 7. Strategy, Growth Outlook & Management Quality

**Strategic pillars (consistent across calls):**
1. **Deepen LatAm moat** — branded generics, branded marketing (CNS line launching Sept 2026), a liquid-oral manufacturing facility in Central America, more registrations.
2. **Scale the US injectables franchise** — move up the complexity curve (pre-filled syringes, BFS ophthalmic, IV bags, oncology), own-label growth.
3. **Backward integration** — captive APIs (Vizag + Thervoy) for supply security and margin; 90+ APIs R&D-completed, first DMF filings targeted.
4. **Geographic expansion** — Mexico, Brazil, Canada, Australia, MENA, Russia/CIS.
5. **China 2.0** — asset-light partnerships (80–130+ partner ANDAs/MAs) for biosimilars, peptides, complex products.
6. **Stay debt-free** — all ~₹1,000 Cr capex funded from internal accruals.

**Growth outlook:** Management's clearest numbers: US/CSL +25–30% in FY27; own-label to double to ~₹200 Cr; 12 US + 10 CSU launches in FY27; ~₹510 Cr residual capex over 18–24 months. The 20–25% consolidated growth ambition is pegged to "FY28+" as new plants (oncology, OSD, COL injectable) and new markets (Mexico, Chile) mature. GLP-1/semaglutide is a real but secondary option (launch ~FY27-28, post patent expiry).

**Management quality:** Strong operators on margin, cash, and compliance; disciplined capital allocation (declined a ~$300M PE deal requiring ~20% dilution; prioritizes ANDA buys, LatAm distribution, and facilities). Conservative to a fault on numeric guidance. The Partheeban family (Chairman + two Vice-Chairmen sons) controls the company, with professional MD/CFO below — a typical, well-run Indian promoter pharma structure.

## 8. Risks

- **Credit rating trajectory:** Caplin has **no credit rating** on record — it is effectively debt-free and self-funds capex from accruals, so there is no rating to monitor and no refinancing risk. This is a structural positive; the risk instead is *opportunity cost* of holding ~₹1,500 Cr in low-yield cash.
- **Growth deceleration / capacity overhang:** Fixed assets roughly doubled over 3 years against ~50% top-line growth; if new plants don't fill, ROCE (already slipped from 68% to 25% over a decade) faces further pressure.
- **Working-capital drift:** 263 working-capital days; receivables 138 days. FX translation and lumpy government tenders (El Salvador) inflate this. Management expects no credit losses, but a deterioration is the key near-term quality watch.
- **US regulatory/competition risk:** US injectables margins (~10% PAT for the segment) are thin vs the core; the segment is exposed to FDA inspection risk, price erosion, and tariff uncertainty (Section 232 — generics currently exempt, but the situation is not settled).
- **Currency:** No hedging; relies on natural hedge (China sourcing ~25–30%, USD-linked local currencies). Net beneficiary of INR depreciation, but swings create earnings noise.
- **Oncology execution:** New oncology facility + acquired ANDAs are a new capability; commercialization of oncology injectables is different from generics (tenders, site transfers, dedicated units). Delays already visible.
- **Geographic concentration:** ~76% of revenue from LatAm — one region's macro/political shock (Venezuela-style currency collapse, government payment delays in Chile) could hit hard.
- **Key-man/promoter dependence:** Strategy is heavily tied to the Partheeban family; recent senior-management turnover (3 exits) was downplayed but is worth noting.
